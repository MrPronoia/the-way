# Uniquely Ethiopian Biblical Texts: English Translation Guide

Research into the English availability of the 8 texts found in the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo canon that have NO parallel in Western (Protestant or Catholic) Bibles. These are not the well-known deuterocanonical books (Tobit, Judith, Sirach, etc.) or the familiar pseudepigrapha (1 Enoch, Jubilees) -- these are the truly unique Ethiopian canonical texts.

---

## Overview & Context

The Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church canon contains 81 books (some counts say 84 or 88, depending on how composite texts are divided). Beyond the familiar Old and New Testament books and the deuterocanonical texts shared with Catholic/Orthodox traditions, the Ethiopian canon includes texts found nowhere else in Christianity. These fall into two categories:

**Old Testament unique additions:**
- 1, 2, and 3 Meqabyan (Ethiopian Maccabees -- entirely different from the Greek Maccabees)
- Josippon (Ethiopian recension of Jewish history)

**New Testament "broader canon" (ecclesiastical books):**
- Sinodos (4 sections)
- Book of the Covenant / Mashafa Kidan (2 sections)
- Ethiopian Clement / Qalementos (1 book)
- Didascalia (1 book)

These 8 texts (some composite) represent the most distinctive elements of the Ethiopian biblical tradition. Until the 21st century, most were only available in Ge'ez or Amharic. English translations have been emerging slowly, and many are still incomplete or only available as paid publications.

## 1. First Meqabyan (1 Meqabyan)

### What It Is
The first of three books unique to the Ethiopian Old Testament canon. Sometimes called "Ethiopian 1 Maccabees" but it has NOTHING to do with the Hasmonean-era Maccabees found in Catholic/Orthodox Bibles. Written in Ge'ez, the language of composition is Classical Ethiopic.

### Structure
36 chapters.

### Content Summary

**Chapters 1-4:** A tyrant king named **Tseerutsaydan** (ruler of Midian and Median territory) worships idols and compels his subjects to do the same. Three brothers -- **Abya (Abijah), Seela (Shelah), and Fentos (Pantera)** -- sons of a man named Maccabeus from the tribe of Benjamin, refuse to worship the idols, declaring they serve the one true God. They are powerful warriors who escape to the mountains. The king's forces threaten to destroy the city unless its inhabitants turn over the brothers. The brothers voluntarily surrender, are tortured, refuse again to worship idols, and suffer further persecution.

**Chapters 5-14:** Continued narrative of faith under persecution, consequences of idolatry, and divine vindication of the righteous.

**Chapter 15 onward:** A second group of brothers is introduced -- **Judah, Meqabis (Maccabeus), and Mebikyas** -- who lead a successful revolt against the ruthless King **Akrandis** of Midian.

**Throughout:** The text is a moral/theological narrative about the consequences of idolatry and the rewards of faithfulness to God. Much of the book concerns the Devil and how he tempts humans to sin. Themes of sacrifice, righteousness, and last judgment are prominent.

### English Translations Available

1. **Wikisource (Free, Online):** A complete English translation exists at:
   - [First Book of Ethiopian Maccabees](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/First_Book_of_Ethiopian_Maccabees) -- D.P. Curtin translation (2018), translated from Amharic
   - [1 Meqabyan (Translation)](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Translation:1_Meqabyan) -- Wikisource volunteer translation from Ge'ez

2. **D.P. Curtin / Bekele Tesfaye (Print):** *First Book of Ethiopian Maccabees: with additional commentary* (Dalcassian Press, revised 2023). Available on Amazon and Barnes & Noble. Considered the best scholarly English edition.

3. **Feqade Selassie (Print/eBook):** *1st Ethiopian Book of Meqabyan, in Standard English* (Lulu Press, 2008). An earlier free online version exists in Iyaric (Rastafari liturgical English) style, which can be difficult to read.

4. **Rejected Scriptures website:** [Ethiopian Apocrypha I](https://rejectedscriptures.weebly.com/ethiopian-apocrypha-i.html) -- Contains excerpts/text of 1 Meqabyan.

### Status: FULL ENGLISH TEXT AVAILABLE (free online via Wikisource)

## 2. Second Meqabyan (2 Meqabyan)

### What It Is
The second of the three Ethiopian Maccabees, again unique to the Ethiopian canon with no Western parallel.

### Structure
21 chapters.

### Content Summary

Opens with: *"After he found the Jews in Syrian Mesopotamia."*

A king of Moab named **Meqabis** made war against Israel as divine punishment upon them. Later he repented of his sins and taught the Israelites God's law. After Meqabis' death, **Tsirutsaydan** introduced idolatry and burned the sons of Makabis.

The text tells how Meqabees found the Israelites in Mesopotamia (part of Syria) and killed them in that region, beginning from Jabbok all the way to the square of Jerusalem, and how he destroyed the country.

The narrative continues themes of divine judgment, repentance, the consequences of turning from God, and the ultimate vindication of the faithful.

### English Translations Available

1. **Wikisource (Free, Online):** [2 Meqabyan](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Translation:2_Meqabyan) -- Translation from Ge'ez by Wikisource volunteers.

2. **D.P. Curtin (Print):** *Second Book of Ethiopian Maccabees: with additional commentary* (Dalcassian Press, 2023). Available on Amazon.

3. **Feqade Selassie (Print/eBook):** *2nd Ethiopian Book of Meqabyan, in Standard English* (Lulu Press, 2008).

### Status: FULL ENGLISH TEXT AVAILABLE (free online via Wikisource)

## 3. Third Meqabyan (3 Meqabyan)

### What It Is
The shortest of the three Ethiopian Maccabees. More theological/didactic than narrative.

### Structure
10 chapters.

### Content Summary

Opens with: *"And the islands of Egypt shall rejoice."*

A diffuse account of salvation and punishment, illustrated from the lives of various biblical patriarchs: **Adam, Job, David**, and others. The text draws moral lessons about faithfulness, sin, and divine justice from these figures' experiences. Less narrative-driven than 1 and 2 Meqabyan -- more of a theological meditation on righteousness and its rewards.

### English Translations Available

1. **Feqade Selassie (Print/eBook):** *3rd Ethiopian Book of Meqabyan, in Standard English* (Lulu Press, 2008). This is currently the primary English translation. Note: D.P. Curtin has NOT published a translation of 3 Meqabyan (he only did 1 and 2).

2. **Rejected Scriptures website:** [Ethiopian Apocrypha III](https://rejectedscriptures.weebly.com/ethiopian-apocrypha-iii.html) -- Contains text/excerpts.

3. **Various "Complete Ethiopian Bible" compilations on Amazon** -- Multiple publishers (Thornbridge Press, Holy Apocrypha, Bob Henry Oakland, etc.) include Meqabyan I-III in compiled volumes. Quality and accuracy vary. The Ethiopian Orthodox Bible Project has warned readers to beware of many of these compilations, noting that some are poorly sourced or inaccurate.

### Status: ENGLISH TEXT AVAILABLE (paid editions primarily; some excerpts free online)

## 4. Ethiopian Clement (Qalementos)

### What It Is
A late-medieval Ge'ez church-order text that presents itself as the teachings of the **Apostle Peter to his disciple Clement of Rome**, as recorded by Clement. This is completely different from the Western "1 Clement" and "2 Clement" (early church epistles). The Ethiopian Clement is a unique blend of apostolic teaching, post-biblical legend, and Ethiopian Christian ethos.

### Content Summary

The text covers:
- **Mysteries of Golgotha** -- theological significance of the crucifixion site
- **Adam's tomb** -- the tradition that Adam was buried at Golgotha
- **The Ark of the Covenant** -- its significance and location traditions
- **Ecclesiastical order** -- church governance and structure
- **Liturgical practices** -- worship instructions and rituals
- **Moral instruction** -- ethical teaching attributed to Peter through Clement
- **Visions of Peter** -- apocalyptic and revelatory material

### English Translations Available

1. **Breandan Lumpkin (Print/Kindle):** *The Book of Clement from the Ethiopian Bible: The First English Translation of Peter's Visions Given to Clement* (2024). Available on Amazon. Described as the first English translation. This is a paid publication -- no free online version.

2. **Michael Mikhail (New Edition, 2025):** A Coptic Orthodox Christian chanter who has translated the missing verses from Ethiopian Clement (specifically Ethiopic Clement 6:83-86, which were partially lost due to manuscript damage). This fills gaps in previous editions. Published through the Ethiopian Orthodox Bible Project.

3. **Academic analysis:** Alessandro Ferretti published academic work on the Ethiopic Book of Clement: *"The Ethiopic Book of Clement: the case of a recent Ethiopian edition and a few additional remarks"* (available on [Academia.edu](https://www.academia.edu/32686260/The_Ethiopic_Book_of_Clement_the_case_of_a_recent_Ethiopian_edition_and_a_few_additional_remarks)).

### Status: FULL ENGLISH TEXT AVAILABLE (paid only -- Lumpkin 2024 edition on Amazon)

## 5. Didascalia (Ethiopian Didesqelya)

### What It Is
The Ethiopian Didascalia (Didesqelya) is a book of church order in 43 chapters. It is distinct from the "Didascalia Apostolorum" known in other traditions, but similar to Books I-VII of the Apostolic Constitutions, where it most likely originates. It contains the earliest and most faithful summary of received Christian precepts and traditions as preserved in the Ethiopian church.

### Content

Covers:
- Questions of morality
- Studying the Scriptures
- Marriage duties
- Duties of Christian ministers
- Baptism and its theology
- Observance of Christian holidays
- Warnings against heresy
- Prayers for baptism and other occasions
- Lists of bishops ordained by the Apostles

### English Translations Available (FULL TEXT, FREE ONLINE)

1. **J.M. Harden Translation (1920):**
   - *The Ethiopic Didascalia* (Society for Promoting Christian Knowledge, London, 1920). xxiii + 204 pages.
   - **Free on Internet Archive:** [Full book](https://archive.org/details/cu31924096083336)
   - **Full text (plain text):** [OCR text](https://archive.org/stream/cu31924096083336/cu31924096083336_djvu.txt)
   - **Free PDF:** [Lion of Judah Society PDF](https://lojs.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/ethiopic-didascalia-the-apostolic-constitutions-and-ethiopic-church-orders-as-translated-by-j-m-harden.pdf)
   - Also available on Open Library (PDF, ePub, DAISY formats).

2. **Thomas Pell Platt Translation (1834):**
   - *The Ethiopic Didascalia; or, the Ethiopic Version of the Apostolical Constitutions, Received in the Church of Abyssinia* -- the earlier scholarly edition with both Ge'ez text and English translation.
   - **Free on Internet Archive:** [Full book](https://archive.org/details/ethiopicdidascal00platrich)
   - **Full text (plain text):** [OCR text](https://archive.org/stream/ethiopicdidascal00platrich/ethiopicdidascal00platrich_djvu.txt)
   - Also on HathiTrust: [HathiTrust](https://babel.hathitrust.org/cgi/pt?id=coo1.ark%3A%2F13960%2Ft84j12m8w)

### Status: FULL ENGLISH TEXT AVAILABLE (free online -- multiple editions on Internet Archive)

## 6. Book of the Covenant (Mashafa Kidan)

### What It Is
Also known as the **Kidan** or **Dominos** (from the Greek/Latin "Lord"), this is a two-part work presenting itself as the teachings of Jesus to the apostles during the 40 days between his resurrection and ascension. The two books correspond to:

- **1 Dominos = Testamentum Domini** (Testament of Our Lord) -- a church order describing the ideal church, its architecture, worship, and eschatological teaching
- **2 Dominos = Epistula Apostolorum** (Epistle of the Apostles) -- a letter from the apostles recounting Jesus' post-resurrection teachings

### English Translations Available

**For 1 Dominos / Testamentum Domini:**

1. **James Cooper & Arthur John Maclean (1902):**
   - *The Testament of Our Lord, translated into English from the Syriac with introduction and notes.*
   - **Free on Internet Archive:** [Full book](https://archive.org/details/cu31924029296170)
   - **Full text (plain text):** [OCR text](https://archive.org/stream/cu31924029296170/cu31924029296170_djvu.txt)
   - Note: Translated from Syriac, not directly from Ge'ez, but the Syriac and Ethiopian versions are closely related.

**For 2 Dominos / Epistula Apostolorum:**

1. **M.R. James (1924):**
   - From *The Apocryphal New Testament* (Oxford: Clarendon Press, 1924), pp. 485-503.
   - **Free online:** [Early Christian Writings](https://www.earlychristianwritings.com/text/apostolorum.html)
   - Based on: (a) a Coptic MS of the 4th/5th century at Cairo (mutilated); (b) a complete Ethiopic version; (c) a leaf of a 5th-century Latin palimpsest in Vienna.

2. **Anthony Alcock (2012):**
   - English Translation from the Coptic text.
   - **Free PDF:** [Roger Pearse](https://www.roger-pearse.com/weblog/wp-content/uploads/2013/01/epistula-apostolorum_alcock_2012.pdf)
   - **Free on Internet Archive:** [Full text](https://archive.org/details/EpistulaApostolorum)

3. **Academic edition from Coptic and Ge'ez:** Available on [Academia.edu](https://www.academia.edu/40394617/The_Epistula_Apostolorum_English_Translation_from_Coptic_and_Geez).

4. **Michael Mikhail (2025):**
   - *The 2 Ethiopic Books of "Dominos" (Covenant): Testamentum Domini and Epistula Apostolorum* -- described as the best combined edition specifically focused on the Ethiopian versions. Available on Amazon (paid).

### Status: FULL ENGLISH TEXT AVAILABLE (free online for both parts via Internet Archive and Early Christian Writings)

## 7. Sinodos (Alexandrine Sinodos)

### What It Is
The Sinodos (also called the Alexandrine Sinodos or Clementine Heptateuch) is a massive collection of ancient church canons and ordinances. It is counted as 4 books in the Ethiopian New Testament broader canon. It is a 4th/5th century CE compilation of Egyptian provenance that was particularly used in ancient Coptic and Ethiopian Christianity.

### Structure (4 sections)

The Sinodos is composed of:
1. **Serata Tsiyon (Order of Zion)** -- church governance
2. **Tizaz (Commandment)** -- regulations
3. **Gitsew** -- canons
4. **Abtilis** -- apostolic traditions

These contain:
- Constitutions of the Apostles
- Statutes of the Apostles
- Canons of the Apostles
- Canons of various councils: Nicaea, Gangra, Sardica, Antioch, Neo-Caesarea, Ancyra, and Laodicea
- Various theological and pastoral treatises

### English Translations Available (PARTIAL)

1. **Rev. G. Horner (1904):**
   - *The Statutes of the Apostles or Canones Ecclesiastici* (London, 1904) -- contains translations of the Serata Tsiyon, Abtilis, and Tizaz sections.
   - **Free on Internet Archive:** [Full book](https://archive.org/details/statutesapostle00unkngoog)

2. **George H. Schodde (1885):**
   - *"The Apostolic Canons, Translated from the Ethiopic"* (Journal of Biblical Literature, Vol. 5, 1885, pp. 61-72) -- contains the Gitsew section.
   - **Free on Internet Archive:** [JSTOR article](https://archive.org/details/jstor-3268629)
   - **Free PDF:** [Biblical Studies UK](https://biblicalstudies.org.uk/pdf/jbl/1885_061.pdf)

3. **The Apostolic Tradition of Hippolytus** (closely related text):
   - Translation by Burton Scott Easton, available on [Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/61614/61614-h/61614-h.htm).

### What's Missing
No single complete English translation of the entire 4-part Sinodos exists in one volume. The Horner and Schodde translations together cover the major sections, but are from the late 19th/early 20th century and can be difficult to read. A modern comprehensive English edition has not yet been produced.

### Status: PARTIALLY AVAILABLE (historical translations of individual sections free on Internet Archive; no complete modern edition)

## 8. Josippon (Ethiopian Recension -- Zena Ayhud)

### What It Is
The Ethiopian version is called **Zena Ayhud** ("History of the Jews"). It is the Ethiopic translation (via Arabic) of a Hebrew work known as **Sefer Yosippon**, a medieval compilation of Jewish history drawing on Josephus and other sources. The Ethiopic translation was produced in the latter half of the 14th century. It is recognized as canonical by both the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church and the Eritrean Orthodox Tewahedo Church.

It has 8 parts and covers Jewish history from ancient times through the destruction of the Second Temple.

### What Makes It Different
The Ethiopian recension differs significantly from the Hebrew original. It was translated through Arabic, introducing changes, and the Ethiopian church incorporated it into their canon -- something no other Christian tradition did with Josippon or any Josephus-derived text.

### English Translations Available (VERY LIMITED)

**There is NO complete English translation based on the Ethiopian version.**

1. **E.A. Wallis Budge (Partial):**
   - *The Life and Exploits of Alexander the Great* -- contains a very small section of Ethiopian Josippon in English on pages 403-428. This represents only a tiny fraction of the full text.

2. **Hebrew-based English translations (NOT the Ethiopian version):**
   - *The Abbreviated English Josippon: The Wonderful and Deplorable History of the Jews* -- Peter Morwyn's 1558 translation, modernized by Jacob Paul Michaels (2019). Based on the Hebrew version, which differs substantially from the Ethiopian recension.
   - **Free PDF:** [Jannawam PDF](https://jannawam.files.wordpress.com/2019/07/josipponweb.pdf)
   - Steven B. Bowman, *Sepher Yosippon: A Tenth-Century History of Ancient Israel* -- scholarly edition based on Hebrew.

3. **Ethiopian Orthodox Bible Project (In Progress):**
   - The project acquired the book of Josippon in 2017 and has been working toward an English translation from the Ethiopian version. As of the latest updates, this translation does not appear to be complete.

### Status: NO COMPLETE ENGLISH TRANSLATION FROM THE ETHIOPIAN VERSION. Only a small excerpt (Budge, pages 403-428) and Hebrew-based English translations (which differ from the Ethiopian recension) are available.

## What's Still Missing

The following remain unavailable in complete English translation:

1. **Josippon / Zena Ayhud (Ethiopian recension)** -- The Ethiopian Orthodox Bible Project acquired the text in 2017 but a full English translation has not been published. Only ~25 pages of Budge's partial translation exist.

2. **Sinodos (complete)** -- Individual sections have been translated by Horner (1904) and Schodde (1885), but no unified modern English edition covers all four sections comprehensively.

3. **3 Meqabyan** -- While translations exist (Feqade Selassie), there is no free online complete text. D.P. Curtin, who produced the best English editions of 1 and 2 Meqabyan, has not published a translation of 3 Meqabyan.
