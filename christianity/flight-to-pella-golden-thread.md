# What Happened to Jesus's Original Followers After 70 CE?

**Date:** 2026-03-12
**Type:** Research synthesis
**Sources:** Eusebius — *Church History* 3.5.3, 3.11, 3.32 (c. 325 CE); Epiphanius — *Panarion* 29.7, 30.2 (c. 374 CE); Hegesippus — fragments in Eusebius *Church History* 2.23, 3.32, 4.22 (c. 170 CE); Josephus — *Jewish War* (c. 75 CE); Gospel of Thomas, Saying 12; Acts 12:17, 15:13, 21:18; Galatians 1:19, 2:9, 2:12

---

## The Flight That Preserved Jesus's Movement

When Rome destroyed Jerusalem and its temple in 70 CE, the original Jewish followers of Jesus didn't die with the city. They survived — because Jesus reportedly warned them to leave before the siege.

Eusebius records:

> "The people of the church in Jerusalem were commanded by a revelation, vouchsafed to approved men there before the war, to leave the city and dwell in a certain town of Perea called Pella." — Eusebius, *Church History* 3.5.3

Epiphanius confirms:

> "This sect of Nazarenes is to be found in Berea near Pella... since all the disciples had settled in Pella after their removal from Jerusalem, Christ having told them to abandon Jerusalem... It was from this that the Nazarene sect had its origin." — Epiphanius, *Panarion* 29.7

This is the **golden thread** — the direct, traceable line connecting Jesus's original disciples to the Nazarene and Ebionite communities that survived for centuries in the Trans-Jordan region. It is one of the most important and most ignored facts in Christian history.

---

## The Chain of Leadership

The continuity wasn't just geographical. It was dynastic. Jesus's movement was led by his **blood relatives** for generations:

| Leader | Relationship | Period | Source |
|--------|-------------|--------|--------|
| **James the Just** | Brother of Jesus | c. 30–62 CE | Hegesippus (in Eusebius 2.23); Josephus *Antiquities* 20.9.1; Acts 15:13 |
| **Simeon bar Clopas** | Cousin of Jesus | c. 62–107 CE | Hegesippus (in Eusebius 3.11, 3.32); reported age at martyrdom: ~120 (possibly symbolic) |
| **Desposynoi** (kinsmen of Jesus) | Extended family | Through 2nd century | Hegesippus; Julius Africanus (in Eusebius 1.7) |

### James the Just — The Bridge

James is the critical link. He led the Jerusalem church for approximately 30 years after Jesus. He is multiply attested as:

- **Vegetarian from birth** — "He drank no wine nor strong drink, nor did he eat flesh" (Hegesippus, in Eusebius 2.23)
- **Perpetually in prayer** — "His knees became hard like a camel's from his constant kneeling in prayer" (Hegesippus)
- **So righteous that even enemies respected him** — He was called "the Just" by everyone, including non-Christians
- **Appointed by Jesus himself** — "It is for James that the whole universe has come into being" (Gospel of Thomas, Saying 12)
- **Died like his brother** — While being stoned to death, he cried out: "Forgive them, Father, they know not what they do" (Hegesippus)

If James was raised vegetarian in the Nazarene tradition by Mary and Joseph, the implication is clear: Jesus almost certainly was too. Paul never deploys the "mic drop" argument that Jesus ate meat against James's faction — suggesting that argument wasn't available because Jesus didn't.

### Simeon bar Clopas — The Succession

After James's martyrdom in 62 CE, leadership passed to Simeon, another relative of Jesus. Hegesippus records that Simeon led the community at Pella and was martyred under Emperor Trajan at the reported age of 120 — a figure that may be legendary or symbolic (120 = the age of Moses at death) rather than literal, though it aligns with reports of Essene longevity (Josephus notes that many Essenes lived past 100).

## What They Believed at Pella

The communities at Pella and surrounding areas maintained Nazarene beliefs with remarkable consistency for four to five centuries — rare in religious history. Their core convictions, attested by multiple hostile witnesses (church fathers writing to condemn them):

| Belief | Attestation |
|--------|------------|
| Jesus was the Messiah but not God incarnate | Epiphanius, *Panarion* 29–30 |
| Salvation through keeping commandments + works of love | Consistent with James 2:14-26 |
| Rejection of Paul as an apostate | Epiphanius records their hostility to Paul |
| Vegetarianism and opposition to animal sacrifice | Epiphanius, *Panarion* 18; Clementine literature |
| Use of the "Hebrew Gospel" (not Greek Matthew) | Epiphanius, Jerome |
| Continuation of Jewish observances | Epiphanius, *Panarion* 29 |

The remarkable thing about this list is how closely it matches what Jesus actually taught in the Synoptic Gospels — and how far it is from what the later church would teach.

---

## Why This Was Suppressed

The existence of a direct, unbroken line from Jesus through James through the Pella communities to the Nazarene sect creates an uncomfortable problem for institutional Christianity. If Jesus's own family and handpicked successors taught one thing, and the church that claims his authority teaches something different, which lineage is authentic?

The church's solution was straightforward: **declare Jesus's own sect heretical.** Epiphanius catalogs the Nazarenes and Ebionites alongside Gnostics and other "heresies" in his *Panarion*. The irony is staggering — the tradition that knew Jesus personally, was led by his brother, and survived by his direct warning was condemned as heretical by a church that never met him.

## The Geographic Evidence

The Trans-Jordan connection is not coincidental. Every major figure and event in the Nazarene lineage connects to this region:

| Person/Event | Trans-Jordan Connection |
|-------------|------------------------|
| John the Baptist | Ministry based at the Jordan River |
| Jesus's baptism | Occurred in the Jordan |
| Jesus's 40-day fast | Trans-Jordan wilderness |
| Feeding of the 4,000 | Trans-Jordan/Decapolis region |
| "On this rock" declaration | Caesarea Philippi, near Mount Hermon |
| Flight to Pella | Pella is in the Decapolis, Trans-Jordan |
| Nazarene communities (2nd–5th century) | "Berea near Pella" (Epiphanius) |

## Cross-Tradition Parallel

The pattern of an original teaching community being displaced and later condemned by a dominant institution repeats across traditions:

| Tradition | Original Community | Institutional Override |
|-----------|-------------------|----------------------|
| **Christianity** | Nazarenes at Pella | Roman Catholic Church |
| **Buddhism** | Theravada (elder monks) | Mahayana institutional expansion |
| **Islam** | Ahl al-Bayt (Prophet's family) | Umayyad Caliphate |

In each case, the blood relatives or closest disciples preserved one version of the teaching while a political institution promoted another.

## Source Verification

The primary sources for the Flight to Pella are Eusebius (*Church History* 3.5.3) and Epiphanius (*Panarion* 29.7), both quoting or relying on the earlier work of Hegesippus (c. 170 CE). Hegesippus was a Jewish Christian who traveled extensively to verify the apostolic succession — his testimony is considered highly reliable for 1st–2nd century Jewish Christianity. All texts are independently accessible in standard translations.
