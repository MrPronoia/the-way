# Was Jesus God Incarnate? What the Red Text Actually Says

---

## The Question

Was Jesus claiming to be the one and only God who stepped into human form? Or was he teaching that all humans share the same divine nature — that the unity he experienced with the Father is available to everyone?

This is arguably the most consequential question in the history of Christianity. The answer determines whether Jesus was founding a religion of worship or demonstrating a path of becoming. The red text — Jesus's own recorded words — answers it clearly when you read all of it, not just the passages the institution selects.

---

## The Passages Used to Claim Exclusive Divinity

Four red-text passages are most often cited to argue that Jesus was claiming to be uniquely God:

| Passage | Text |
|---------|------|
| John 10:30 | "I and the Father are one." |
| John 8:58 | "Before Abraham was born, I am." |
| John 14:6 | "I am the way, the truth, and the life. No one comes to the Father except through me." |
| John 14:9 | "Anyone who has seen me has seen the Father." |

### The Critical Textual Observation

These grand "I Am" declarations appear almost exclusively in **John's Gospel** — the latest canonical gospel, written approximately 90-100 CE, the most theologically developed, and the one Elaine Pagels argues was written partly to counter the Gospel of Thomas (*Beyond Belief*, 2003). The Synoptic Gospels (Mark, Matthew, Luke) contain virtually none of these proclamations. In the Synoptics, Jesus is far more humble: he asks "Who do YOU say I am?" rather than declaring it, tells people NOT to tell others he's the Messiah (the "Messianic secret" in Mark), and explicitly separates himself from God (Mark 10:18).

This does not mean John's red text should be discarded. It means it should be read alongside — not above — the Synoptic and Thomasine traditions.

## What Happens When You Read the REST of the Red Text

### John 10:30 in Context — Jesus's Own Rebuttal

"I and the Father are one" is the passage everyone quotes. But the scene does not end there. The Jews immediately pick up stones. And Jesus's response is the interpretive key to the entire question:

> **"Is it not written in your Law, 'I have said you are gods'?"** — John 10:34, quoting Psalm 82:6

Jesus is saying: Scripture calls ALL of you gods. Why are you upset that I said the Father and I are one? He is not defending exclusive divinity — he is **deflecting it**. He is saying what he has, you have too. The Law already told you this. If this passage proves Jesus is uniquely God, it simultaneously proves every person addressed in Psalm 82 is uniquely God.

### John 17:21-23 — The Prayer That Extends Unity to Everyone

> "That all of them may be one, Father, just as you are in me and I am in you. May **they also** be in us... that they may be one **as we are one** — I in them and you in me." — John 17:21-23

Jesus explicitly extends the **exact same unity** he has with the Father to every person who follows his teaching. If "I and the Father are one" (John 10:30) means "I am uniquely God," then "that they may be one as we are one" (John 17:22) means every believer becomes uniquely God too. The claim of exclusivity cannot survive the very chapter it appears in.

### John 14:12 — The Statement the Church Never Preaches

> "Very truly I tell you, whoever believes in me will do the works I have been doing, and they will do **even greater things than these.**" — John 14:12

Greater. Not equal. *Greater.* If Jesus were the one and only God walking in human form, no human could surpass him. This statement only makes sense if he is demonstrating a capacity that exists in everyone — a way of being in unity with the Father that his followers can not only match but exceed.

### Matthew 5:48 — A Command to Become What God Is

> "Be perfect, therefore, **as your heavenly Father is perfect.**" — Matthew 5:48

Not "worship me as perfect." Not "accept that I am perfect on your behalf." *Be* perfect as God is perfect. This is a direct command to attain divine qualities — a command that only makes sense if the divine nature is accessible to those receiving it.

## Where Jesus DISTINGUISHES Himself from God

These passages receive far less attention in institutional Christianity, but they are red text — Jesus's own words — and they are unambiguous:

| Passage | Text | What It Means |
|---------|------|---------------|
| Mark 10:18 | "Why do you call me good? **No one is good except God alone.**" | Jesus explicitly separates himself from God. If he were God, this statement is deceptive — and deception contradicts everything else he taught. |
| John 17:3 | "This is eternal life: that they know you, **the only true God**, and Jesus Christ **whom you have sent.**" | He calls the Father "the only true God" and identifies himself as someone *sent by* that God. Two distinct identities in one sentence. |
| John 14:28 | "**The Father is greater than I.**" | Direct statement of subordination. Incompatible with co-equality. |
| Mark 13:32 | "But about that day or hour no one knows, not even the angels in heaven, **nor the Son**, but only the Father." | Jesus admits not knowing something. An omniscient God cannot lack knowledge. Bart Ehrman documents in *The Orthodox Corruption of Scripture* (1993) that later scribes removed "nor the Son" from the parallel passage in Matthew 24:36 to protect the doctrine of omniscience. The earlier manuscripts include it. The later ones delete it. |
| Matthew 28:18 | "All authority in heaven and on earth **has been given to me.**" | Authority was *received*, not inherently possessed. You cannot be given what you already have. |
| 1 Timothy 2:5 | "There is one God and one mediator between God and men, **the man Christ Jesus.**" | Even Paul's tradition identifies Jesus as "man" and places him as distinct from God. |

## The Gospel of Thomas — The Clearest Red Text on This Question

The Gospel of Thomas preserves a sayings tradition that many scholars argue predates the canonical gospels or draws from the same early source pool (Koester, Patterson, DeConick). On this specific question, Thomas is unambiguous.

### Saying 108 — The Goal Is Becoming

> "Whoever drinks from my mouth **will become like me**; I myself shall **become that person**, and the hidden things will be revealed to that person."

The teacher becomes the student. The student becomes the teacher. The distinction dissolves. This is not a hierarchy — it is mutual identity. This saying only makes sense if Jesus is teaching a *state of consciousness* available to all, not claiming a unique ontological identity reserved for himself alone.

### Saying 13 — "I Am Not Your Teacher"

> Jesus said, **"I am not your teacher.** Because you have drunk, you have become intoxicated from the bubbling spring that I have tended."

Jesus tells Thomas directly: you have arrived at the same level. The relationship is no longer master-student. This is the exact opposite of "I am the one and only God, and you are permanently below me."

### Saying 3 — Children of the Living Father

> "When you know yourselves, then you will be known, and you will understand that **you are children of the living Father.**"

Children. Plural. All of you. The divine identity is not exclusive to one person — it is the birthright of everyone who achieves self-knowledge.

### Saying 77 — The Light in Everything

> "I am the light that is over all things. I am all: from me all came forth, and to me all attained. Split a piece of wood; I am there. Lift up a stone, and you will find me there."

This is often read as "Jesus is God." But the light is described as being *in everything* — wood, stones, everywhere. If the light is uniquely Jesus, then wood and stones are Jesus. The far more coherent reading: Jesus is speaking *as* the divine consciousness that dwells in all things — the same consciousness every human participates in. The Kabbalists call these the *nitzotzot* — divine sparks hidden in all matter (Zohar, Lurianic Kabbalah). Ra says "The Creator IS all there is" (Ra Material, Session 1.7). The Tao "flows everywhere, to the left and to the right" (Tao Te Ching, Chapter 34).

## What Every Other Tradition Independently Concluded

When 15+ traditions separated by centuries and continents arrive at the same answer, that is signal worth paying attention to. The perennial pattern is unanimous: the divine nature dwells in all beings. Enlightened teachers demonstrate what is possible, not what is exclusive to them.

| Tradition | Teaching | Source |
|-----------|---------|--------|
| **Hermeticism** | The "Anthropos" — the divine human archetype. Every person can achieve full divine realization. After rebirth, the initiate declares: "I'm in heaven, in earth, in water, air... I'm everywhere." | Corpus Hermeticum I (Poimandres); XIII.11 |
| **Kabbalah** | Every soul contains a divine spark (nitzotz). Devekut — union with God — is available to all who do the inner work. | Zohar; Lurianic Kabbalah |
| **Sufism** | Wahdat al-Wujud — the Unity of Being. Everything IS God. Not one man. All of creation. | Ibn Arabi, *Fusus al-Hikam* (13th century) |
| **Hinduism** | "Tat Tvam Asi" — Thou art That. YOU are Brahman. The Upanishads do not teach that one avatar is uniquely divine — they teach that the Self in every being is identical with the Absolute. | Chandogya Upanishad 6.8.7 |
| **Buddhism** | Buddha-nature exists in ALL beings. Siddhartha Gautama was not unique — he was the first in his era to wake up and show others the path. The Diamond Sutra warns against seeing the Buddha as a fixed self. | Tathagatagarbha Sutra; Diamond Sutra |
| **Law of One** | "You are the Creator" — every entity in every density is the One Infinite Creator experiencing itself. No being is more the Creator than any other. | Ra Material, Session 1 |
| **Eastern Orthodoxy** | Theosis — the purpose of human life is to BECOME God by participation. "God became man so that man might become God." The oldest surviving stream of institutional Christianity teaches becoming, not just believing. | Athanasius, *On the Incarnation* 54.3 (c. 318 CE) |
| **Taoism** | The Tao flows in everything and everyone. No single person is uniquely the Tao. "The Tao flows everywhere, to the left and to the right." | Tao Te Ching, Ch. 34 |
| **Walter Russell** | The fifth and final stage of human unfoldment — full omniscience, full omnipotence — is the endpoint of what every human is evolving toward. Not reserved for one man. Coming for everyone. | *The Secret of Light* (1947) |

## What the "Unique Incarnation" Reading Requires

To maintain that Jesus was claiming to be the one and only God incarnate, you must:

1. **Privilege John's Gospel** over the Synoptics, Thomas, and the entire Pauline corpus
2. **Ignore John 10:34** — Jesus's own rebuttal when accused of claiming divinity
3. **Ignore John 17:21-23** — where he extends the same unity to all believers
4. **Ignore John 14:12** — where he says his followers will surpass him
5. **Ignore Mark 10:18** — "No one is good except God alone"
6. **Ignore John 14:28** — "The Father is greater than I"
7. **Ignore Mark 13:32** — He admits not knowing something God would know
8. **Ignore Matthew 28:18** — Authority was given, not inherent
9. **Ignore Thomas 108** — "Whoever drinks from my mouth will become like me"
10. **Ignore Thomas 13** — "I am not your teacher"
11. **Ignore what every mystical tradition independently concluded** about the divine in all beings
12. **Accept Trinitarian vocabulary** (ousia, hypostasis, homoousios) that does not appear in Scripture and was not invented until centuries later as what Jesus meant

## Why This Distinction Matters — The Downstream Implications

The two readings produce completely different religions:

| If Jesus taught "we all share the divine nature" | If Jesus taught "I alone am God" |
|---|---|
| No blood atonement needed — the cross was a murder, not a payment | The cross is a cosmic transaction — required by God |
| No institutional church needed — you can find God within yourself | You need an institution to explain the mystery and dispense grace |
| Direct access to God for everyone | Priestly mediation required |
| Every human has the same divine potential | You can never reach his level — worship, don't become |
| The purpose of life is transformation through practice | The purpose of life is correct belief and doctrinal assent |
| "Follow me" means *do what I did* — live the Way | "Follow me" means *worship me* — believe the creed |
| Self-knowledge is salvation (Thomas 3) | Self-knowledge is irrelevant — faith alone saves (Romans 10:9) |

The institutional church chose the second column. It had to. The first column makes the institution unnecessary. If the Kingdom is within you and you can find it through self-knowledge, transformed living, and direct communion with God — you do not need a priest, a creed, a building, or a tithe.

Thomas Saying 39 diagnosed this dynamic two thousand years ago: *"The Pharisees and the scribes have taken the keys of knowledge and hidden them. They themselves have not entered, nor have they allowed to enter those who wish to."*

## The Message Mankind Was Supposed to Take

Based on the full red text — canonical and non-canonical — read without institutional bias and cross-validated by every mystical tradition that independently arrived at the same conclusion:

**You are children of the living Father.** The Kingdom of God is already here — spread out upon the earth — and you are not seeing it. Know yourself and you know God. The divine spark in you is the same divine spark in everything — in wood, in stone, in the stranger, in the enemy. The unity Jesus experienced with the Father is not reserved for one man. It is available to everyone who seeks it: "that they may be one as we are one." The goal is not to worship a teacher. The goal is to *become* what the teacher demonstrated: "Whoever drinks from my mouth will become like me." Do what he did. Live how he lived. And then do even greater things.

This is not a ceiling reserved for one man. It is the floor available to every human being.
