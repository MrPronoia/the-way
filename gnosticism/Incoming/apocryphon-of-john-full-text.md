# The Apocryphon of John (Secret Book of John) — Full Text

> The foundational Gnostic cosmological text. Discovered at Nag Hammadi (1945), Egypt. Four copies found — the most-copied Gnostic text, indicating its central importance.
> Translation: Marvin Meyer, *The Nag Hammadi Scriptures* (HarperOne, 2007). Cross-referenced with Frederik Wisse in James M. Robinson, *The Nag Hammadi Library in English* (1977/1990).
> The "longer version" from Codex II is used here (the standard scholarly text).

**Setting:** After the crucifixion, John (son of Zebedee) goes to a mountainous place in distress. A Pharisee named Arimanios challenges him. The heavens open, and a figure of light appears — Christ — to reveal the true nature of reality.

---

## Part I: The Invisible Spirit (The Monad)

The teaching begins with the nature of the ultimate God — utterly unlike the biblical creator:

> The Monad is a monarchy with nothing over it. It is God and Father of everything, the invisible Spirit, who is over everything, who exists as incorruption, which is in the pure light into which no eye can look.

> The Spirit is not to be thought of as a god or as something similar. For it is more than a god, since there is nothing above it, for no one has authority over it.

> It does not exist in anything inferior to it, since everything exists in it alone. It is eternal, since it does not need anything. For it is total perfection.

> It is illimitable, since there is nothing before it to limit it. It is unfathomable, since there is nothing before it to fathom it. It is immeasurable, since there was nothing before it to measure it. It is invisible, since no one has seen it. It is eternal, since it exists eternally. It is unutterable, since no one was able to comprehend it to utter it. It is unnameable, since there is no one before it to name it.

> It is the immeasurable light, pure, holy, immaculate. It is unutterable, being perfect in incorruptibility. Not in perfection, nor in blessedness, nor in divinity, but far surpassing these.

> It is not corporeal. It is not incorporeal. It is not large. It is not small. It is not a quantity. It is not a creature. No one can think of it. It is not something among existing things, but something far superior.

**Cross-tradition parallel:** This negative theology (describing God by what God is NOT) matches:
- Kabbalah: Ein Sof — the Infinite, beyond all attributes, beyond even "being"
- Plotinus: The One — "beyond being, beyond thought, beyond description"
- Sufism: The Divine Essence (Hadrat al-Dhat) — absolute unity beyond names and attributes
- Vedanta: Nirguna Brahman — Brahman without qualities
- The Mandukya Upanishad's Turiya: "unthinkable, indescribable, the cessation of the manifold world"

## Part II: The Emanation of the Aeons (The Pleroma)

From the Invisible Spirit, the divine realm unfolds:

> The Spirit looked into its own pure light flowing around it. Its thought (Ennoia) performed an act and she came forth — she appeared before it in the shine of its light. This is the first power that was before all of them and that came forth from its mind: the Forethought (Pronoia) of the All.

> Her light shines like the light of the Spirit. She is the power that appeared before the All. She is the perfect Forethought, the image of the invisible Spirit, the first power, the glory of Barbelo, the perfect glory in the aeons, the glory of the revelation. She glorified and praised the invisible Spirit, since it was for its sake that she had come forth.

> This is the first thought, the image of the Spirit. She became the universal womb, for she precedes everything: the Mother-Father, the first Human, the holy Spirit, the triple male, the triple power, the triple-named androgynous one, and the eternal aeon among the invisible ones, and the first to come forth.

**Barbelo** — the first emanation — is described as both male and female, the "Mother-Father." She is the first thought of God, the womb from which all subsequent emanations emerge.

From Barbelo, through the Invisible Spirit's will:

> The Father looked into Barbelo with the pure light that surrounds the invisible Spirit and its radiance, and she conceived from it. It begot a spark of light in a light resembling blessedness. This was the only-begotten of the Mother-Father who came forth — its only offspring, the only-begotten of the Father, the pure light.

This "only-begotten" is **Autogenes** (Self-Generated) — the Christ, the divine Mind:

> The invisible Spirit looked upon Autogenes with the pure light and gave it Mind (Nous). It gave it Will (Thelema). It established it over everything. It gave it Truth (Aletheia).

From Autogenes, four great luminaries are established, and within them the aeons (eternal divine beings) are generated — including the twelve aeons of the Pleroma (the divine fullness).

## Part III: The Fall of Sophia

The critical turning point — the origin of the material world through error:

> Sophia of the Epinoia, being an aeon, conceived a thought from herself and the thought of the invisible Spirit and foreknowledge. She wanted to bring forth a likeness out of herself without the consent of the Spirit — it had not approved — and without her consort, and without his consideration.

> And though the personage of her masculinity had not approved, and she had not found her agreement, and she had thought without the consent of the Spirit and the knowledge of her agreement, yet she brought forth. And because of the invincible power within her, her thought did not remain idle, and something came out of her that was imperfect and different from her appearance, because she had created it without her consort.

> It did not resemble its mother, for it had another form. When she saw the consequences of her desire, it changed into a form of a lion-faced serpent. Its eyes were like lightning fires that flash. She cast it away from her, outside that place, so that no one of the immortal ones might see it, for she had created it in ignorance.

> She surrounded it with a luminous cloud and placed a throne in the middle of the cloud, so that no one might see it except the holy Spirit, who is called the Mother of the Living. She called his name Yaltabaoth.

**Yaltabaoth** (also spelled Yaldabaoth) — the Demiurge, the blind creator god:

> This is the first archon who took a great power from his mother. He removed himself from her and moved away from the places in which he was born. He became strong and created for himself other aeons with a flame of luminous fire that still exists now.

> And he was stupefied in his madness that dwells in him and begot authorities for himself... He placed seven kings — one for each firmament of heaven — over the seven heavens, and five over the depth of the abyss, that they might rule.

> He shared his fire with them, but he did not send to them any of the pure light of the power he had taken from his mother. Therefore, he ruled over them because of the glory of the power of the light of the mother that was in him. And therefore he called himself God, and he did not obey the place from which he came.

The Demiurge, ignorant of the realms above, declares:

> "I am God and there is no other God beside me."

**Cross-tradition parallel:**
- Kabbalah: Shevirat HaKelim (Shattering of the Vessels) — the breaking that scatters divine sparks into the material world. Sophia's "error" parallels the shattering
- Plotinus: Rejected the Gnostic view (Enneads II.9) — for Plotinus, the material world is beautiful, not a mistake. But he acknowledges that Soul can "fall" into excessive attachment to matter
- Sufism: The "Hidden Treasure" that creates — but in Sufism, creation is an act of love, not error. The Gnostic framing inverts the Sufi reading
- Hinduism: Maya/Avidya — the veil of ignorance that creates the appearance of separation. Not personal error but cosmic principle

## Part IV: The Creation of Adam and the Divine Spark

The Demiurge and his archons create the physical human body, but they cannot give it life:

> The blessed one, the Mother-Father, the beneficent and compassionate, had mercy on the power of the Mother that had been brought forth out of the chief archon, lest they again gain power over the natural and perceptible body.

> It sent, through the beneficent Spirit and great mercy, a helper to Adam — a luminous Epinoia that comes out of it, who is called Life (Zoe). She assists the whole creature, by toiling with it, by restoring it to its fullness, and by teaching it about the descent of its seed, and by teaching it about the way of ascent, the way it had descended.

> The luminous Epinoia was hidden in Adam, so that the archons might not know about it, and so that the Epinoia might be a correction of the deficiency of the Mother.

The key teaching: **a spark of the divine light is hidden within every human being.** The archons cannot see it. It is the real Self, trapped in the material body created by the ignorant Demiurge.

> The human being came forth because of the shadow of the light within it. Its thinking was superior to all those who had made it. When they looked up, they saw that its thinking was superior, and they took counsel with the whole array of archons and angels. They took some fire and earth and water and mixed them together with each other and the four fiery winds. They wrought them together and caused a great disturbance.

> They enclosed him in the shadow of death. They enclosed him in a body of matter, in the bond of forgetfulness.

**Cross-tradition parallel:**
- Kabbalah: Nitzotzot — the divine sparks trapped in the qliphoth (shells), awaiting liberation through tikkun
- Hermeticism: "Know thyself" — the soul's recognition of its divine origin trapped in matter
- Vedanta: Atman — the true Self hidden beneath the five koshas (sheaths), awaiting recognition
- Law of One: The divine spark within each entity — "You are every thing, every being, every emotion"
- Christianity: "The Kingdom of Heaven is within you" (Luke 17:21)

---

## Part V: The Path of Return

Christ reveals the way back:

> "I am the remembrance of the Pleroma."

> "I entered the middle of the prison, which is the prison of the body. And I said: 'Let whoever hears arise from deep sleep.' And that person wept and shed tears, heavy tears, and wiped them away and said: 'Who is it that calls my name? And from where has this hope come to me, while I am in the chains of the prison?'

> And I said: 'I am the Forethought of the pure light. I am the thought of the virgin Spirit... Arise and remember that it is you who has listened, and follow your root, which is I, the merciful one, and guard yourself against the angels of poverty and the demons of chaos.'"

The path of return is **gnosis** — not belief, not ritual, but direct knowledge of your true nature and origin. The divine spark within recognizes its source and ascends back through the archonic realms to the Pleroma.

---

## Part VI: The Three Descents of Forethought (Pronoia's Hymn)

The text concludes with a hymn in which Pronoia (divine Forethought — the first emanation, Barbelo) describes her three descents into the material world to awaken trapped souls:

> "I am the Forethought of the All. I dwell in those who exist.
> I move in every creature.
>
> The first time I descended, I entered the middle of the underworld.
> I penetrated to its foundations.
> I shook the foundations of the underworld.
>
> The second time I descended to the realm of darkness.
> I stood among them, hidden from the archons.
> I gave them shape.
>
> The third time I descended.
> I entered the midst of the prison, the body.
> I said, 'Let whoever hears arise from deep sleep.'
> A person wept, shedding tears, wiping them,
> and said, 'Who calls my name?
> From where does this hope come,
> as I sit in the chains of this prison?'
>
> I said, 'I am the Forethought of pure light.
> I am the thought of the virgin Spirit.
> Rise up, remember that you have heard.
> Trace back your root to me —
> I, the merciful.
> Guard yourself against the angels of poverty
> and the demons of chaos.'"

*Translation: Marvin Meyer, The Nag Hammadi Scriptures (2007). Cross-referenced with Frederik Wisse in Robinson's Nag Hammadi Library (1990). The Apocryphon of John is the most-copied Gnostic text (four copies at Nag Hammadi), indicating its central importance to the Gnostic communities. It provides the complete cosmological narrative — from the unknowable God through the Pleroma, Sophia's fall, the creation of the Demiurge and the material world, the trapping of divine sparks in human bodies, and the path of return through gnosis.*
