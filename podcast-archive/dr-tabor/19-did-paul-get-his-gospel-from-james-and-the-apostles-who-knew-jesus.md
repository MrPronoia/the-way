## Summary

This is the companion to Tabor's "How Paul Created His Gospel" — but here he is reporting back from an academic conference where he tested the argument with three top Paul scholars (Paula Fredriksen, Ben Shields, John Barclay) and discovered just how alone he is on it. The conventional scholarly consensus, even among critical scholars who differ from evangelicals on almost everything else, is that Paul did inherit the basic Christ-message from the Jerusalem pillars and then opened it up for Gentiles — same fundamentals, just a wider audience. Tabor's bombshell pushback: **no, Paul's gospel is independent — and the synoptic gospels themselves are post-Pauline carriers of Paul's message, not independent witnesses to what James, Peter, John, and the Twelve actually taught.**

The hinge of the argument is the standard reading of "I received... I delivered" language in 1 Corinthians 11 and 15. Everyone treats these verbs (*paralambanō* / *paradidōmi*) as official chain-of-tradition language proving Paul was downstream of the apostles. Tabor reads the same passages and notes that Paul *explicitly* sources his message elsewhere: Galatians 1:11-12 ("not man's gospel... not taught it... revelation of Jesus Christ"), 1 Corinthians 11:23 ("I received from the Lord"). The Last Supper words — supposedly the surest piece of apostolic tradition — Paul claims came to him *from the risen Jesus*, not from people who were physically at the table. When scholars in his conference session pushed back ("come on, he obviously got the narrative from the apostles who were there"), Tabor's reply was the inverse of theirs: Mark didn't get the Last Supper from oral tradition; **Mark lifted it from Paul.**

What Tabor wants by the end of the lecture is for the listener to ask the simple test question: is what John the Baptist, James, Peter, and the Twelve preached in the Galilee really *baptism into the death and resurrection of Christ for mystical union and substitutionary atonement*? He doesn't think so — and he points to the surviving witnesses to the Jerusalem movement to show what it actually sounded like: the Q source (the Sermon-on-the-Mount material), the Letter of James (pure Jewish ethical piety, "knees callous like a camel"), the *Didache* (a Eucharistic prayer that thanks God for "the holy vine of David" and "the knowledge" — no body, no blood, no atonement), Hegesippus on James the Just dying for confessing Jesus as Son of Man (Daniel 7, not Isaiah 53), and an apocalyptic Jewish-Christian piety that looks much closer to Qumran than to Paul. The conclusion: if you actually map the testimony of the people who walked with Jesus, Paul's gospel doesn't fit there. It is its own creation.

## Key Teachings

### "My Gospel Was Not Man's Gospel" — Galatians 1 Is the Anchor Verse

Tabor reads Galatians 1:11-12 as the load-bearing wall of his case: *"For I would have you know, brethren, that the gospel which was preached by me is not man's gospel. I did not receive it from a human, nor was I taught it, but it came through a revelation of Jesus Christ."* Almost every scholar in the conference session, Tabor notes, soft-pedaled this — interpreting it as Paul claiming divine inspiration *on top of* what he received from the apostles. Tabor refuses to read it that way. Paul says he did not receive it from man. He says he was not taught it. He says it came through *apokalypsis* — revelation. The plain meaning is that Paul did not inherit his gospel from the Jerusalem pillars.

> "I didn't confer with flesh and blood. Well, who did you confer with then? Angels perhaps, or Jesus himself? This is high cotton, as they say down south. This is amazing a claim. This is like the Mormons. This is like David Koresh."

### "I Received from the Lord" Means the Lord, Not the Apostles

Tabor sets up the test case that exposes the conventional reading. **1 Corinthians 11:23** — the Last Supper passage — uses the same "I received... I delivered" formula as 1 Corinthians 15:3. Every scholar in his conference session read 15:3 as Paul receiving the death-burial-resurrection creed from Peter and James. So Tabor asked: do you also read 11:23 that way? Did Paul get the Last Supper narrative from the apostles who were at the table?

The room thought it was crazy when Tabor pressed the opposite: *Mark got the Last Supper narrative from Paul, not the other way around.* The narrative in Mark 14 lines up so cleanly with Paul's account in 1 Cor 11 that the simpler explanation is dependence in Mark's direction. Paul himself says he got it from "the Lord" — by revelation — and Mark, written 15-20 years later by someone heavily influenced by Paul, narrativized it.

### Paul's Mystical Communion Is Not What the Jerusalem Church Preached

Tabor presses the substantive content question: take the actual theology of Paul's Eucharist — the *koinonia*, the mystical participation in the body and blood of Christ ("Schweitzer's *Mysticism of Paul the Apostle*") — and ask whether this is what the Jerusalem movement was teaching in the Galilee. He doesn't think so. Paul's Eucharistic theology is mystery-religion-flavored union with a dying-and-rising savior. The *Didache*'s Eucharist — the only window we have into a non-Pauline early Christian meal liturgy — has none of it. The *Didache* gives thanks for "the holy vine of David" and "the life and knowledge made known through Jesus your child." That is it. No body. No blood. No atonement. It looks like a Jewish berakhah with a Jesus garnish.

> "Sometimes Protestants dumb it down because they don't want to go the Catholic way and say you know, transubstantiation. But — the real presence, the idea that this mystical participation, comes from Paul."

### "I Worked Harder Than All of Them" — Paul's Tone Toward the Pillars

Tabor lingers on the small but revealing tells in Paul's text. In 1 Corinthians 15:10, immediately after listing the appearances (including to himself last), Paul says: *"I worked harder than any of them."* Harder than Peter. Harder than James. Harder than the Twelve. In Galatians 2 he says *"what they are means nothing to me — the so-called pillars."* In 1 Corinthians 14:37-38 he claims direct command-of-the-Lord authority and threatens that anyone who doesn't recognize his authority "is not recognized."

This is not the voice of a junior member catechized by the original community. This is the voice of someone who believes his own revelation outranks the people who walked with Jesus.

### The Test Question: Is This What the Galilee Movement Preached?

Tabor frames the whole lecture around a single test: list out the core elements of Paul's gospel, then ask whether John the Baptist, James, Peter, and the Twelve preached this. The core:

- Baptism *in the name of Christ* into the death and resurrection (Romans 6) — mystical union, mystery-religion style
- Salvation by grace through faith in the atoning blood of Jesus (Romans 1-5)
- A New Covenant that supersedes Moses (the glory of Sinai has "faded")
- A new spiritual Israel for all humankind, where Abraham's "seed" is reinterpreted as plural
- Jerusalem above replacing earthly Jerusalem
- Cosmic transformation — the elect ruling angels and the world

His answer: Mark gives John the Baptist *one* line — "a baptism of repentance for the forgiveness of sins." The Q source (the double tradition in Matthew and Luke) gives more of John's actual teaching, and it sounds like *Qumran*, not Paul. Repent, undergo initiation, walk in the way of the sons of light. There is no atoning blood. There is no mystical participation. There is no New Covenant superseding Moses. **None of the core elements of Paul's gospel appears in the actual non-Pauline witnesses to the Jerusalem movement.**

### What Survived from the James-Led Movement

Tabor catalogs what we still have that tells us what the original movement sounded like — and it sounds nothing like Paul:

- **The Q source / Sermon-on-the-Mount material** — the double tradition embedded in Matthew and Luke that doesn't come from Mark. Pulsating with prophetic ethical piety (Jeremiah, Hosea, Amos, Micah, Joel). What does God really want in the heart.
- **The Letter of James** — even if not directly written by James (it's in polished Greek), the *piety* of James shines through. It reads like Jewish wisdom literature. Hegesippus describes James the Just as having knees calloused "like a camel" from praying three times daily — the Amidah pattern. "I think the flavor of James is captured still in the Jewish prayer book." (Tabor holds up his 1990 Birnbaum siddur.)
- **The *Didache* (Teaching of the Twelve Apostles)** — found in 1873 in a Constantinople library; manuscript dated 1056 but the underlying text is very early. Its Eucharistic prayers thank God for "the vine of David" and for "the knowledge made known through Jesus your child." No body, no blood. The instruction *"don't give what is holy to the dogs"* (Matt 7:6) is preserved as a baptismal Eucharist gate. The final chapter is apocalyptic Daniel-flavored eschatology.
- **Gospel of Thomas, logion 12** — "The disciples said to Jesus, 'We know you will leave us. Who will be great over us?' Jesus said, 'Wherever you have come, you will go to James the Just, for whose sake heaven and earth came into being.'" Tabor reads this as direct evidence that James — not Peter, not Paul — was understood as the successor figure.
- **Hegesippus on James's martyrdom** — when the priests demanded *"what is the gate of Jesus?"* (Tabor reads this as a garbled *"what is the way of salvation?"*), James answered by quoting **Daniel 7**: *"you will see the Son of Man coming in the clouds of heaven."* Not Isaiah 53. Not substitutionary blood. The Danielic apocalyptic kingdom — the collective people of God receiving rule, authority, and power.
- **The Book of Revelation** (or its older underlying strata) — *"those who keep his commandments and hold the testimony of Jesus."* A law-abiding remnant, not a justification-by-faith community.

### James and the Dead Sea Scrolls Piety

Tabor revisits Robert Eisenman's claim that James-style piety is reflected in the Dead Sea Scrolls — particularly the Community Rule and the Thanksgiving Hymns. He doesn't go all the way with Eisenman's "James wrote the scrolls" theory, but he affirms the structural parallels: apocalyptic readiness, "preparing the way in the wilderness," baptism for initiation into the community, sacred meals of bread and wine *without* mystical body-and-blood theology, and a "righteous teacher" figure. The piety of Mark, Tabor argues, is *not* close to Qumran. The piety of Paul *is not* close to Qumran. But James and the Jerusalem movement are. That is a strong indicator that the Jerusalem movement's theology is upstream of Qumran-style apocalyptic Judaism — and downstream of *nothing Pauline*.

## Misconceptions Addressed

| Misconception | Tabor's Response |
|---------------|------------------|
| "Paul inherited his gospel from the Jerusalem apostles" | He explicitly denies this in Galatians 1:11-12 — *not man's gospel, not taught it, revelation of Jesus Christ*. Critical scholars (including ones Tabor admires) keep soft-pedaling this verse. Take it at its word. |
| "The 'I received... I delivered' formula in 1 Cor 15 proves apostolic transmission" | Then apply the same logic to 1 Cor 11:23 — where Paul uses the *same verbs* but explicitly names *the Lord* as his source, not the apostles. The formula doesn't settle the source question; Paul's own statements do. |
| "Mark's Last Supper narrative is independent evidence — eyewitnesses told the story" | Tabor reverses it: Mark is *post-Pauline*, written 15-20 years after 1 Corinthians, by someone steeped in Pauline theology. The Last Supper narrative entered the synoptic tradition *through Paul*, not around him. |
| "The synoptic gospels give us independent access to the Jerusalem movement" | They are post-Pauline reflectors of Paul's gospel. Matthew and Luke inherit Mark's Pauline frame. The Q material embedded inside them is the actual non-Pauline survival — but it's hidden in plain sight inside a Pauline container. |
| "James, Peter, and the Twelve preached substitutionary atonement and mystical union with Christ" | There is no evidence of this in *any* non-Pauline source. The Letter of James doesn't have it. Q doesn't have it. The *Didache* Eucharist explicitly *doesn't* have it. Hegesippus has James confessing the Danielic Son of Man, not Isaiah 53. |
| "Paul's mystical Eucharist is the original Christian meal" | The *Didache* — the one early non-Pauline Eucharistic liturgy we have — thanks God for "the vine of David" and "the knowledge made known through Jesus." No body. No blood. No mystical participation. |
| "James the Just is just the local Jerusalem bishop, not head of the movement" | Gospel of Thomas logion 12 calls him *the one for whose sake heaven and earth came into being* — the *tzaddik*, the world-sustaining righteous one. He is the movement's actual successor. |
| "Paul's 'so-called pillars' language is just rhetorical modesty" | Combined with "I worked harder than all of them" (1 Cor 15:10) and the "command of the Lord" authority claim (1 Cor 14:37-38), this is the language of someone who outranks the people who walked with Jesus.
