## Summary

This is Tabor's third pass at the "did Paul think Jesus was God" question — and the most textually granular of the three. The setting is a Zoom session of Tabor's *Quest for the Historical Paul* course, so the tone is classroom: no graphics, no guests, just Tabor with his Greek New Testament walking line-by-line through every passage in the seven undisputed letters that seems to bear on the question. Where Video #08 was the long-form Philippians 2 demolition with Derek Lambert, and Video #12 was the broader survey with Philippians 2 as the climax, **Video #26 is the deep textual dive** — Tabor going slowly through the Hebrew Bible background of "Son of God," the seven specific terms Paul uses for Jesus, and an extended treatment of the "stone passages" in Romans 9-10 that gets almost no airtime in the other two videos.

The argument is the same destination — Paul did not think Jesus was God in the trinitarian sense — but the route is different. Tabor opens with the Hebrew Bible background most modern Christians never see: **every anointed Davidic king of Israel was called "son of God."** Psalm 2:7 ("you are my son, today I have begotten you"), Psalm 110:1 ("the Lord said to my lord, sit at my right hand"), Psalm 89 ("you are my father, my God, the rock of my salvation"), 2 Samuel 7 (Yahweh promising to be father to the Davidic king). Luke 3 takes the genealogy all the way back to "Adam, the son of God." The phrase carries no trinitarian freight in its native context — it's covenantal language for the anointed king, and ultimately for every human being made in God's image. When Paul calls Jesus "Son of God," he's working within this framework, not the fourth-century one.

Tabor then catalogs the **seven terms Paul actually uses for Jesus** (Christ, Lord/*kyrios*, Son of God, Son of David, Firstborn of many brothers, Wisdom of God, Second Adam) — none of which equate Jesus with Yahweh — and walks carefully through six passages: 1 Corinthians 8:6 (one God *and* one Lord), Romans 1:3-4 (designated Son of God *by* resurrection), Galatians 4:4, 1 Corinthians 15:24-28 (Jesus subjected to the Father — "there goes the Trinity"), Philippians 2 (Adam Christology), and the Romans 9-10 "stone" amalgamation where Paul stitches Isaiah 28:16, Isaiah 8:14, and the rejected-cornerstone Psalm into a single Messianic argument. Throughout, Tabor recommends three primary resources for going deeper: **James Dunn's *Christology in the Making*** (the heaviest scholarly treatment), **Kermit Zarley** (for evangelical readers), and **James McGrath's *The Only True God: Early Christian Monotheism in Its Jewish Context*** (the new recommendation, not flagged in #12) — McGrath's thesis being that early Jesus followers were monotheistic; Jesus is exalted to God's right hand and worshipped, but the distinction between Yahweh and Jesus is never collapsed.

This is the video to use when someone wants the **scholarly underpinning** — verse references, Greek terms, scholar names, and the multi-text Hebrew Bible cross-references that the other two videos summarize. It also includes Tabor's most direct statement of a position he flags as **controversial even among his own colleagues**: he does not think Paul believed Jesus pre-existed. Most New Testament scholars disagree. Tabor's reading turns on which letters one accepts as authentically Paul's.

## Key Teachings

### Every Anointed King of Israel Was Called "Son of God"

The piece of background most modern Christians have never been told. The phrase "Son of God" in the Hebrew Bible is *not* unique to Jesus — it's the standard designation for the anointed Davidic king.

- **2 Samuel 7** — Yahweh promises David: "I will be his father, and he shall be my son. When he commits iniquity, I will chasten him with the rod of men."
- **Psalm 2:7** — "I will tell of the decree of the Lord: He said to me, *You are my son, today I have begotten you.*" (Originally a coronation psalm for any Davidic king.)
- **Psalm 110:1** — "The Lord said to my lord, sit at my right hand until I make your enemies a stool for your feet." (The king sits at God's right hand, metaphorically.)
- **Psalm 89** — The Davidic king cries to God: "You are my father, my God, and the rock of my salvation. I will make him firstborn, the highest of the kings of the earth."
- **Luke 3:38** — The genealogy traces Jesus all the way back to "Adam, the son of God."

So when Paul calls Jesus "Son of God," he is operating within this covenantal framework — anointed king, firstborn of a family, sitting at God's right hand as an envoy. Tabor's line: "If somebody asked me 'do you think Jesus is the son of God,' I could say, *Yeah, I believe he was the son of God, and I think David was the son of God too. And I think you're a son of God.*"

This also explains Psalm 89's "I will make him firstborn." Firstborn doesn't mean preexistent divine being — it means *first in rank within a family of children of God*. Paul picks up that meaning directly in Romans 8: Jesus is "the firstborn of many brothers and sisters."

### The Seven Terms Paul Uses for Jesus

Tabor catalogs every title Paul applies to Jesus in the seven undisputed letters. None of them equate Jesus with Yahweh:

1. **Christ** (Messiah, anointed one)
2. **Lord** (*kyrios* — master, sir; in some Jewish texts a woman calls her husband Lord — it's a term of respect, not the divine name)
3. **Son of God** (Davidic-king language; cf. above)
4. **Son of David** (human Davidic descent)
5. **Firstborn of many brothers** (Romans 8:29 — *of many*, not "only-begotten")
6. **Wisdom of God** (1 Cor 1:24)
7. **Second Adam** / **last Adam** (1 Cor 15:45)

Tabor's punchline: "Other than that, he just calls him a man, the man Jesus, the human being." None of these seven titles are *theos* (God). When Paul says "Lord Jesus," the Greek is *kyrios* (master), not the divine name. The English capital-L LORD that translates Yahweh in the Hebrew Bible is a typographical convention; the Greek doesn't make that distinction, so context is everything.

### The Capital-L LORD Problem in English Bibles

A translation issue most readers don't see. In English Bibles, Yahweh is rendered "LORD" (small caps) and Adonai or kyrios is rendered "Lord" (lowercase). Most readers can't tell the difference and conflate them. The old Jerusalem Bible (Catholic) and the public-domain American Standard Version put **Yahweh** or **Jehovah** directly — useful for seeing where the divine name actually appears versus where "lord" just means master.

When Paul says "confess that Jesus is Lord" (Romans 10:9, Philippians 2:11), he uses *kyrios*. Tabor: does Paul mean *master/boss* — which is the normal Greek sense — or does he mean Yahweh, the divine name? Tabor's answer: master/boss. The exaltation is real, but Paul never collapses Jesus into the one God of the Shema.

### 1 Corinthians 8:6 — One God *and* One Lord

The cleanest articulation in Paul. Tabor reads it slowly:

> "For although there may be so-called gods in heaven or on earth — as indeed there are many gods and many lords — yet for us there is **one God, the Father**, from whom are all things and for whom we exist, and **one Lord, Jesus Christ**, through whom are all things and through whom we exist."

Two carefully distinguished categories. The one God is the Father (the creator). The one Lord is Jesus Christ (the agent). Paul is *deliberately* echoing the Shema while creating two distinct slots. "It's very clear. One is creation, one is salvation. Because Paul believes through Jesus, God is reconciling the whole world to himself. To himself, not to Jesus. But Jesus is the instrument."

### Romans 1:3-4 — Designated Son of God *By* Resurrection

Paul's most systematic gospel summary. Standard translations smooth it over, but Tabor reads it tight:

> "The gospel concerning his Son, who was descended from David according to the flesh and **designated** Son of God in power according to the Spirit of holiness **by resurrection from the dead**, Jesus Christ our Lord."

Two pieces matter:

- **"Designated"** translates *horizō* — *marked off, declared*. Jesus is a human being of Davidic descent who was *declared* Son of God at a specific moment.
- **"By resurrection from the dead"** — the Greek doesn't say "by *his* resurrection." It says "by *a* resurrection of the dead." Tabor: this is Paul's biggest idea — there's a *thing* called resurrection of the dead. Not just one corpse coming back. A cosmic process that transforms flesh-and-blood Adams into glorified spirit beings. Jesus is the first one through that door.

This destroys the "Jesus was eternally Son of God" reading. He's *designated* Son of God in power *by* a resurrection process — a process that, in Paul's framework, many more brothers and sisters will undergo.

### 1 Corinthians 15:24-28 — "Then the Son Himself Will Be Subjected" / "There Goes the Trinity"

The most decisive single passage against Pauline trinitarianism, in Tabor's reading. After Jesus reigns and puts all enemies under his feet (cosmic application of Psalm 110), what happens?

> "When all things are subjected to him, then the Son himself will also be subjected to him who put all things under him, that God may be everything to everyone."

Tabor: "**There goes the Trinity.** The Son himself will be subjected. Who put all things under him? God. So they can't be the same."

He also notes Paul's quote of Psalm 8 here ("all things in subjection under his feet"). Psalm 8 is originally about *human beings* — God made humans "a little less than Elohim" and gave them dominion over the earth. Paul applies the verse cosmically to Jesus. But the source verse is about humans being just below the gods — exactly Paul's framework for what Jesus has become and what the followers will become.

### Philippians 2 — Adam Christology, Not Preexistence (Compressed)

Tabor walks Philippians 2 again, briefly, since #08 already did the long form. The compressed version:

- The first four verses (which everyone skips) are about humility — *count others better than yourselves, look to the interests of others*. The "have this mind" of verse 5 *refers back to that*.
- "In the form of God" = Genesis 1:26 (humans in the image and likeness of God).
- "Did not count equality with God a thing to be grasped" = Genesis 3 (Adam *grasping* the fruit to become like God).
- "Emptied himself, took the form of a servant" — opposite of grasping. The second Adam reversed the first Adam's pattern.
- **"Therefore God has highly exalted him and bestowed on him the name above every name"** — equality with God is *given* as a gift, not seized. And it is given *by* God *to* Jesus — two separate parties.
- "Every knee should bow... and every tongue confess Jesus Christ is Lord — **to the glory of God the Father**." Even at the moment of universal acclaim, Paul preserves the distinction. Jesus is the agent through whom the cosmos is brought to glorify the Father.

### The Stone Amalgamation — Romans 9-10 (The Section Most Other Treatments Skip)

This is the most distinctive section of #26 vs. the other two videos. Tabor spends extended time on a textual move that's easy to miss: in Romans 9-10, Paul stitches together **three separate Hebrew Bible passages about a stone** to build his Messianic argument:

1. **Isaiah 28:16** — "I am laying in Zion a foundation, a tested stone, a precious cornerstone. He who believes will not be in haste." (The Hebrew says "not be in haste"; the Septuagint that Paul quotes says "not be put to shame" — Tabor notes Paul is using the Greek, not the Hebrew.)
2. **Isaiah 8:14** — "He [Yahweh] will be a sanctuary, a stone of offense and a rock of stumbling to both houses, a trap and a snare to the inhabitants of Jerusalem."
3. **Psalm 118:22** (alluded to, not quoted by Paul here) — "The stone which the builders rejected has become the chief cornerstone."

Paul amalgamates these into a single sentence: "I am laying in Zion a stone that will make men stumble and a rock that will make them fall, and he who believes in him will not be put to shame." That's *not* what Isaiah 28 actually says. Paul is doing something interpretively creative — taking pieces of three different texts and combining them around the idea that the stone Yahweh lays in Zion *is* the Messiah, and that those who believe in this stone will be vindicated.

This matters for the divinity question because of how Paul then connects it to **Joel 2:32** ("everyone who calls on the name of the Lord will be saved"). Joel's "Lord" is Yahweh. Paul applies it to Jesus. Some readers take this as proof Paul identified Jesus with Yahweh. Tabor's read: Paul sees Jesus as the **agent** — calling on Jesus's name leads to salvation *to the glory of the Father*. It's always derivative. Jesus is the appointed stone *that Yahweh laid*. The laying and the laid are not the same.

### Romans 9:5 — Punctuation Problem, Not Divinity Prooftext

A favorite proof-text Tabor handles quickly. The Greek has no punctuation. Repunctuate naturally:

> "To them belong the patriarchs, and of their race is the Christ according to the flesh. [Doxology:] God who is over all be blessed forever. Amen."

This is **Paul's emotion overflowing** as he laments Israel's situation in Romans 9-11. The "amen" at the end is liturgical — it's a prayer/blessing of God, not a statement that Christ *is* God. Paul never elsewhere calls Jesus God. "Amen" is the tell.

### Tabor's Controversial Position: Paul Did Not Believe Jesus Pre-existed

Tabor flags this as his own position — controversial even among his own colleagues. Most scholars think Paul *did* think Jesus pre-existed in some form. Tabor doesn't. The disagreement turns on which letters with Paul's name one accepts:

- **Seven undisputed letters only:** Tabor says no preexistence is required by the text. Adam Christology (Philippians 2) and exaltation Christology (Romans 1) cover everything.
- **Including Colossians, Ephesians, Pastorals:** Now you have "firstborn of creation, agent of creation" language that *sounds* preexistent — and you have a much stronger basis to read it back into Philippians 2 and the rest.

This is why the methodological fence matters so much. Tabor's case stands or falls on accepting the standard scholarly consensus that Colossians and Ephesians are deutero-Pauline.

## Misconceptions Addressed

| Misconception | Tabor's Response |
|---------------|------------------|
| "Calling Jesus 'Son of God' means Jesus is God" | "Son of God" is the standard Hebrew Bible title for the anointed Davidic king (Psalm 2, Psalm 89, 2 Samuel 7). Adam is also called "son of God" (Luke 3:38). Paul uses it in this covenantal sense, not in the later trinitarian sense of "God the Son." |
| "Calling Jesus 'Lord' (kyrios) means he's Yahweh" | *Kyrios* in Greek normally means *master* or *sir* — Sarah even calls Abraham "lord" in the Septuagint. English Bibles obscure this by using capital-L "LORD" for Yahweh and lowercase "Lord" for kyrios. When Paul says "Jesus is Lord," the Greek is *master*, not the divine name. |
| "Romans 1:4 ('declared Son of God by his resurrection') means Jesus became Son of God because he was resurrected" | The Greek doesn't say "by *his* resurrection" — it says "by a resurrection of the dead." Paul means: there's a cosmic *process* called resurrection of the dead (transformation from flesh to spirit), and Jesus is the first to go through it. He's *designated* (Greek *horizō*) Son of God *in power* by going through that process. |
| "Romans 9:5 calls Jesus 'God over all'" | Punctuation problem. The Greek has no punctuation. Repunctuated naturally: "From them comes the Messiah. [Doxology:] God who is over all be blessed forever. Amen." Paul never elsewhere calls Jesus God. The "amen" at the end is the giveaway — it's a liturgical blessing of God the Father. |
| "Romans 10:13 ('everyone who calls on the name of the Lord') proves Jesus is Yahweh, since Joel 2:32 was about Yahweh" | Paul sees Jesus as the *agent*. Calling on Jesus leads to the Father — it's always "to the glory of God the Father" (Phil 2:11). Jesus is the stone Yahweh *laid* in Zion. The laying and the laid are not the same party. |
| "Philippians 2 ('every knee shall bow') equates Jesus with the Yahweh of Isaiah 45" | Isaiah 45 has Yahweh saying "every knee shall bow." Paul redirects: every knee bows at the name of Jesus *to the glory of God the Father*. Jesus is the agent through whom the cosmos is brought to glorify Yahweh. Agency, not identity. |
| "1 Corinthians 10:4 ('the rock was Christ') means Jesus is the Yahweh of Sinai" | Allegory comparing Christian baptism/eucharist to Israel's wilderness experience. Not ontology. Tabor (in #12): "Paul would be appalled at people calling Jesus the one God who delivered Israel from Egypt." |
| "Galatians 4:4 ('sent forth his son, born of a woman') proves the incarnation" | God also "sent forth" John the Baptist and the prophets. "Sent forth" doesn't entail preexistence. "Born of a woman" is Hebrew idiom for *human as opposed to angelic*. Jesus uses the same phrase about John in the Q source. |
| "1 Corinthians 15:24-28 ('then comes the end... that God may be all in all') is consistent with the Trinity" | "Then the Son himself will be subjected to him who put all things under him." Two distinct subjects. The Son is subordinated to the Father at the end. **"There goes the Trinity."** |
| "Paul's view of Jesus's preexistence is just like John's logos prologue" | The seven undisputed letters never teach preexistence in John's sense.
