## Key Teachings

### Four Pauls: Historical, Secondary, Legendary, Theological

Tabor's opening typology is the methodological core. There is not one Paul but several:

- **Historical Paul** — what we can reconstruct using academic critical methods from the earliest sources
- **Secondary Paul** — material attributed to Paul but written by followers (the disputed and pseudonymous letters)
- **Legendary Paul** — apocryphal traditions, hagiography, later church stories
- **Theological Paul** — the Paul of systematic theology and church doctrine, often a synthesis grafted onto the letters

The course's stated aim is to recover the first — "Paul as he really was" — and to be honest about how the other three layers accumulated on top.

### Why Paul Polarizes — Apostle or Apostate?

The subtitle of the course is deliberately confrontational. Tabor names the two camps with no euphemism:

- Some Christians **build their entire spiritual existence on the revelations Paul said he received from Jesus Christ** — Paul is the apostle, the foundation.
- Others — **including many Christians** — intensely dislike Paul. Sticking points: his statements about women, his confidently-predicted-and-never-arrived apocalypse, his distance from the Jewish Jesus tradition.

The historical-critical question ("what can we actually know?") is the only honest way through the polarization. You can't adjudicate apostle-vs-apostate by raising your voice — you have to do the source work.

### The Course Method Mirrors a University Class

Tabor stresses the course is **modeled after the college-level Paul class he taught for 30+ years** at UNC Charlotte (and earlier at Notre Dame and William and Mary). Components:

- 12 lectures (4K professionally filmed video)
- 60-page course guide (PDF) with maps, outlines, artwork
- A separate PDF printout of the seven undisputed letters with margin space for notes
- Monthly live Zoom Q&A sessions for six months (recorded for those who can't attend live)
- Self-paced — no tests, no exams, no required attendance

The structural commitment: students should **finish all the letters under their belt** ("eat them, devour them, absorb them") before the two "summing up" sessions that close the formal course. New questions will branch out from that foundation in the live Zooms.

### Message and Mission as the Organizing Frame

Tabor mentions the analytical scaffold he uses in the course — borrowed from the subtitle of his earlier book *Paul's Ascent to Paradise*: **"The Apostolic Message and Mission of Paul."** Almost any Pauline topic, he notes, falls under one or the other:

- **Message** — what Paul taught (Christology, eschatology, justification, ethics, the new creation)
- **Mission** — what Paul did (the Gentile turn, the collection for Jerusalem, the church-planting strategy, the conflicts with the Jerusalem pillars)

This is the same split #10 develops in long form. In #62 it's just the labeled box on the course's table of contents.
