## Summary

A wide-ranging two-hour Q&A with MythVision's Derek Lambert covering the core architecture of Tabor's Pauline reading — built around an extended close-reading of Philippians 2 as **Adam Christology** rather than incarnation theology, followed by audience super-chat questions that pull Tabor into Romans 9:5 translation problems, Enoch's garments tradition, the Talpiot tomb, Hercules and pagan apotheosis parallels, Eastern Orthodox theosis, and a closing meditation on why Tabor spent 60 years inside Second Temple Judaism rather than (his wife's suggestion) Shakespeare.

The episode's structural backbone is the **Two Adams** framework. Adam-1 in Eden was told by the *nachash* ("slick one," not "Satan") that he could be equal to God by grasping the fruit — he did, and was sent back to the dust. Adam-2 (Yeshua, born of a woman, fully human) reversed this: did NOT grasp at equality with God, but emptied himself, took the form of a *doulos* (slave), and went to the lowest point — execution as a criminal. Therefore God exalted him as the firstborn of a new humanity. This is not pre-existent God-becomes-man-then-becomes-God-again (the Mediterranean incarnation pattern). It's the **first successful Adam** — the prototype of a transformed humanity. Believers are "many sons of God" in waiting; the cosmos itself is "groaning in labor pains" to give birth to this new family.

The Q&A section is dense with payoff moments: Tabor's response to a Liberty University apologist's "minimal facts" approach to the resurrection (rejected — Paul is our ONLY first-person witness, and Paul says he cannot describe the resurrection body); the Romans 9:5 punctuation problem (whether Jesus is "God over all blessed forever" or not depends on where you place the comma, and Tabor accepts the ambiguity); the Apollos Christian Apologetics question about the Carmen Christi (Tabor recommends Charles H. Talbert's 1967 JBL article "The Problem of Pre-Existence in Philippians 2:6-11" — the foundational Adam-Christology paper); the *avaros* question about ossuaries and bones (Tabor describes the actual Talpiot inscription "O God Yahweh raise up raise up" he and Simcha Jacobovici discovered with a robotic camera). The conversation closes on a deeply personal note: Tabor's hook isn't justification-by-faith, it's the cosmic-transformation moment when one Adam finally got it right.

## Key Teachings

### The Two Adams Framework (Philippians 2:5-11)

Tabor's signature reading of the Carmen Christi as Adam-typology, not incarnation:

**Adam-1 (Eden):**
- "In the form of God" (Genesis 1:26-27 — image and likeness)
- The *nachash* ("slick one" / "shining one" — NOT Satan in the original Hebrew) tells him he can be like God if he eats the fruit
- Adam-1 grasps at equality with God → expelled to the dust → "dust you are, to dust you shall return"

**Adam-2 (Yeshua):**
- Also "in the form of God" (born of a woman, fully human, made in God's image)
- Does NOT grasp at equality with God
- Empties himself, takes the form of a *doulos* (slave — the lowest social rank)
- Obedient unto death, even crucifixion (the most shameful death in the Roman world)
- THEREFORE God highly exalted him → name above every name → Lord of the cosmos

The structural inversion is the whole point. Adam-1 *grasped*. Adam-2 *gave*. The first Adam tried to *take* equality; the second Adam *received* it as gift after total self-emptying. This is not God-becomes-human-then-becomes-God-again. It's the **first human to successfully traverse the cosmic transformation** from dust-creature to glorified ruler.

### Why "In the Form of God" Doesn't Mean "Pre-Existent Divine Being"

The standard incarnation reading: Jesus was eternally God, gave it up, came to earth, died for sins, returned to heaven. Tabor's objections:

1. **Adam was also "in the form of God"** (Genesis 1:26 — God made humans in his image and likeness). The phrase doesn't require pre-existence; it describes the image-of-God status all humans share.
2. **The parallel structure makes this clear:** "in the form of God" parallels "in the likeness of men" / "in human form" later in the hymn. Both phrases describe the *same* being — a human being made in God's image.
3. **The point of the hymn would be undermined** if Jesus were already God: how can you imitate (v. 5 — "have this mind in you") a divine pre-existent being's choice to descend? Only an Adam-like Jesus offers a pattern other Adams can follow.
4. **No "emptying" of divinity required** — Jesus emptied himself of *ego*, of self-promotion, of the human grasp-at-equality that doomed Adam-1.

Tabor recommends Charles H. Talbert's foundational 1967 JBL article, "The Problem of Pre-Existence in Philippians 2:6-11" — the article many subsequent Adam-Christology readings stem from.

### Paul's Big Religious Question Is NOT "How Do I Get Saved"

A core methodological reframe. The Lutheran/Augustinian tradition read Paul as fundamentally about *individual salvation*: the guilt-ridden self seeks deliverance, justification by faith resolves the existential crisis. Tabor rejects this read for **Paul himself** (it may fit Luther, but it doesn't fit Paul).

Paul's actual question: **How is it that a human being — born of a woman, flesh and blood, dust — has been exalted into the highest heaven and become ruler of the cosmos?**

The question is **cosmological**, not personal-soteriological. Justification by faith is *real* in Paul, but it's the *prerequisite* for participating in cosmic transformation — not the destination. The destination is becoming one of "many sons of God" ruling the cosmos with the firstborn Adam-2.

### 1 Corinthians 15 Is the Transformation-of-Cosmos Chapter

The chapter every Bible labels "the resurrection chapter" Tabor renames. It's about **metamorphosis** — Greek *metamorphoo*, the literal caterpillar-to-butterfly word. The dead come up *incidentally* — Paul addresses them because the Corinthians worried whether those who died would miss out on the imminent transformation.

The actual subject: **the entire cosmos** undergoes transformation at the parousia. Living and dead together are metamorphosed in "a moment, in the twinkling of an eye, at the last trumpet" from corruptible to incorruptible, mortal to "life-giving spirit." The body Paul describes is NOT a resuscitated corpse. He explicitly says: "I cannot tell you what kind of body" — uses the seed/plant analogy (the oak doesn't look like the acorn).

### Paul Is the Only First-Person Witness — And His Account Cannot Be Used Apologetically

Tabor's challenge to Gary Habermas / William Lane Craig / Liberty University apologetics:

- The Gospels (Matthew, Luke, John) are SECONDHAND. Luke explicitly says he wasn't an eyewitness (Luke 1:1-4). Mark's oldest manuscripts have no appearances at all.
- Paul claims direct first-person experience: "I have seen the Lord" (1 Cor 9:1); "last of all he appeared to me" (1 Cor 15:8). Tabor prefers the term "sighting" over "appearance."
- But when asked what the resurrection body looks like, **Paul says he cannot describe it**. He doesn't say "I ate fish with him" or "I touched his wounds." His experience was a *blinding light*, possibly a *voice*; in 2 Corinthians 12 he says he doesn't know "whether in the body or out of the body."

The apologetic argument requires *physical* eyewitness encounter. The actual first-person witness reports something *visionary*. The apologetic argument has therefore been built on the *late embellished* gospel accounts, with the actual first-person witness conveniently forgotten.

### The Two-Body Framework (2 Corinthians 5)

Paul's two analogies for what happens at the transformation:

**Clothing analogy:** Your body is like old clothes. At death you take them off — you're "naked" (in an interim state of nothingness/sleep). Then you receive **new clothes** — a glorified body — *further* clothing. Paul explicitly does NOT want to be found "naked" but "further clothed." The new body is not the old body resuscitated; it's a new garment entirely.

**House analogy:** "If the earthly tent is destroyed, we have a building from God, a house not made with hands, eternal in the heavens." The dwelling-place changes, the inhabitant doesn't.

Neither analogy requires the original body to come back. **Paul doesn't care about Jesus's bones.** "If you found a finger of Adam, what would Paul care? Adam went back to the dust. Jesus did the same. Jesus's body probably rotted in a reburial tomb. What got exalted was the *new* Adam-2."

### The Reburial Hypothesis (Anti-Empty-Tomb Apologetic)

Tabor's reconstruction (also covered in #22, #36):
- Jesus crucified Friday afternoon
- Joseph of Arimathea takes the body to a *tomb of convenience* near the cross — Passover is starting, you cannot leave bodies exposed
- Sometime after Passover, the burial party (Joseph) moves the body to its proper family tomb
- That tomb may be the Talpiot tomb, where Tabor and Simcha Jacobovici discovered an inscription "O God Yahweh raise up raise up" via robotic camera

This explains:
- The empty first tomb (Mary Magdalene's John 20 report: "they have taken the Lord and we don't know where they laid him" — natural confusion of someone arriving Sunday morning to find the temporary tomb empty because the body was moved)
- The body never being produced by opponents (it's in another tomb; ossuary not yet collected)
- Why this DOESN'T affect Paul's framework — Paul doesn't need the body to be missing for the resurrection to be real

### Albert Schweitzer's "Mysticism of Paul the Apostle"

Tabor's most-recommended book on Paul. Schweitzer's argument: Paul is apocalyptic-mystical at the core, not Lutheran-judicial. Believers are *united with Christ* in a participatory eschatological transformation. Justification by faith is real but secondary; the primary frame is mystical participation.

What makes Schweitzer's book remarkable: he intuited the framework *without* the Dead Sea Scrolls (discovered 1947). Now, with scrolls like the Hodayot (Thanksgiving Hymns), we can read the Qumran community's leader saying things like "I sit among the gods, I have been exalted, I am above all the Elohim" — confirming Schweitzer's mystical-apocalyptic Judaism framework from inside the source material.

### Romans 9:5 — The Translation Crux for Paul's Christology

The verse that could read either "Christ, who is God over all, blessed forever" (Trinitarian-friendly) OR "Christ... God who is over all, blessed forever" (separating Christ from God). Metzger's textual commentary identifies *three* possible punctuation schemes, all defensible from the Greek.

Tabor's response: "I don't know how to translate it." Either reading is consistent with his framework — *if* Christ is "God over all," it's because he was *exalted* to that status (Phil 2:9 — "therefore God highly exalted him"), not because he was eternally God. The Adam-Christology reading absorbs either translation.

### Tabor Is "Not a Believing Christian" — His Project Is Historical-Critical

A clarifying moment. Tabor is interested in *what Paul actually thought*, not in defending or attacking Christianity. His own faith stance is "very interested in what Paul taught and in getting the idea straight" — but he is not personally a believing Christian.

This is methodologically important: it lets Tabor read Paul without protecting the church's later doctrinal investment in him. Paul can be wrong about the timing of the parousia, can have misread Habakkuk and Genesis 15, can be a "second Christ" figure, can have presented his gospel as foreign to James — and none of this is a problem for Tabor because his task is to understand the historical Paul, not to defend Pauline Christianity.

### The Eastern Orthodox Apotheosis Parallel

Eastern Orthodox Christianity preserves a Pauline-friendly framework Western Christianity lost: **theosis** — believers becoming "partakers of the divine nature" (2 Peter 1:4). Becoming God (small "g") through grace, not as ontologically equal to the Father but as part of the Divine family.

Tabor notes this fits Paul better than Augustinian Western theology does. The Western tradition treats the Adam-1/Adam-2 framework as a one-time substitutionary exchange (Christ paid the price, you accept by faith). The Eastern tradition treats it as a participatory process — Christ is the *firstborn* of many who will share the same exaltation.

### Why Tabor Spent 60 Years on Second Temple Judaism (M to M)

A closing personal reflection. Tabor's wife once asked: with the same time investment, you could be the world's foremost Shakespeare expert. Why this?

His answer: Second Temple Judaism (roughly 200 BCE to 200 CE — "from the Maccabees to the Mishnah") is "the M to M period" where everything that became Western religious civilization flowed through a 400-year bottleneck. The Hebrew Bible's whole prophetic tradition, Hellenization, Roman imperialism, apocalyptic literature, the Dead Sea Scrolls, the historical Jesus, rabbinic Judaism, Christianity — all of it crystallized in those four centuries. Even Eastern influences (Zoroastrianism, Persian thought) flowed in via the Silk Road.

"That's my thing. The M to M. And everything influencing Western history flowed out of that."

## Misconceptions Addressed

| Misconception | Tabor's Response |
|---------------|------------------|
| "Philippians 2 proves Jesus pre-existed as God, gave it up, became man, then got it back" | Read as Adam-Christology: Jesus, like Adam, was "in the form of God" (humans bear God's image); unlike Adam, didn't grasp at equality. Therefore exalted as firstborn of a new humanity. The Mediterranean incarnation pattern (god comes down, then returns) is NOT what Paul is saying. |
| "We have eyewitness testimony to the resurrection" | Of the seven undisputed Pauline letters, Paul alone is first-person. The Gospels are secondhand. Luke explicitly says he's not an eyewitness. Mark in oldest manuscripts has no appearances at all. |
| "Paul's resurrection experience matches the gospel encounter stories" | Paul describes a vision he cannot describe and admits doesn't know "whether in the body or out of the body." That's not Thomas touching wounds or breakfast on the beach. |
| "1 Corinthians 15 is the proof of bodily resurrection" | It's the transformation-of-cosmos chapter. Paul explicitly says he cannot describe the resurrection body. Uses seed/plant analogy. Calls the new Adam "a life-giving spirit." "Flesh and blood cannot inherit the Kingdom" (1 Cor 15:50). |
| "Paul's gospel is justification by faith / forgiveness of sins" | That's there but secondary. Paul's primary frame is cosmic transformation — many sons of God exalted as Adams to rule the cosmos with Christ as firstborn. |
| "The empty tomb proves the resurrection" | The empty tomb could be explained by reburial — the temporary Passover tomb of convenience was emptied when the burial party moved the body to its proper family tomb. This doesn't disturb Pauline framework at all because Paul doesn't need the body to be missing. |
| "Romans 9:5 settles that Paul called Jesus God" | The Greek is ambiguous — three defensible punctuation schemes. Even if it does call Jesus "God over all," that's because he was *exalted* to that status, not because he was pre-existent. |
| "If Jesus's bones were found, Christianity is finished" | Not for Pauline Christianity. Paul's framework is cosmic transformation — Jesus's earthly body went back to the dust like every Adam's. What got exalted was the new Adam-2 in a "life-giving spirit" body. |
| "Galatians 4:14 — Paul thought Jesus was an angel" | The Greek *angelos* just means "messenger." Paul is praising the Corinthians for receiving him "as if I were an angel/messenger of God" — describing how they treated him despite his physical infirmities, not making Christological claims about Jesus's ontological status. |
| "Resurrection means body-and-soul reunited" | Paul uses two analogies — clothing and house. Both describe the inhabitant putting on something *new*, not the original clothing/house being reanimated. The "naked" intermediate state (death) is followed by *further* clothing — not the same clothing. |
