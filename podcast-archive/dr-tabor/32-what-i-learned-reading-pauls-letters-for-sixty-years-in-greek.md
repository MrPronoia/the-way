## Summary

This is Tabor reading aloud the Preface to his 2012 book *Paul and Jesus: How the Apostle Transformed Christianity* — a personal, reflective piece. He frames his entire career as an attempt to investigate **the silence between two back-to-back lines of the Apostles' Creed**: that Jesus was "born of the Virgin Mary" and "crucified, died, and buried." The creed jumps from birth to death and skips Jesus's life entirely. How did Christmas and Easter — the entrance and exit points of the heavenly visitor — come to define the religion, when the actual life and teachings of the historical Jewish Messiah largely dropped out? Tabor's answer: this whole framework comes from **one man — Saul of Tarsus** — not from Jesus, Peter, John, or James. And there was a pre-Pauline Christianity, affirmed by Jesus and his original followers, with tenets "sharply opposed" to Paul's, with "little in common beyond the name Jesus itself."

The personal frame: Tabor encountered Paul at age 17 in a Greek reading course taught by Abraham Malherbe, going phrase-by-phrase through the letters in the original Greek. He was hooked for life. PhD at Chicago on Paul (the dissertation became *Things Unutterable*). Forty-five years of teaching the course just titled "Paul." His first-day-of-class line to students: **"Paul is the most influential person in human history."** The piece ends with Tabor describing his May 2010 pilgrimage to the newly excavated tomb of Paul at the Basilica San Paolo Outside the Walls in Rome — Vatican carbon-14 testing in 2009 confirmed the bones date to the late 1st or early 2nd century. Kneeling at the sarcophagus, Tabor felt his 45-year quest had come full circle.

## Key Teachings

### The Silence the Creed Skips Over

The Apostles' Creed jumps from "born of the Virgin Mary" directly to "crucified, died, and buried." **The entire life of Jesus — the teachings, the kingdom proclamation, the ethics, the way he actually lived — is absent from the foundational confession of Christianity.** Tabor calls this "striking" and treats the question of how this happened as the organizing problem of his career. The Christmas-and-Easter religion is a religion of entry and exit points. The middle — what Jesus actually said and did — went missing.

His thesis in one line: "To be a Christian is to believe in the virgin birth and resurrection of Christ" — and that framework originates not from Jesus or the original apostles but from **Saul of Tarsus.**

### Two Christianities, Sharply Opposed

Tabor states it directly: there was a version of Christianity before Paul — "the lost and forgotten Christianity of James the brother of Jesus, leader of the movement following Jesus's death, and the Christianity of Peter and all the apostles." This pre-Pauline Christianity affirmed things "quite opposite" to Paul's gospel. **The message of Paul and the message of the historical Jesus and his earliest followers "were not the same. In fact, they were sharply opposed to one another with little in common beyond the name Jesus itself."**

This is the consistent thesis underneath all of Tabor's Paul work — Christianity as we know it is Pauline Christianity, layered on top of and obscuring a different, earlier Jewish-Messianic movement.

### Why Greek Matters — The Phrase-by-Phrase Encounter

Tabor's seminal experience was a Greek reading course taught by Abraham Malherbe at age 17. The class went through Paul's letters **phrase by phrase in the original Greek**, in a small seminar setting, discussing every aspect — ideas, background, context. This is the methodological foundation Tabor returns to again and again: you cannot understand Paul through translation. The English smooths over the wrestling. The Greek surfaces it.

He likens his own engagement to Jacob wrestling the angel at the Jabbok: "I lay hold on Paul and swore that I would not let go until I understood his complex ideas and probed his mysterious depths." Sixty years later, he's still wrestling.

### "Paul Is the Most Influential Person in Human History"

Tabor's opening-day line to students. He admits the language is "somewhat extravagant" but defends it: Western civilization's basic assumptions about reality, ethics, and selfhood rest on Paul. Every mainstream Christian service — Catholic, Protestant, Greek Orthodox — is built on Paul's theological vocabulary: the creeds, the hymns, the sermons, the sacraments of baptism and Eucharist. Birth, baptism, confirmation, marriage, death — **"it is predominantly Paul who is invoked to express meaning and significance."**

Meanwhile, "Jesus as a historical figure — a Jewish Messiah of his own time who sought to see the kingdom of God established on Earth — has been largely lost to our culture."

### Paul's Doctrines vs. Jesus's Mission

Tabor enumerates the doctrines that trace back to Paul, not Jesus:
- Christ is God born in the flesh
- His sacrificial death atones for the sins of humankind
- His resurrection guarantees eternal life to believers
- Spiritual union with Christ through baptism
- Communion with his body and blood through bread and wine

**Every one of these doctrinal pillars is Pauline.** None of them comes from the historical Jesus's actual proclamation — which was about the in-breaking of the kingdom of God on earth, a Jewish-Messianic mission.

This is the Christianity that "separated from Judaism and put it on the road to becoming a new religion."

### The Pilgrimage to Paul's Tomb

The piece closes with Tabor's May 2010 trip to Rome — visiting the sarcophagus at the Basilica San Paolo Outside the Walls. Discovered by Vatican archaeologists in 2006 just below the central altar, dated to the 4th century AD, inscribed *Paulo Apostolo Mart* ("To Paul Apostle Martyr"). Tradition says Paul was beheaded under Nero at Tre Fontane at the end of the Via Laurentina, buried two miles north along the Ostian Way. Constantine built the original small basilica over the cemetery in AD 324.

In 2009, Pope Benedict XVI announced the Vatican had carried out carbon-14 testing — a clandestine night operation, drilling a small hole through the sarcophagus to extract bone and fabric samples. The bones dated to the late 1st or early 2nd century AD. **For Tabor, this is one of the rare Catholic relic sites that may actually be authentic** — he says most lack any historical authenticity, but this one "seems different."

He describes kneeling before the tomb, surrounded by pilgrims and photographers, deeply moved: "Somehow that physical proximity to what might likely be Paul's earthly remains marked a milestone to my 45-year search for the historical Paul. I felt I had come full circle."
