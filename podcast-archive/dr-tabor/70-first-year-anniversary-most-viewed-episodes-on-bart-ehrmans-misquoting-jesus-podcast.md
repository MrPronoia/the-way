## Summary

A short anniversary clip from Bart Ehrman's *Misquoting Jesus* podcast revealing the platform's first-year metrics — **3.7 million YouTube views and 32,000 audio downloads** — and the two most-watched episodes of the year. Production manager Chris Huntley compiled the stats, and Ehrman walks through the result on air: **Dr. James Tabor's interview on Paul was the second most-watched episode of the entire year**, beaten only by Ehrman's own episode "Why You Don't Believe in God."

The clip is essentially Ehrman tipping his hat to the popularity of the Tabor-on-Paul conversation — confirming that the historical-Paul material (what we can know about Paul and how we know it) struck a major nerve with the audience. Ehrman also reflects on the surprise hits and misses of the year (an interview with a Ukrainian theologian arguing that Ukrainian Christians see the US as the Antichrist underperformed), and notes that personal-belief and Trinity content consistently drew large audiences alongside Paul-focused episodes.

---

## Key Teachings

### Tabor on Paul Was the Year's #2 Episode

Ehrman names the two most-watched episodes of the first year directly: #1 was "Why You Don't Believe in God" (Ehrman's personal-faith episode), and #2 was his interview with **Dr. James Tabor on the historical Paul**. The Tabor episode covered "our quest for the historical Paul — what we know and how we know it." This is meaningful signal: among hundreds of hours of biblical scholarship content, audiences responded most strongly to (1) Ehrman's deconversion story and (2) Tabor's recovery of Paul.

### What Audiences Want: Paul, Trinity, Personal Belief

Ehrman's read of the year's metrics: episodes on Paul consistently overperformed, episodes on the Trinity overperformed, and any episode touching personal belief overperformed. Scholarly-but-niche material (e.g., the Ukrainian theology interview) underperformed even when Ehrman thought the content was excellent. The audience is hungry for the foundational figures and the personal-stakes questions, not the academic curiosities.

### Scholarship and Faith Are Not Mutually Exclusive

In reflecting on the #1 episode, Ehrman draws a careful line: "you can agree with the kind of scholarship I do and still be a believing Christian." He emphasizes he never tries to deconvert anyone — students often tell him their faith was *strengthened* by his work. The reasons he personally left faith are separate from the historical-critical scholarship itself. This framing matters for how viewers approach Tabor's material too: critical scholarship on Paul, the resurrection, and Christian origins doesn't require abandoning faith.
