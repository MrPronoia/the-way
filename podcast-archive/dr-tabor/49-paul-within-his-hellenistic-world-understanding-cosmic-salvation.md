## Summary

This is the direct companion to Tabor playlist #31 ("How Greek Were Paul's Core Ideas?") — but with the added voltage of a live dialogue between Tabor and M. David Litwa, two of the field's leading scholars on Paul-within-the-Greco-Roman-world, hosted by Derek Lambert. Where #31 is Tabor's classroom version of the thesis (Paul uses Hellenistic vocabulary but operates from a Hebrew Bible apocalyptic frame), this episode shows what happens when Tabor's cosmic-salvation reading meets Litwa's even more aggressive insistence that **Judaism is itself a Hellenistic religion** and that the apologetic protection of Paul "from the pagan world" must be dismantled. The conversation roams across cosmology, magic, deification, the law given by angels, Paul's sexuality, the bodily ascension, and Philippians 2 — all under the umbrella of the central question: *what is salvation for Paul, and what is the cosmos he thinks we're being saved out of?*

Both scholars converge on a single move: **back off**. Don't microscope two chapters of Romans your whole life. Step back and see Paul in the wider ancient Mediterranean — Lucian, the Mithras Liturgy, the Greek magical papyri, the Hermetica, Enoch, the Hekhalot literature, the Ascension of Isaiah, the Nag Hammadi corpus, the Naassene Preacher. The reason matters: when you read Paul inside that broader world, salvation stops being "your sins are forgiven so you can go to heaven when you die" and becomes what Tabor calls the real Paul — a man trapped in a lower cosmos under principalities and powers, trying to figure out how to ascend through the layered heavens, get a new body fit for the upper ecology, and join (or *become*) the immortal ones. The judicial/courtroom reading of salvation (Augustine, Luther, modern evangelicalism) is what Krister Stendahl correctly diagnosed as the *introspective conscience of the Western world* projected backward onto Paul. Paul's real concern is the mortal flesh, the dying universe, and the cosmic war with the rulers of this age.

Litwa pushes hard on the politics of Pauline interpretation: orthodox readings have always wanted to *protect* Paul — protect him from "pagan stuff," protect him by sealing him inside a constructed "Judaism" that is itself a Christian apologetic invention. The early-20th-century *Religionsgeschichtliche Schule* (history-of-religions school) in Germany broke that seal, and the mid-century neo-orthodox backlash slammed it shut again. Litwa wants it broken again. Tabor agrees but holds that Paul's apocalyptic framework — two ages, cosmic war, Belial/Satan ruling the present age, the new creation breaking in — comes from the Hebrew Bible / Dead Sea Scrolls stream, not from Plato or the mystery cults. So the synthesis you walk out with: Hellenistic vocabulary, apocalyptic Jewish grammar, and a target (deification / becoming a god) that the later church had to flatten because it threatened the unique deity of Jesus.

The third hour of the conversation (which the Q&A unlocks) gets to the payoff: Paul's gospel is **apotheosis**. Litwa argues deification means quite literally *becoming a deus* — an immortal being with superhuman power, the same category as the Roman emperor, Heracles, Asclepius, Romulus. Tabor agrees and points to Philippians 2 (Adam Christology — Jesus as the model for everyone, not the unique exception) and the Naassene Preacher's late-second-century reading of Paul's ascent as deification. The Orthodox tradition kept the *word* theosis but, by the third and fourth centuries, walled off an "iron ceiling" of ontology that Paul never had: a metaphysical gap between God and creation that the deified can never actually cross. The episode closes by saying what most modern preaching cannot say without losing its job — Paul thought Christians were becoming gods.

## Key Teachings

### Back Off and See the Mediterranean World

Tabor opens with his University of Chicago teacher Jonathan Z. Smith ("the wizard of Hellenistic religions"). Smith's training move was simple: don't spend your life microscoping two chapters of Romans. *Back off.* Look at the whole ancient Mediterranean — Lucian the satirist, the Mithras Liturgy in the Greek magical papyri, Poimandres in the Corpus Hermeticum, the Hekhalot mystical texts, the Nag Hammadi corpus. Smith insisted Tabor could just as legitimately have written his New Testament dissertation on any of those bodies of literature. Why? Because they're all asking the same question: *how do you get out of this lower cosmos and back to God?*

That's the high-end PhD-level move. And it's exactly what Litwa's career is about — his book titles (*Mediterranean Myths*, *Mediterranean Thought*, *Becoming Divine*) name the project. Paul is one node in a Mediterranean conversation, not a hermetically sealed Jewish island floating in an alien sea.

### The Politics of Protecting Paul

Litwa names the unspoken rule of two centuries of Pauline scholarship: **protect Paul**. Orthodox readers want Paul protected from the Greco-Roman world. "Pagan" carries a negative edge — it's the bad stuff. So if you can put Paul inside Judaism, and Judaism inside a sealed-off "Hebrew" container, you can keep him safe from the contamination of mystery cults, magic, deification, multiple heavens, archons, and astral immortality.

The problem: Litwa argues Judaism is *itself* a Hellenistic religion. It isn't exceptional. There is no "judeo-Christian buffer zone" that protects Paul (or Jesus) from the Mediterranean world. The "judeo-Christian tradition" was invented by Christians, not Jews, and it functions as an apologetic shield. This goes back thousands of years and "must be resisted at all costs."

This isn't an attack on Jewish context. It's a refusal to let the Jewish context be weaponized to keep Paul ontologically separated from things like the Greek magical papyri, which Paul absolutely sounds like in places.

### What Is Salvation for Paul?

Both scholars hit the same drum: salvation, for Paul, is *not* judicial. It is not "you stand before a judge, you're declared guilty, Jesus pays your bail, you walk free." That's E.P. Sanders's diagnosis (and Stendahl's *Paul and the Introspective Conscience of the West*): the courtroom reading is a Western projection.

Paul's real worry: **you are trapped in this lower cosmos**, surrounded by principalities and powers, in a mortal dying body in a dying universe, and *how do you get out*? How do you ascend through the seven planetary spheres, through the angelic guardians, past the "god of this world" (Satan, 2 Cor 4:4), and join the ultimate God who is beyond all gods? That's the question. Sins-forgiven is *a* piece of it, not the engine.

Tabor: "Salvation is how are you going to live trapped in this lower cosmos with all the angels and principalities bearing down upon you, and how are you ever going to be able to ascend out of this and join the ultimate God?"

### The Cosmos as a Real Physical Architecture

Litwa hammers on this: the multi-layered heavens were not metaphors and they were not "dimensions." They were **real cosmic spaces**. Ancients believed if you had a vehicle (a Lucianic spaceship in *True History*, a magical technique like Poimandres or the Mithras Liturgy, or a divinely granted vision like Paul's) you could literally travel up there.

How many levels? They disagreed:
- Paul: at least three (third heaven = paradise)
- Ascension of Isaiah: seven
- 2 Enoch (late 1st c. Alexandria): ten
- Letter of Eugnostos (Nag Hammadi): 360
- Basilides (per Irenaeus): 365 (one per day of the year)

The disagreement on the *number* is itself the proof that the *reality* was assumed. They argued about how many floors the building had because they all agreed the building was there.

And the beings in those layers were **material** — composed of refined air, ether, or pneuma. Not invisible-incorporeal-modern-spirit-stuff. More like the shimmer of heat off a hot road. Smoky. Diaphanous. They could breathe the smoke of sacrifices. They could give laws. They could harass you. The "spiritual" in the ancient world is a *kind* of body, not a non-body.

### Magic, Religion, and the False Dichotomy

Tabor and Litwa both demolish the religion-vs-magic divide. The standard Christian apologetic move: religion is what *we* do (pious devotion to the true God); magic is what *they* do (pagan paraphernalia, spells, manipulation). Tabor cites Martin Smith ("one person's religion is another person's magic") and Durkheim ("there is no church of magic").

Concretely:
- When Jesus spits on the ground, makes mud, touches eyes, says *ephphatha* — that's "religious healing"
- When Asclepius or Apollonius of Tyana does the exact same thing — that's "magic"

It's apologetic gerrymandering. Paul curses people from a distance (Galatians 1:8-9 — "let him be anathema"). He delivers the offender in 1 Corinthians 5 over to Satan for the destruction of the flesh. He *projects his spirit* into the Corinthian house church when they assemble (1 Cor 5:3-5). These are not differences in kind from what scholars call magic in non-Christian texts.

Tabor: "If you don't like the word *magic*, call it a spiritual curse. But it's the same operation."

Poimandres and the Mithras Liturgy involve no paraphernalia. You go into meditation and ascend. The Egyptian gold-foil burial tablets (third c. BCE, 17 known specimens) tell the dead how to negotiate the cosmic gatekeepers: *show some spunk, name the powers, declare your right to pass*. That's exactly what a Christian would do, ascending in the name of Jesus. Same structure, different agents.

### The Torah Was Given by Angels (And Maybe Bad Ones)

Lambert pulls Tabor and Litwa into Galatians 3:19 — "the Torah was ordained by angels through an intermediary." Why does Paul say that? It sounds suspiciously like a slam.

Litwa goes hard: Epistle of Barnabas 9:4 calls the angel who gave the Jews physical circumcision an *aggelos poneros* (evil angel) who "played the sophist" with them. Barnabas thinks Paul is saying the Jewish ritual laws were given by tricky lower archons to deceive Israel — that physical circumcision, Sabbath, dietary laws are all the work of these intermediate beings, not the will of God. This is reception history, but it's *very early* reception history (mid-second century at the latest).

Is that what Paul meant? Tabor leans there too. Keep reading into Galatians 4 — the *stoicheia tou kosmou* (elemental spirits of the cosmos) Paul says enslaved both Jews and Gentiles. "You observed days, seasons, months, and years" — that's calendar religion under planetary archons. Paul thinks the Torah ritual law was part of this tutelage system, now made obsolete by the arrival of the seed (Christ). The Decalogue itself may be from God (through angelic mediation), but the 613 commandments of ritual observance are part of a holding pattern under lower powers — and Paul thinks that pattern has expired.

This is *not* full-blown Gnosticism (no evil demiurge), but it has the same architecture: lower cosmic rulers, intermediate revelation, escape now possible.

### Resurrection IS Ascension IS Deification

Litwa's structural insight: in the late first century, most Christians did **not** separate resurrection, ascension, and deification into three events. They were a single transformation.

- Paul's "meeting the Lord in the air" (1 Thess 4:17) — that's resurrection *and* ascension
- The thief on the cross: "today you will be with me in paradise" — instant rise
- 2 Corinthians 5: tent torn down, permanent dwelling acquired — same metamorphosis
- 1 Corinthians 15: sown physical, raised spiritual — same change

You need a new body to survive in the upper ecology. Litwa's analogy: if you went to Mars in this body, you'd die in seconds. You need a spacesuit. The resurrection body is the spacesuit — a *soma pneumatikon*, possibly something like a photonic body, a star body. Paul says the stars differ in glory and so will the risen. The new body lasts much longer than this 40-to-80 year mortal envelope.

It's only the author of Acts (second century, Tabor argues) who separates resurrection and ascension by 40 days. Mark, with the short ending at 16:8, has no resurrection appearances at all — because resurrection *is* ascension for Mark. The Transfiguration (Mark 9) is the closest thing: Jesus, Moses, and Elijah shining like the stars forever (Daniel 12), already inside the upper ecology.

The thread that ties all of this together: **resurrection-ascension-deification are one event**. You rise from the dust into the photonic ecology, you get a body that fits, and you become an immortal being with superhuman power. That is *theos* in the Greco-Roman world. That's deification.

### Philippians 2 as Adam Christology

Both Tabor and Litwa converge (with Charles Talbert, James Dunn, and others) on a non-pre-existent reading of Philippians 2:5-11:

- "Though he was in the *form* (morphe) of God" — so was Adam (Genesis 1:26-27, *kat' eikona* — in the image)
- "Did not consider equality with God a thing to be grasped" — Adam *did* grasp. The serpent (*nachash*, "the shining one") said: *eat and you will be as gods* (Genesis 3:5)
- "Emptied himself, took the form of a servant" — Jesus chose the path Adam refused
- "Therefore God exalted him" — exaltation as response, not as homecoming

This isn't a divine being descending from heaven, dying, and re-ascending. It's two Adams contrasted. And critically, **Philippians 2 is not just about Jesus** — it's the model for everyone. Romans 8:17 — "provided that we suffer with him, that we may be glorified with him." If you skip the suffering and grab the fruit, you end up outside the gates of Eden, dying. If you act like the second Adam, you get exalted to God's right hand. Jesus is the firstborn of many, not the unique exception.

Tabor: "Many people would say, well, *he* was exalted, not you. But Paul makes it very clear that the saints are going to be exalted. Jesus is the firstborn of many. He showed how to do it."

### Deification Means Becoming a God (Not a "Like-God")

This is the cleanest Litwa moment in the conversation. What does deification mean in the ancient world?

- A *theos* is an immortal being with superhuman power
- Power = great intelligence, great beauty, or great strength (Heracles)
- The Roman emperor was rationally called *theos* — not as political games, but because ancients believed an immortal genius animated him and he sometimes performed miracles (Vespasian)
- So when Paul goes around the Greco-Roman world saying "you can become immortal like the emperor, like Heracles, like Asclepius, like Romulus, and you'll have superhuman cosmic power because you'll judge the angels" — *that's a coherent message in his cultural milieu*

The Naassene Preacher (late second century Alexandria) is the first reception-history witness to read Paul's ascent as deification. He says Paul became a god when he ascended to the third heaven. Litwa stresses: this isn't his invention. He's reading Paul the way a contemporary or near-contemporary reader would.

The Eastern Orthodox tradition kept the *word* — *theosis* / *theopoiesis* (literally "god-making") — but starting in the third and fourth centuries, the church philosophically erected an "iron ceiling": an ontological wall between God and creation that the deified can never cross. So when modern Orthodoxy says "we believe in theosis," they mean "you become an immortal spirit being, but you remain as ontologically distant from God as a blade of grass." That is *not* what Paul meant. Paul has no iron ceiling. In Paul, you become a god. Full stop.

Litwa: "When the Orthodox people talk about deification, they don't really mean becoming God. They mean you become a spirit being, immortal, powerful — but you are as far away from God ontologically as a piece of grass. There is a linguistic overlap, but in terms of the philosophical meaning, they are miles and miles away from Paul."

### The Apocalyptic Frame Still Holds (Tabor's Anchor)

Even as Litwa pushes the Hellenistic vocabulary, Tabor anchors Paul's *framework* in Jewish apocalyptic. The two-ages structure, the cosmic war against Belial/Satan, the imminent end of the *schema* of this world, the Daniel-9-and-11 apocalyptic clock — these come from the Hebrew Bible / Dead Sea Scrolls / 1 Enoch stream, not from Plato or the mystery cults.

Aramaic Enoch fragments at Qumran (dated by Michael Stone to the third c. BCE) put apocalyptic Judaism *centuries* before Paul. By Paul's day, 1 Enoch was scripture for late first / second century Christians (Jude quotes Enoch as scripture). Enoch's ascent forms the model for Jesus's ascent and Paul's own ascent. The Hekhalot literature is later (rabbinic, third-to-sixth century), but it comes from the same stream.

So the structural claim: Paul is using Hellenistic loanwords *inside* a Hebrew Bible apocalyptic grammar. The cosmic-ascent, multi-layered-heavens, deification vocabulary is real and Hellenistic. The two-ages, cosmic war, *imminent* historical crisis, dust-creature-becoming-glorified frame is Hebrew apocalyptic. Both are true at once — and that's what makes Paul *Paul*.

### Was Paul Asexual? Was Paul Gay? (Brief)

Lambert presses for opinions. Both decline to make it definitive (would require a time machine). Litwa's lean: probably *asexual*, not gay. Like later 2nd-c. Christians such as Julius Cassianus and some encratite groups who weren't attracted in either direction. Tabor's lean: Paul was struggling with sexual temptation toward women — the *thorn in the flesh* (2 Cor 12:7) and the "good that I would I do not" struggle of Romans 7 sound to Tabor like sexual continence under pressure, not orientation. Both note the obvious caveat: speculation, evidence-thin, and the loud-against-it-because-fighting-it pattern is a real psychological thing.

## Misconceptions Addressed

| Misconception | Tabor and Litwa's Response |
|---------------|-----------------------------|
| "Paul is about how guilty sinners get their sins forgiven (judicial salvation)" | That's the Augustinian-Lutheran reading Stendahl and Sanders dismantled. Paul's real concern is escaping a mortal dying body in a dying universe ruled by hostile cosmic powers. Sins-forgiven is one piece, not the engine. |
| "Paul was Jewish, therefore not Hellenistic" | Litwa: Judaism *is* a Hellenistic religion. The "judeo-Christian buffer zone" is a Christian apologetic invention designed to protect Paul (and Jesus) from the Greco-Roman world. There is no such buffer. |
| "The multiple heavens are metaphors / dimensions" | They were real physical cosmic spaces. Ancients argued about how many floors the building had (3, 7, 10, 360, 365) but agreed the building was there. |
| "Spirits are non-physical / immaterial beings" | In the ancient world, spirits were *material* — composed of refined air, ether, or pneuma. Diaphanous, breathable smoke, but bodily. Paul's *soma pneumatikon* is a kind of body, not the absence of one. |
| "Religion is sincere devotion; magic is manipulation; Paul did religion, not magic" | A Christian apologetic fiction. Paul curses, projects his spirit, delivers people to Satan, performs ritual exclusion with effect. Whether you call it religion or magic depends on whose side you're on. |
| "Galatians 3:19 (Torah given by angels) is just rabbinical flourish" | Possibly more pointed. Barnabas 9:4 reads Paul as saying an evil angel tricked the Jews. Galatians 4 confirms: the elemental spirits of the cosmos held both Jews and Gentiles in bondage via calendar religion. |
| "Jesus's resurrection and ascension are two separate events 40 days apart" | Acts's invention. Late-first-century Christians (including Paul) treated resurrection-ascension-deification as one event. You rise into the upper ecology and acquire a body that fits. |
| "Philippians 2 proves Jesus pre-existed as a divine being" | Tabor, Litwa, Dunn, Talbert: this is Adam Christology. Two Adams contrasted. Jesus in the form of God (so was Adam); Jesus did not grasp at equality (Adam did); therefore Jesus was exalted. It's a model for *everyone*, not a unique exception. |
| "Deification means becoming sort-of-like-God in a watered-down sense" | In Paul's day it meant becoming a *theos* — an immortal being with superhuman power, same category as the Roman emperor, Heracles, Asclepius. The Orthodox tradition kept the word but, by the 4th c., erected an "iron ceiling" of ontology Paul never had. |
| "Christians becoming gods is heresy" | It's the explicit teaching of the Naassene Preacher (late 2nd c.) reading Paul; of the early patristic *theopoiesis* tradition before the ontological wall went up; and of Paul himself when read in his Greco-Roman context without the Augustinian filter. |
| "Paul's apocalyptic urgency is just leftover Jewish framing — strip it out and you have his real message" | Strip the apocalyptic and you have nothing Paul would recognize. The *schema* of this world is passing away, the appointed time has grown very short — every ethical instruction is calibrated to imminent cosmic transformation. |
| "The Torah came directly from God, full stop" | Paul's actual language (Galatians 3:19): given through angels by an intermediary. Combined with the *stoicheia* in Galatians 4, Paul thinks the ritual law was part of a holding pattern under lower powers — now obsolete with the arrival of the seed. |
