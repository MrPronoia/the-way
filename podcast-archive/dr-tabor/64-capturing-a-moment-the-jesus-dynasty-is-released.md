## Summary

This is the KGNU Boulder/Denver interview Tabor did during the April 2006 release week of *The Jesus Dynasty: The Hidden History of Jesus, His Royal Family, and the Birth of Christianity*. Tabor is calling in from Jerusalem — he is doing follow-up work on the James Ossuary case and on the two candidate tombs his book identifies. Liz Lane, host of KGNU's *Metro* show, comes in calmer and more curious than the network-TV interviewers of the same week, and Tabor uses the hour to lay out the actual argument of the book to a public-radio audience that includes lapsed Catholics, secular listeners, and Christians of various stripes. The first third focuses on the family of Jesus: Mary as a Jewish mother of seven children rather than the non-sexual Queen of Heaven; the four brothers (James, Joses, Judas, Simon) and two sisters (Mary and Salome) who are named in the New Testament; and the centuries-long theological process by which Mary was elevated to Perpetual Virgin and finally bodily assumed into heaven (the Assumption dogma formalized as late as the 1870s). Tabor frames the dynasty argument carefully: this is not a *Da Vinci Code*-style claim that Jesus married and fathered children; this is the historically documentable claim that Jesus was the firstborn of a large family whose surviving brother James succeeded him as leader of the movement.

The middle section gets into the archaeology — and this is where this interview is distinct from the other 2006 release-week appearances. Tabor walks Lane through the James Ossuary (the limestone bone box inscribed "James son of Joseph brother of Jesus" that surfaced on the Antiquities Market in 2002), the Israel Antiquities Authority forgery accusation, the recent (as of 2006) Krumbein study confirming the "brother of Jesus" phrase is authentic patina, and his own denied request to do DNA testing on the bone fragments inside. He then explains the two candidate family tombs: the **Tomb of the Shroud** (which he, Shimon Gibson, and UNCC students discovered by chance in 2000 while hiking in the Hinnom Valley, containing the only authenticated first-century Jerusalem burial shroud) and the **Talpiot Tomb** (excavated 1980, containing ossuaries inscribed Jesus son of Joseph, Mariamne, Maria, Joseph, with one ossuary missing whose dimensions match the James Ossuary). Tabor refuses to be sensational about either possibility — his stated purpose is to give readers an emotional sense of connection to the historical family, not to claim he has found the bones of Jesus. He also addresses the geography of the crucifixion (he argues for the Mount of Olives, not the traditional Church of the Holy Sepulcher site) and the two-stage burial scenario (Sabbath-eve temporary tomb, then later family tomb).

The final third pivots to message and movement. Tabor's dedication of the book to Albert Schweitzer — released on the centennial of Schweitzer's 1906 *Quest of the Historical Jesus* — frames Jesus as the man who "threw himself on the wheel of history." Lane and Tabor walk through the two-Messiah configuration (priestly John, kingly Jesus); the historical relationship between John the Baptizer and Jesus (cousins through their mothers, with John as Jesus's teacher); the political threat the pair posed to Herod Antipas and the Roman occupation; the message of the kingdom of God as *tikkun olam* — fixing the world here, not escape to heaven; and the three irreversible things Paul did that no Jew could accept: declaring a human to be God, removing the Torah, and voiding the Covenant with the Jewish people. Tabor closes by suggesting that the Christianity people are running toward in the *Da Vinci Code* phenomenon is the same Christianity his book offers — a more human Jesus, a real family, a movement grounded in justice and righteousness in this world. Secular readers can come away with a new admiration. Christian readers can come away with a renewed faith in a different and more meaningful form. The closing image is the Sophie character in the *Da Vinci Code* film putting her foot in the water with a smile — "not yet" — but pointing toward the recovery of a Jesus who was *in this world*.

## Key Teachings

### Jesus Was the Firstborn of a Large Jewish Family — Seven Children Total

The structural claim of *The Jesus Dynasty*. Jesus was the eldest of seven children: four brothers (James, Joseph/Joses, Jude, and Simon — all named in the Gospels) and two sisters (Mary and Salome — mentioned but not named as sisters in the surviving New Testament text). Tabor draws this directly from the Gospel texts themselves; the work is reading the Bible carefully against the centuries of overlay that obscured it.

The reason this family has been forgotten is the slow elevation of Mary into a non-sexual spiritual figure. Tabor traces the progression: (1) Virgin conception → (2) Perpetual Virginity (Mary never had marital relations) → (3) Joseph reframed as a "spiritual" husband with no physical contact → (4) the Assumption of Mary, in which her body is taken bodily into heaven (formalized as Catholic dogma only in the 1870s). The Greek philosophical assumption underneath this progression is that holiness equals asexuality and finally bodily removal from the world. Tabor's project rejects that assumption and recovers Mary as a real first-century Jewish woman raising seven children.

### Protestant and Catholic Strategies for Erasing the Brothers

The "brothers and sisters of Jesus" problem has two confessional solutions, both of which Tabor rejects:

- **Protestant biblical literalists** generally acknowledge the brothers as physical siblings born to Mary after Jesus — accepting the Virgin Birth of Jesus but allowing Mary's other children to be born of normal sexual union with Joseph
- **Roman Catholics** preserve Mary's perpetual virginity by reinterpreting the brothers as cousins (Jerome's solution) or as children of Joseph from a prior marriage (the Eastern Orthodox solution)
- **Tabor's reading:** The Gospel texts present these as Jesus's physical siblings in a matter-of-fact way ("Jesus and his mother and his brothers moved here, did this, did that"); the simplest historical reading is the right one

A graduate of 12 years of Catholic education and Jesuit university (Lane) confirms during the interview that she never heard a hint of Jesus having siblings. The information has been there in the New Testament the whole time; the confessional traditions have simply trained readers not to see it.

### James the Brother as Designated Successor — Not the Apostle James

This is the dynasty argument proper. After Jesus's death, leadership of the movement passed to **James the brother of Jesus** — not to Peter, and definitely not (initially) to Paul. Tabor stresses this is not the James of the fisherman trio (Peter, Andrew, James and John); this is another James, the actual blood brother.

Three things make this a **dynasty** rather than just a brother taking over a fallen sibling's project:

1. Jesus was of the royal lineage of King David
2. The Romans and Herod's puppet regime knew this — there is documented evidence that later Roman emperors (Hadrian among them) arrested grandsons of Jude (Jesus's brother) in the second century, looking for Davidic descendants who might claim the throne
3. James's succession is therefore not just fraternal solidarity but actual bloodline continuity of the Davidic claim

"For James to succeed Jesus, it's not just a brother but it's actually the bloodline in succession."

### The James Ossuary — A Limestone Bone Box Inscribed "James Son of Joseph Brother of Jesus"

The archaeological piece. In 2002 a limestone ossuary surfaced on the Jerusalem Antiquities Market inscribed in Aramaic: **"James son of Joseph brother of Jesus."** Tabor explains the burial practice that produced ossuaries — body laid out in a rock-cut tomb for a year covered in a shroud, then the bones gathered and placed in a limestone chest sized for a femur bone, with the name of the deceased inscribed on the side.

The combination of three common names (James, Joseph, Jesus) plus the **brother** relationship (rare on ossuary inscriptions, since the father is normally enough identification) makes the statistical case that this is the bone box of Jesus's brother James. Tabor notes that scholars who have run the statistical odds are largely convinced.

The forgery controversy: six months after the ossuary went public — front page of the *New York Times*, AP wires everywhere — the Israel Antiquities Authority declared part of the inscription a forgery, claiming "brother of Jesus" had been added by a modern forger. **But more recent work by Professor Krumbein in Germany** (referenced as appearing in the *Jerusalem Post* that very week of the interview) has concluded that the "brother of Jesus" phrase is authentic and in the original patina. The forgery accusation appears to be collapsing.

### The Antiquities Trade and How Tombs Are Robbed

A grim aside that turns out to be central. When ossuaries surface on the Antiquities Market, they are by definition from recently robbed tombs — opening a Jewish tomb is illegal in Israel and Orthodox Jewish communities oppose it vehemently. The robbery is done at night.

What the robbers want is the **inscribed face of the box**. Jews were not buried with jewelry, coins, or grave goods — only bones in named boxes. So robbers take a hammer, knock off the corner where the name is written, leave the rest of the heavy box behind, and carry away a bag of name-pieces. They then sell them illegally to tourists: "Here's the name Simon from the first century — would you like to buy it for your mantle?" This desecrates the tomb and destroys the archaeological identification work entirely.

### Two Candidate Family Tombs — The Tomb of the Shroud and Talpiot

Tabor identifies two tombs as the most likely candidates for being the Jesus family tomb. The James Ossuary may have come from one of them.

**The Tomb of the Shroud** — Discovered in 2000 by Tabor, archaeologist Shimon Gibson, and UNCC students hiking the Hinnom Valley near Akeldama. The tomb had been robbed shortly before, and it contained a corpse with the burial shroud still intact — the **only authenticated first-century Jerusalem burial shroud ever found**, since shrouds were normally discarded once the bones were gathered. Carbon-dated to the period. Other ossuaries in the tomb name a Mary and a Salome — names that could match either Jesus's mother and sister or his two sisters. Mitochondrial DNA from the Mary in the tomb could in principle establish whether she is a sibling or the mother of the James in the ossuary, since mitochondrial DNA is passed mother-to-child and shared by siblings.

**The Talpiot Tomb** — Excavated in 1980 in Jerusalem. Contains ossuaries inscribed **Jesus son of Joseph**, **Mariamne** (a name often associated with Mary Magdalene), **Maria**, **Joseph**, and others — essentially the Holy Family configuration. The tomb also has a **missing ossuary** whose dimensions match the James Ossuary. The press has not picked up that the dimensions match.

Tabor refuses to be sensational. His stated purpose is not to claim he has found the bones of Jesus but to give readers a sense of emotional reality and connection — "to say you've entered a Jewish tomb at all from the first century is an amazing thing, but to say that this might be the tomb of his family, it makes the family just come alive."

### DNA Testing Denied — The Forgery Case Excuse

Tabor has formally requested DNA testing of the bone fragments and body fluid residue still present inside the James Ossuary. The Israel Antiquities Authority has denied the request on the grounds that the ossuary is evidence in an ongoing forgery criminal case and therefore cannot be released for testing. Tabor expresses calm frustration: this is the kind of question that could be answered definitively one way or the other if testing were allowed. "I think we'll get to do it" — but it requires patience that one of his fellow archaeologists noted is the chief required virtue of the field.

### Jesus and John as Cousins, Co-Workers, and Two Messiahs

The two-Messiah configuration Tabor draws from the Dead Sea Scrolls and the prophet Zechariah. Mary the mother of Jesus is the *kinswoman* of Elizabeth the mother of John the Baptizer — when Mary (probably 14 years old) becomes pregnant, she leaves Galilee with haste and travels south to **Ein Karem in the hill country of Judea** (where Tabor is calling in from for the interview, doing fieldwork) and stays with Elizabeth for six months. The families grow up close.

John the Baptizer is the son of the priest Zechariah and is therefore of the **Aaronic priestly lineage**. Jesus is of the **Davidic royal lineage**. The term *Messiah* means "anointed one" and applies to **both** priests and kings — both were ritually anointed with oil. Zechariah the prophet describes a king on his throne with his anointed priest at his side — two Messiahs. The Dead Sea Scrolls explicitly speak of the coming of the Messiah of David and the Messiah of Aaron together. When John and Jesus appear on the scene together, they are recognized as the realization of this expectation.

The Gospel of John preserves the historical sequence the Synoptics obscure: John appears first, baptizes thousands while Jesus is also baptizing in parallel for a year in the south; then John is arrested in the north; then Jesus goes underground; then a year and a half later Jesus emerges in Galilee, calls the fishermen, and resumes the work alone. The movie/Sunday-school conflation that compresses this into a single Easter-week narrative misses the year-long political and religious drama.

### The Political Threat to Herod Antipas

Herod Antipas, ruler of Galilee, was hated as an illegitimate half-Jewish puppet of the Romans, cruel and immoral. The simultaneous emergence of two legitimately-lineaged figures — Jesus from the royal house of David and John from the priestly house of Aaron — preaching that "the kingdom is near, repent and believe the gospel" was an existential threat. The kingdom of God preaching was **not** a private spiritual message; it had immediate political implications about who would rule the land. This is why John was arrested first and beheaded, and why Jesus's eventual ride into Jerusalem on a donkey at Passover (with crowds shouting Davidic kingdom slogans) ended the way it did.

### The Kingdom of God as Tikkun Olam — Fixing This World, Not Escape from It

Tabor's pastoral and political core. The kingdom of God in the prayer Jesus taught — "your kingdom come, your will be done on earth as it is in heaven" — is not a Heavenly escape realm but the Jewish prophetic vision of **a transformed world right here**. This is the vision of Isaiah ("wolf lying down with the lamb"), of Hosea, Amos, Jeremiah, Isaiah — the apocalyptic prophets who arose after the Jewish disasters of the 7th, 6th, and 5th centuries BCE and who envisioned a peaceable kingdom of justice and righteousness on earth.

Tabor connects this to **tikkun olam** — the Jewish concept of fixing or repairing the world. The verse cut into the wall across from the United Nations flags in New York — "they shall beat their swords into plowshares and their spears into pruning hooks, neither shall they learn war anymore" — is Isaiah being claimed by the 20th-century institution that explicitly exists to repair the world. Jesus, John, and James preached this prophetic vision as their actual gospel. They did not preach Jesus as the message; they preached the message **with** Jesus.

When more liberal churches focus on the Social Gospel — feeding the poor, opposing injustice, running hospitals, fighting for righteousness — they are remembering the original kingdom-of-God preaching of Jesus, John, and James. When Tabor watches Al Gore's *An Inconvenient Truth* and thinks about the religious responsibility to be good stewards of the earth, he sees it as recovering the Hebrew vision that the original movement carried.

### The Three Things Paul Did That No Jew Could Accept

Tabor's clearest articulation of the structural break between Pauline Christianity and the original Jewish-Christian movement. Picture Judaism as a three-legged stool. **Paul cut off all three legs**:

1. **He declared a human (Jesus) to be God** — "Jews cannot do that"
2. **He removed the Torah** as binding — replaced by a "new way"
3. **He voided the Covenant with the Jewish people** as no longer valid

Any one of these would have been a fatal break. All three together created an entirely different religion. Jesus, John, and James as historical Jews would not have affirmed any of the three.

The mirror image, Tabor says, applied to a Christian: imagine someone saying Jesus is not the Messiah, Jesus is not the Son of God, Jesus did not die for your sins, when you die you're just dead, and now follow this new path. A Christian would say: "Wait — you've taken away our fundamental pillars." Jews feel exactly the same way about what Paul did.

### Why Christians and Jews Separated — Not What You Think

The popular explanation for the Jewish-Christian schism is that Christians blamed Jews for killing Jesus. Tabor argues the deeper and earlier cause was **Paul's rejection of the Torah and his abolition of the Covenant**. The blood-libel rhetoric came later. The structural break was theological and legal — a removal of the foundations of Jewish life — not a grievance over the crucifixion.

### Jesus Knew the Script and Acted It

The Triumphal Entry is not accidental. Jesus consciously stepped into the Davidic role and acted out the prophecies deliberately. Zechariah 9:9 says "Lo, your king comes, lowly, riding on a donkey." Jesus arranges to ride into Jerusalem on a donkey at Passover. The crowds shout "Blessed is the kingdom of our father David." Tabor calls this "prophetic pantomime" — signaling in symbols the first-century Jewish crowd understood and Pilate's guards understood as dangerous. "These are dangerous slogans to yell at Passover with Pilate across the city."

Tabor explicitly rejects Hugh Schonfield's *Passover Plot* reading in which Jesus staged his death and survived. Jesus was not staging his survival; he was fulfilling the script knowing it would likely end in his death.

### Albert Schweitzer's Wheel of History

The book is dedicated to Albert Schweitzer, whose *Quest of the Historical Jesus* (1906) appeared exactly 100 years before Tabor's book and was the first scholarly attempt to recover the historical Jewish Jesus from the theological overlay. Tabor calls Schweitzer "maybe the greatest figure of the 20th century" — philosopher, writer, historian, missionary, musician.

Schweitzer's image of Jesus that Tabor finds moving: Jesus threw himself on the wheel of history to try to turn things, and the wheel turned and crushed him — and yet we are still talking about him 2000 years later. The image captures something Tabor wants to recover: Jesus as the human figure of moral courage who walked deliberately into the political fire of his moment, not the divine being who came as a spiritual rescue mission.

The Vietnam-era priests Tabor remembers from his own coming of age are Schweitzer-style figures: people who believed that justice and righteousness require you to face the enemy, walk right up, put yourself on the block, and not withdraw. That is the spirit Tabor sees in Jesus's choice to ride into Jerusalem.

### Nietzsche's Hyperbole — "There Was Only One Christian"

A line Tabor finds profound. Nietzsche, the great atheist, said "there was only one Christian and he died on the cross." Tabor reads this as hyperbole — not literally claiming nobody else ever did this — but the point is that Jesus's later followers tended to remember him in a "comfortable" way (the guy who died so we can go to heaven) rather than taking up the actual call he took up. Saint Francis did. Dietrich Bonhoeffer did. Most professed Christians, then and now, do not. The Christian call as Nietzsche heard it is terrifying — and Tabor admits he himself finds it scary to think about what it really means.
