## Summary

Dr. James Tabor opens his Paul series by overturning the standard evangelical apologetic for the resurrection. The argument that "we have eyewitness testimony" turns out to be false in the most basic sense: of all the New Testament writings, **the only undisputed first-person account of seeing the risen Jesus comes from Paul**. The gospel accounts in Matthew, Luke, and John are all secondhand reports of what others allegedly saw — and Luke explicitly says he is not an eyewitness. Mark in its oldest copies has no resurrection appearances at all.

But there's a deeper irony. Paul, the only first-person witness, describes an experience that bears almost no resemblance to the physical encounters of the gospels. His "seeing" was a blinding-light vision; he himself admits in 2 Corinthians 12 that he cannot tell whether it was "in the body or out of the body." When asked what kind of resurrection body Jesus had, Paul gives the kicker: **"I can't tell you what kind of body."** Far from a resuscitated corpse with wounds you could touch, Paul describes a transformation — corruptible to incorruptible, mortal to a "life-giving spirit." Tabor argues this is why the church effectively forgot Paul's testimony: it was too abstract and visionary to use as proof. The gospels' physical-encounter stories worked better for apologetics, so they became the church's resurrection story instead.

The interview also walks through Tabor's cosmological reframe of 1 Corinthians 15. The chapter every Bible labels "the resurrection chapter," Tabor argues, should be retitled **"the transformation of the cosmos chapter."** Paul's three-stage framework (Christ first, then those who belong to Christ at the parousia, then the final reconciliation when "God will be all in all") describes a cosmic rebirth — Romans 8's cosmos in labor pains, birthing many sons of God. Tabor suggests Paul may even be a universalist: every last Adam of the cosmos eventually redeemed. Read this way, Paul's gospel isn't "confess Jesus and go to heaven" — it's "you were born for cosmic glorification, above the angels."

## Key Teachings

### Paul Is the Only First-Person Resurrection Witness in the New Testament

Tabor's central claim: among writings critical scholars agree come from the named author's own hand, **only Paul** gives a first-person account of seeing the risen Jesus. The gospel resurrection appearances (to Peter, James, the Twelve, the women, the Emmaus disciples) are all reported by third parties. Luke (1:1-4) explicitly says he is not an eyewitness. Mark in our oldest manuscripts ends at the empty tomb with no appearances. Even Mary Magdalene as "first witness" — we have nothing in her own hand.

The 1 Corinthians 15 creed lists appearances to Peter, James, the Twelve, 500 brethren, then Paul himself — but Paul lists himself last and "equates" his experience with theirs, suggesting all of them were the same kind of visionary "sighting."

### 1 Corinthians 15 Is the "Transformation of the Cosmos" Chapter

The chapter every Bible labels "the resurrection chapter" Tabor wants to rename. Its real subject is how God will transform the entire cosmos into the "new heavens and new earth" of Isaiah. The dead come up *incidentally* — Paul addresses them because his audience worried whether those who had already died would miss out on the imminent transformation. The chapter's actual frame is cosmic, not corpse-revival.

### Resurrection ≠ Resuscitation

Tabor distinguishes two categories the church has merged:

- **Resuscitation** — a corpse comes back in the same body, still mortal. Lazarus. The widow's son at Nain. People still die again afterward. This is what the gospel encounter stories often look like.
- **Resurrection** (Pauline) — corruptible body transformed into incorruptible "glorious body." Mortal to immortal. Powered by the same creative force that "enables Jesus Christ to rule the whole cosmos." Not the same person with new wrappers — a metamorphosis (Paul uses the literal Greek word, the same root behind caterpillar-to-butterfly).

The church, Tabor argues, smuggled the resuscitation imagery of the later gospels back into Paul's language and lost Paul's actual meaning.

### "I Can't Tell You What Kind of Body"

The kicker passage: Paul explicitly says he cannot describe the resurrection body. If he were thinking resuscitation, he would have said "of course you know — it'll be the guy you knew, maybe a perfect 30-year-old version." Instead he reaches for the seed analogy: plant an acorn, an oak tree comes up. There's continuity, but you cannot describe the plant from the seed. Then he switches to sexual/birth imagery: Romans 8's cosmos in labor pains, the universe as a "birth machine" for the new creation of humanity.

### Two Adams (1 Cor 15)

Paul's anthropology in 1 Cor 15:
- **First Adam** — of the dust, mortal (quoting Genesis 2 / Genesis 3:19, "to dust you shall return")
- **Second Adam** — a "life-giving spirit"

People resist this because they want physical continuity between the two ("don't you need the body to make continuity with the spiritual?"). But that question, Tabor says, comes from reading the gospels back into Paul. Stay with Paul on his own terms.

### Paul's Three-Stage Cosmic Resurrection (1 Cor 15)

The framework Paul lays out — found only here in the New Testament:

1. **Stage 1:** Christ alone. He is the firstfruits, already raised, seated at God's right hand in glorified spiritual body.
2. **Stage 2:** Those who belong to Christ at his parousia (the "appearance" — Tabor notes this was the Greek term for an emperor's official visit to a city). Living and dead are transformed "in the twinkling of an eye, at the last trumpet."
3. **Stage 3:** Interim period in which Christ conquers all rule, authority, and power — angelic and demonic. The last enemy destroyed is death itself. Then "God will be all in all."

### Paul May Be a Universalist

Tabor reads stage 3 as potentially universalist. "All in all" sounds like every Adam of the cosmos, subject to decay, eventually redeemed. The alternative is an annihilationist reading (the unredeemed simply cease to exist) — Tabor notes Ehrman's *Heaven and Hell* explores how Sheol in the Hebrew Bible was originally nothingness, not torment.

### Paul's Vision Was Light, Not Flesh

Tabor doesn't take Acts 9's account as exact — that's Luke writing decades later. But Acts is sympathetic to Paul and the consistent through-line is a **blinding light experience**. Paul himself in 2 Corinthians 12 describes ascending to paradise, seeing things "unutterable" and "not lawful to reveal." He cannot say whether it was "in the body or out of the body." This is not someone who shared a meal with a resurrected friend.

### Why the Church Forgot Paul's Testimony

Paul's account was abstract, visionary, and theological — useless for the apologetic task of proving to skeptics that the resurrection was real. The gospels' encounter stories (Thomas touching the wounds, breakfast on the beach, the road to Emmaus) gave the church usable proof. So the church *kept Paul as scripture but forgot Paul as witness*, building its resurrection apologetic on the very accounts that no first-person witness wrote.

### Paul's Gospel Is Cosmic Glorification, Not "Go to Heaven"

What was Paul preaching? Not "confess Jesus and go to heaven when you die." His gospel: **you were born to become a glorified son of God, above the angels, ruling the cosmos with Christ**. "Don't you know the saints are going to judge the world? They're going to judge angels." Tabor notes this sounds "Mormon" — and observes that Mormonism actually picked up some of this directly from Paul.

## Misconceptions Addressed

| Misconception | Tabor's Response |
|---------------|------------------|
| "We have eyewitness testimony to the resurrection" | Of writings critical scholars accept as from the named author's own hand, **only Paul** is first-person. The gospels are all secondhand reports — and Luke says so explicitly. |
| "Paul's resurrection experience matches the gospel appearances" | Paul describes a blinding light and admits he doesn't know if it was "in or out of the body." That is not the same category as Thomas touching wounds or Jesus eating fish. |
| "1 Corinthians 15 is the proof-text for bodily resurrection" | It's actually the chapter where Paul says he **cannot describe** the resurrection body — and where he calls the second Adam "a life-giving spirit," not a flesh-and-blood body. |
| "Paul's gospel was 'believe and go to heaven'" | Paul's actual gospel was cosmic transformation — humanity ascending to a glorified state above the angels, ruling the cosmos with Christ. |
| "The dead get a different kind of body than the living" | No — Paul puts living and dead through the *same* metamorphosis at the parousia. Stage 2 sweeps up both. |
