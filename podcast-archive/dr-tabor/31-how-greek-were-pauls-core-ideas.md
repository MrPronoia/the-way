## Summary

This is Tabor's clearest single statement on a question that has haunted Pauline studies since Adolf von Harnack: **was Paul thinking like a Greek or like a Jew?** The traditional framing — "Athens or Jerusalem" — Tabor calls a false dichotomy. Following his teacher Jonathan Z. Smith, Tabor argues that by Paul's time the categories had largely **merged**. Hellenistic ideas had penetrated Judaism (especially mystical Judaism, apocalyptic Judaism, and the Dead Sea Scrolls community), and Hellenistic religion itself had absorbed Near Eastern and Jewish elements. What emerges in Paul is a **Hebraicized version** of cosmic salvation language that looks Greek on the surface but operates from a fundamentally Hebrew Bible framework.

The lecture (delivered as the overview portion of a Zoom meeting for Tabor's online Paul course) walks systematically through the seven undisputed letters and pulls out every cosmological reference: Satan, demons, the elemental spirits, the "god of this age," the multiple heavens, the ascent to paradise, Adam, Christ as the new Adam, the schema of this world passing away, the present evil age, principalities and powers, the things above and below. Tabor reads these against Hugh Schonfield's summary of "phariseeism" with its esoteric / merkabah mystical layer, and against the broad Hellenistic salvation-religion question: *how do we escape the lower world of fate, sin, suffering, and death?*

Paul's answer turns out to be radically un-Greek at the decisive point. Where Plato and Hellenistic mystery religions teach that the soul originally belonged in the heavenly realm and **fell** into matter and needs to **escape back up**, Paul teaches that humanity was **made from dust on purpose** as a corruptible prototype, and is destined to be **transformed forward** into a glorified spirit body. Death is not the friend (Plato); death is the enemy (Paul, 1 Cor 15:26). The world isn't a prison the soul escapes from; it's a womb in labor (Romans 8) birthing the new creation. So Paul uses Hellenistic vocabulary — multiple heavens, ascent, deliverance from the elemental spirits, cosmic powers — but he is reading Genesis 1-3 the whole time. He is closer in worldview to the Dead Sea Scrolls and apocalyptic Daniel than to Plato or Philo. The Hellenistic vocabulary is the loanword; the Hebrew Bible apocalyptic frame is the grammar.

## Key Teachings

### "Athens or Jerusalem" Is a False Dichotomy

The standard scholarly framing — that there are two hermetically sealed worlds, Greek Hellenistic Platonism on one side and Jewish Hebrew thought on the other — Tabor calls misleading. By the first century these worlds had **merged**. Jonathan Z. Smith's lifework demonstrated that what we call "Hellenistic religion" is itself a Mediterranean synthesis that drew on Egyptian, Persian, Mesopotamian, Greek, and Jewish materials. By Paul's time, **any religion worth its salt was primarily focused on cosmic salvation** — getting out of this lower world of fate, sin, suffering, and death. That question was the air everyone breathed. The differences are in how each tradition answers it.

So the better question isn't "was Paul Greek or Jewish?" but "**what does Paul's specific answer to the cosmic-salvation question look like, and which framework dominates when they conflict?**"

### Background Is Foreground

Tabor pushes back on the word "background" itself. Scholars often talk about Paul's "Jewish background" or "Hellenistic background" as if these are decorative context — wallpaper behind a portrait. But for Paul, this *was* the foreground. He lived inside this worldview. The multiple heavens, the angelic mediators, the elemental spirits, the present evil age — these weren't background ideas Paul occasionally referenced. They were the air he breathed and the world he believed he lived in.

This matters because modern readers tend to strip Paul's letters of the cosmology and read them as moral/spiritual instructions for individual souls. Tabor wants us to put the cosmology back in the foreground.

### Schonfield's Pharisaic Mysticism

Tabor leans heavily on Hugh Schonfield's article describing the esoteric layer of Pharisaic Judaism that Paul almost certainly inherited. The package included:

- Belief in angels and demons
- Mapped heavenly abodes (multiple heavens, paradise, gehenna)
- Magic and astrology
- **The archetypal man** (Adam Kadmon) — a pre-existent Adam figure later identified with the Messiah, conceptually similar to Plato's logos as the self-expression of God
- Special creation, pre-existence above and below
- The Messiah as an eternal figure
- Transmigration of souls (Tabor notes: **this one Paul does NOT pick up**)
- Revelation, divine intervention, predestination
- The day of judgment, resurrection of the body, life everlasting
- An **esoteric layer** for the specially trained: merkabah mysticism — contemplating the throne of God, the ascent through the heavens, "going behind the curtain"

Paul calls himself a Pharisee twice in the undisputed letters (Galatians 1:14, Philippians 3:5). He says he "advanced in Judaism beyond many of my own age" — suggesting top-of-class training. If Schonfield is right about what that training contained, this gives us a thick Jewish framework that already includes most of what looks "Hellenistic" in Paul's letters.

### Paul's Cosmos: A Tour Through the Seven Letters

Tabor walks systematically through the seven undisputed letters and pulls out every cosmological reference. The cumulative picture:

- **The present evil age** (Galatians 1:4) — Jesus delivers us out of (exhairo) it. Note: **rescued from, not lifted up out of.** Horizontal, not vertical.
- **The Torah given through angels** by an intermediary (Galatians 3:19)
- **Elemental spirits / stoicheia tou kosmou** (Galatians 4:3, 9) — the basic spiritual forces of the cosmos that hold humans in slavery. Both Jews and Gentiles were under these.
- **God's hidden wisdom decreed before the ages** (1 Cor 2:7) — the secret plan that dust creatures will be glorified
- **The Saints will judge angels** (1 Cor 6:3)
- **Many so-called gods and lords in heaven and earth** (1 Cor 8:5-6) — there are real spiritual powers; pagan sacrifices are made to demons (1 Cor 10:20)
- **The schema of this world is passing away** (1 Cor 7:31) — the apocalyptic clock is ticking
- **The Jerusalem above** (Galatians 4:26) — there is a heavenly counterpart to the earthly Jerusalem
- **Satan** appears repeatedly — tempting, harassing, hindering, disguising himself as an angel of light
- **The god of this world** has blinded the minds of unbelievers (2 Cor 4:4)
- **Visions and revelations**, ascent to the third heaven / paradise, hearing "things unutterable" (2 Cor 12:1-4)
- **Death, life, angels, principalities, powers, things present, things to come, height, depth** — Paul's expanded cosmos catalogue (Romans 8:38-39)
- **Creation in labor pains**, awaiting the revealing of the sons of God (Romans 8:18-25)
- **God of peace will soon crush Satan under your feet** (Romans 16:20) — the cosmic war ends with Satan defeated
- **Christ subdues every rule, authority, and power, and the last enemy is death itself** (1 Cor 15:24-26)

This is a thoroughly cosmologically populated worldview. Paul is not a deist or a moralist. He believes spiritual powers actively run the present age and that those powers are being conquered.

### Where Paul Diverges From Greek Salvation Religion

This is the heart of the lecture. Paul uses Hellenistic-sounding vocabulary (multiple heavens, ascent, deliverance, transformation, glorification) **but at the decisive point he reverses the direction**.

**Greek / Gnostic / Platonic schema:**
- Souls originate in heaven, in the divine realm.
- They **fell** into matter — into the lower, evil, material world.
- They are diamonds stuck in mud.
- Salvation = **escape back upward** to the spiritual realm where they originally belonged.
- The material body is the prison.
- Death is the friend — it frees the soul.

**Paul's schema:**
- Humans were created **from dust on purpose** by God's deliberate design (Genesis 2).
- The first Adam was a **living being (nephesh)** of dust — physical first, by intent.
- The body is not a prison; it is a prototype.
- Salvation = **forward transformation** into a glorified spirit body, becoming a new genus of cosmic being.
- The material cosmos itself is in labor, birthing the new creation.
- Death is the enemy — the last enemy destroyed.

Tabor's clean summary: **"In Paul, death is not the friend. Death is the enemy. Always remember that. Death has to be defeated. It's a very different view."**

This is why Tabor insists Paul's framework is fundamentally **Hebrew Bible apocalyptic**, not Hellenistic mystery religion, even though Paul borrows Hellenistic vocabulary throughout.

### Adam Christology, Not Pre-Existence Christology

In the seven undisputed letters, Tabor argues Paul does **not** teach that Jesus pre-existed as a divine being. The two key passages people cite:

**1 Corinthians 8:6** — "One God the Father from whom are all things... and one Lord Jesus Christ through whom are all things." Often read as pre-existence (Jesus as agent of original creation). Tabor reads it as Jesus as the agent of the **new creation** — the firstborn of many, the engineering prototype who will bring about the transformation of the cosmos (cross-reference 1 Corinthians 15).

**Philippians 2:5-11** — the "in the form of God / did not grasp at equality" hymn. Tabor (following James Dunn and Charles Talbert) reads this as **Adam Christology**: Jesus, like Adam, was made in the image (form/morphe) of God. Unlike Adam, who grasped at equality with God by eating the forbidden fruit, Jesus did not grasp. Therefore God exalted him. The passage is **two Adams contrasted**, not a pre-existent divine being descending and re-ascending.

Romans 1:3-4 confirms: Jesus was "descended from David according to the flesh and **designated** Son of God in power by a thing called resurrection from the dead." He becomes Son of God in power **at** the resurrection — he wasn't eternally one.

If you stay strictly in the seven letters, Tabor argues, you don't get pre-existence christology. You get a Davidic Jewish human transformed into the firstborn of a new genus. Ephesians and Colossians (which Tabor treats as deutero-Pauline) introduce a different cosmology — but those aren't in Paul's authentic voice.

### The Apocalyptic Clock Ticking Through Everything

Paul is not just doing cosmology in the abstract. He believes **the appointed time has grown very short** (1 Cor 7:29) and **the schema of this world is passing away** (1 Cor 7:31). The structures holding the world up are crumbling. This urgency drives every social instruction. Don't get married unless you have to. Slaves stay where you are. The world is on its way out — the new creation is breaking through.

This apocalyptic urgency is **Jewish** through and through. It comes from Daniel 9, Daniel 11, the Dead Sea Scrolls, the prophetic apocalyptic tradition. Plato has nothing remotely like it. The Hellenistic mystery religions don't have it either. The expectation of a cosmic crisis in real historical time is Hebrew Bible apocalyptic.

### Why the Dead Sea Scrolls Are Paul's Best Parallel

Tabor recommends: if you want to understand Paul's cosmology, read the Dead Sea Scrolls. Not Plato, not Philo, not the Corpus Hermeticum. The Qumran community has:

- The two ages (present evil age / age to come)
- The cosmic war between sons of light and sons of darkness
- A figure like Belial / Satan ruling the present age
- Angelic mediators and hierarchies
- Apocalyptic expectation of imminent intervention
- Esoteric mystical materials including hekhalot / merkabah-adjacent literature
- A community that sees itself as the beachhead of the new creation

This is the world Paul lives in. The Hellenistic vocabulary he uses is real but secondary — it's the loanwords, not the language itself.

## Misconceptions Addressed

| Misconception | Tabor's Response |
|---------------|------------------|
| "Paul was thoroughly Greek/Hellenized — basically a Stoic or Platonist" | Paul uses Hellenistic vocabulary but his **framework** is Hebrew Bible apocalyptic. He reverses the Greek schema at the decisive point: dust creation is *good*, the body is *not* the prison, death is the *enemy*, transformation is *forward* not escape *upward*. |
| "Paul was thoroughly Jewish — Hellenism is irrelevant" | Equally false. The "Athens vs. Jerusalem" framing is a false dichotomy. By the first century the categories had merged. Mystical Judaism, apocalyptic Judaism, and the broader Mediterranean world all share the same cosmological vocabulary. Paul lives inside that synthesis. |
| "1 Corinthians 8:6 proves Paul believed Jesus pre-existed" | Tabor reads this passage as Jesus as the agent of the **new creation** — the firstborn of many — not the agent of original Genesis 1 creation. The pre-existence reading only fits cleanly if you import Ephesians and Colossians, which Tabor treats as deutero-Pauline. |
| "Philippians 2 proves pre-existence" | Tabor (with James Dunn, Charles Talbert) reads Philippians 2 as **Adam Christology**: Jesus in the form/image of God (like Adam), did not grasp at equality (unlike Adam), therefore was exalted. Two Adams contrasted, not a divine being descending. |
| "Paul's cosmology is essentially Gnostic — escape the material world" | Paul's framework is the **opposite** of Gnostic. In Gnosticism, souls originally belonged in heaven, fell into matter, and need to escape. In Paul, humans were made from dust on purpose, and the cosmos itself will be transformed forward into the new creation. 1 Corinthians 15:46 is the anti-gnostic verse: "Not the spiritual first, but the physical, then the spiritual." |
| "2 Corinthians 4:18 (seen vs. unseen) is Paul going Platonic" | Surface looks Platonic, but Paul means the **future** new creation, not Plato's eternal forms above the cave. The "unseen eternal" for Paul is the age to come — temporal/horizontal, not metaphysical/vertical. |
| "Paul taught reincarnation / transmigration" | Tabor: this is in the Pharisaic mystical inheritance Schonfield maps, but **Paul does not pick it up**. No transmigration in the seven letters. Paul teaches a single life, then resurrection-transformation. |
| "Paul wasn't really apocalyptic — that's just leftover Jewish framing" | Apocalyptic is **the** clock Paul lives by. Every social ethic in the letters (don't marry, slaves stay, women stay subordinate, rich live as if without goods) is calibrated to the imminent end. Strip the apocalyptic and you have nothing Paul recognizes as his gospel. |
