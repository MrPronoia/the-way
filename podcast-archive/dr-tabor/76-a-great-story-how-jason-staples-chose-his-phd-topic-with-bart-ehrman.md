## Summary

A short, delightful anecdote pulled from Tabor's longer interview with Jason Staples (see #27). Staples recounts the moment in late 2007 or early 2008 when, as a first-year UNC grad student TA-ing for Bart Ehrman, he casually told his advisor — on a five-minute walk to lecture — that he already knew his dissertation thesis. He laid out his radical Romans 9-11 / scattered-Israel argument in about two minutes. Ehrman's response: "How many classes on Paul have you taken?" Staples: "None." Ehrman, half-amused: "Yeah, this kind of sounds like the work of an autodidact. Why don't you go over to Duke and take a few classes with Joel Marcus, Douglas Campbell, and Richard Hayes — and run this by them." The implied prediction: once the Duke Paul scholars got their hands on the idea, they would explain to Staples why it doesn't work and he'd have to write something else. The rest of the story — covered in #27 — is that Staples instead presented the argument at SBL 2008, drew a standing-room crowd, and was told by Robert Jewett, "I've been working on this question for 30 years and I think you've solved it."

This clip is essentially a teaser Tabor released to promote the upcoming Bart Ehrman virtual conference (Sept 21-22, 2024) where Staples is one of ten featured speakers.

## Key Teachings

### The Autodidact Pitch That Worked

The anecdote captures something rare in academic biblical studies — a graduate student walking in with an unsanctioned thesis that turns out to be right. Staples had no formal coursework on Paul. He had read the texts himself, noticed the two-kingdom problem (Israel ≠ Jews), and arrived at a framework for Romans 9-11 that no one in the room at UNC or Duke had on the table. Ehrman's "autodidact" line was meant as a gentle warning. It became, in retrospect, an accurate description of how the argument actually got made — outside the standard Pauline-scholarship guild discourse, by someone willing to follow the data without the inherited assumptions.

### The Duke Gauntlet — Predicted Failure, Actual Vindication

Ehrman's specific move was to send Staples to Duke to be talked out of his thesis by three of the heaviest hitters in American Pauline studies: Joel Marcus, Douglas Campbell, and Richard Hayes. The expectation was disconfirmation. The actual outcome (told in #27) was the opposite — Staples' SBL paper landed with Pauline scholar Robert Jewett, who had spent 30 years working on the same question and immediately said Staples had solved it. The dissertation became *The Idea of Israel in Second Temple Judaism* (Cambridge, 2021) and *Paul and the Resurrection of Israel* (Cambridge, 2024).

### Why Tabor Tells This Story

Tabor uses anecdotes like this to humanize the production of biblical scholarship. The takeaway isn't "Staples is brilliant" — it's that real breakthroughs in this field still happen the way they always have: somebody reads the primary texts carefully, notices something the consensus has been stepping over, and is stubborn enough to keep going when their advisor tells them it sounds like dilettante work.
