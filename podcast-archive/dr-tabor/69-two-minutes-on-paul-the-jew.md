## Summary

In this short documentary clip, Dr. James Tabor makes one quick, decisive point: **Paul and the first-century followers of Jesus were thoroughly Jewish — not "Christian" in any sense we would recognize today.** They kept a Sabbath, observed Jewish festivals (Passover, the Days of Unleavened Bread), prayed to the Hebrew God Yahweh, told stories of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, and would have been unrecognizable as participants in the Sunday-worshiping, Easter-and-Christmas-observing religion that emerged after 70 CE. Paul's so-called "Gentile" converts were being brought into what Tabor calls "the General Matrix of Jewish or Hebrew culture" — they were Judaized, not Christianized. Chronologically and culturally, Paul lived and died as a Jew. The pivot away from Jewish roots is a later development the early movement itself would not have endorsed.

---

## Key Teachings

### Paul Preached the Hebrew God to Gentiles — Not a New Religion

Tabor tells his students: "Look, he's preaching the Hebrew God Yahweh or Jehovah. He's telling the Gentiles about the Hebrew Bible." Instead of Zeus and Apollo and the Greco-Roman pantheon, Paul's audience was being given **Abraham, Isaac, Jacob** — Hebrew stories, Hebrew patriarchs, Hebrew theology. Paul's letters assume his Gentile readers already know these stories or should know them ("our father Abraham," "consider Isaac"). The framework is entirely Jewish; only the access point — through Messiah Jesus — was new.

### The First-Century Jesus Movement Kept Jewish Practice

Tabor's quick checklist of what Paul's communities actually did:

- **No Sunday worship** — they met on the **Seventh-Day Sabbath**
- **No Easter, no Christmas, no Christian liturgical calendar**
- **Jewish festivals observed** — including the Days of Unleavened Bread (the seven days following Passover, a phrase Paul and Luke use as if everyone knows it)
- **Jewish ethical and moral standards**
- **A Jewish messianic hope for the future**

The clip's frame: from the outside, these communities would have looked like a **Judaic form of life**, not a separate religion.

### "Christianity" Is What Happened After 70 CE

The YouTube description states Tabor's larger point explicitly: *"Christianity, as it unfolded after 70 CE, took an entirely different direction — actually turning away from its Jewish roots."* The destruction of the Jerusalem Temple and the Jewish-Roman war created the rupture; Paul himself did not live to see (or endorse) that pivot. **Paul, despite his innovations, lived and died as a Jew.** The Gentile-majority, Torah-rejecting, Sabbath-replacing religion that emerged afterward is a post-Pauline development — not the movement Paul founded.

## Misconceptions Addressed

| Misconception | Tabor's Response |
|---------------|------------------|
| "Paul founded Christianity as a Gentile religion separate from Judaism" | Paul preached the Hebrew God, the Hebrew Bible, and Jewish patriarchs to Gentiles. His communities kept the Sabbath and Jewish festivals. They were a Judaic form of life. |
| "Early Christians worshiped on Sunday and celebrated Easter/Christmas" | Paul's communities met on the seventh-day Sabbath and knew nothing of the Christian liturgical calendar. Those developments are post-70 CE. |
| "Paul abandoned Judaism after his Damascus road experience" | Chronologically, Paul lived and died as a Jew. He brought Gentiles into a Jewish framework — he didn't leave Judaism for something else. |
