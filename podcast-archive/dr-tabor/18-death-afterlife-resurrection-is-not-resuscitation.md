## Summary

This is the seventh installment in Tabor's Death & Afterlife series and the one where he names the central misconception head-on: **resurrection is not resuscitation.** It is not a corpse getting revived and walking out of a tomb. It is not a near-death experience scaled up. It is *metamorphosis* — the same word Paul uses for caterpillar-to-butterfly transformation — the cosmic remaking of a dust-creature into a glorified Spirit being. Tabor's whole hour is a walk through what the earliest Jesus-followers (especially Paul) actually believed, set against what modern Christians inherit through 1700 years of platonization. The line he keeps drawing: between *corpse revival* (Elijah, Elisha, Lazarus, Jairus's daughter, Eutychus — all of whom die again) and *resurrection of the dead* in the Pauline sense (Jesus as firstfruits of a brand-new species of being that has never existed before).

The argumentative spine is 1 Corinthians 15 — which Tabor insists should be renamed **the transformation chapter**, not the resurrection chapter. Paul's framework runs on two-Adam typology: the first Adam is a *nephesh chayyah*, a living breather made of dust, mortal by nature, identical anthropologically to every animal and insect. The second Adam (Jesus) becomes a *life-giving spirit* — not by reviving the same dust-body but by being raised into a categorically new mode of existence. "It is sown a physical body, it is raised a spiritual body." Sown in dishonor, raised in glory. Sown in weakness, raised in power. The wind-body / spirit-body is not flesh-and-blood at all. Tabor walks the *re-clothing* metaphor from 2 Corinthians 5 (Paul wants to be *clothed*, not *naked* — the opposite of Plato) and the *building-not-made-with-hands* metaphor (the tent vs. the permanent house). At the parousia, in the blink of an eye, the dead come forth *already in* the new imperishable body, and the living are transformed without dying. Death itself — the last enemy — is swallowed up.

The closer is Revelation 20-21: a cosmic event, not a private soul-flight. The sea, death, and Hades give up the dead. Old heaven and old earth are gone. New Jerusalem comes down. The river of life flows through the city; the tree of life bears twelve kinds of fruit; the leaves are for the healing of nations. Tabor flags the standard European-art picture of judgment (souls hauled into a sky-throne-room while pitchforked bodies tumble into eternal flame) as Christian-culture inheritance, *not* what the texts actually say. He recommends Edward Fudge's *The Fire That Consumes* on conditional immortality: the punishment is *death*, not eternal torture. He ends by previewing the next video, which will tackle the contradiction this one raises — if Pauline resurrection is transformation, why do the gospels seem to depict a resuscitated corpse walking out of an empty tomb? (That's the topic of Tabor #19 / playlist successor.) The framing throughout: "I am not preaching. I am expounding. Get the ideas straight before you judge them."

## Key Teachings

### Resurrection Is Metamorphosis — Not Corpse Revival

The load-bearing thesis of the whole video. Paul's Greek word is *metamorphosis* — the same root behind caterpillar-to-butterfly. The first Adam is a dust-creature. The second Adam (Jesus, post-resurrection) is a *life-giving spirit* — categorically new, the firstborn of a new race of beings that has never existed before. **It is not a corpse getting up and walking out.** The dust-body is gone. What comes forth from death is a new *mode of existence*.

Tabor draws the line explicitly: corpse revival is what happens to the widow's son raised by Elijah, the child raised by Elisha, Lazarus, Jairus's twelve-year-old daughter, the widow of Nain's son, Eutychus falling out the window in Acts 20. **All of those people died again.** That is resuscitation. Resurrection in the Pauline sense is the *transformation* into an imperishable spirit-body. Completely different category.

### 1 Corinthians 15 — The Transformation Chapter, Not the Resurrection Chapter

Tabor would rename the chapter. The resurrection question is *subsidiary* to the cosmic-transformation question. The Corinthians wrote Paul asking how the dead could possibly be raised — bodies decay, are cremated, return to dust. Paul's answer is not "God will reassemble the atoms." It is: **body is a mode of existence; God will give a new mode of existence appropriate to the new world.**

Paul's stages (1 Cor 15:23-24):
1. Christ the firstfruits (already transformed)
2. Those who belong to Christ at his coming (the parousia)
3. The *telos* — the completion — when Christ delivers the kingdom to the Father, having destroyed every enemy, the last being death itself

This is not "go to heaven when you die." It is a phased, *cosmic* event culminating in the defeat of death.

### Two Adams and Two Bodies — Dust Body vs. Spirit Body

The two-Adam typology is the load-bearing frame:

- **First Adam:** Genesis 2:7 — God breathes into the man and he becomes *nephesh chayyah*, a "living being" in KJV but literally a *living breather*. Tabor hammers this: **the exact same Hebrew phrase is used for cows, monkeys, insects, every crawling thing.** Humans are not anthropologically unique. We are dust creatures with breath.
- **Second Adam:** Jesus, *after* his resurrection. Not pre-existent in Tabor's reading. A real flesh-and-blood human "descended from David according to the flesh" who, in being raised, became a *life-giving spirit* — the prototype of a new species.

Paul then gives the structural logic: God doesn't create the spiritual first. He creates the *physical* first as a kind of perishable mock-up — a test, a stage — and the physical can then be transformed into the eternal. Humans don't already have immortality. They go back to the dust. The ones who are *in Christ* get re-clothed at the end of the age into the imperishable body.

> "Just as we have borne the image of the man of dust, we shall bear the image of the man of heaven."

### Sown a Physical Body, Raised a Spiritual Body — Paul's Contrast List

Paul's four-part contrast in 1 Cor 15:42-44 is the cleanest statement of the framework:

| Sown (the dust-body, buried) | Raised (the new spirit-body) |
|------------------------------|------------------------------|
| Perishable | Imperishable |
| In dishonor | In glory |
| In weakness | In power |
| A physical body (*soma psychikon*) | A spiritual body (*soma pneumatikon*) |

Tabor: *psychikon* is literally a "soul body" — but in Hebrew anthropology *soul* just means a breathing organism. So it's a "breather body" — the kind dust creatures have. *Pneumatikon* is a "wind body" — a *spirit* body. Same material as angels, except Paul thinks the glorified are *above* the angels. Not flesh and blood. Not physical in the sense we mean physical.

### The Two Apocrypha Texts — Sirach (Sadducee) vs. Wisdom of Solomon (Pharisee)

Tabor sets the first-century Jewish backdrop with two texts ~100-150 years before Jesus:

- **Sirach (Ben Sira, ~130 BCE):** The Sadducee position. "Death will not delay... in Hades one cannot look for luxury... all living beings become old like a garment, for the decree from of old is, 'you shall surely die.'" This is the older Hebrew Bible view — death is the end. The Sadducees see themselves as *biblical*: Moses doesn't teach resurrection, the prophets don't teach resurrection, Abraham doesn't teach resurrection. Tabor gives them their fair hearing.
- **Wisdom of Solomon (~first century BCE):** The Pharisee position. "The souls of the righteous are in the hand of God; no torment will ever touch them. In the eyes of the foolish [read: the Sadducees] they seemed to have died... but their hope is full of immortality. They will shine forth and run like sparks through the stubble."

Jesus, when challenged by the Sadducees with the seven-husbands trick question, sides definitively with the Pharisaic-resurrection camp.

### Resurrection Is Not Automatic — "Those Accounted Worthy"

A line most Christians have never noticed. In Luke 20:35, Jesus says: **"Those who are accounted worthy to attain to that age and to the resurrection of the dead."** Tabor flags this hard: this kind of resurrection is not for everybody. It is for those accounted worthy. *Sons of the resurrection. Children of the resurrection.* In that age, the transformed beings neither marry nor are given in marriage — not because they're celibate, but because they have no gender designation, cannot die, and are equal to angels.

Take away sex and death and you have a categorically different kind of being. That is Pauline resurrection. That is *not* the people coming out of tombs in Matthew 27:52-53 — those "saints" presumably went on to live further mortal years and die again.

### Naked vs. Re-Clothed — Paul Is the Opposite of Plato

In 2 Corinthians 5, Paul mixes two metaphors at once:

- **Tent vs. permanent building:** "If the earthly tent we live in is destroyed, we have a building from God, a house not made with hands, eternal in the heavens." Echoes Plato's body-as-tent — but Paul means something different.
- **Clothing:** When you die, you take off your old body and you're *naked*. The dead exist in this naked interim state. The hope is to be *re-clothed* — and Paul says **"not that we would be unclothed, but that we would be further clothed."**

This is the exact inversion of Plato. Plato wants to be naked — to escape the body, leave the prison, fly home. Paul wants a *new body*. The soul-without-a-body is *not the goal*. The goal is the new clothing — the imperishable spiritual body — put on at the cosmic transformation.

### The Parousia in the Blink of an Eye — How the Transformation Actually Happens

Paul's mechanics in 1 Corinthians 15:51-52:

> "Lo, I tell you a mystery: we shall not all sleep, but we shall all be changed, in a moment, in the twinkling of an eye, at the last trumpet. For the trumpet will sound, and the dead will be raised imperishable, and we shall be changed."

Notice the two states. **The dead come forth already in the new imperishable body** — they don't crawl out of graves in whatever state of decay; they emerge from the *state of being dead* (which is sleep / rest in the hand of God) already transformed. **The living, in the same instant, get changed** — Paul apparently pictures the physical body just melting away and being replaced by the spirit-body in a single moment.

> "This perishable nature must put on the imperishable, and this mortal nature must put on immortality... then shall come to pass the saying that is written, death is swallowed up in victory."

### Final Judgment and the Lake of Fire — It's Death, Not Eternal Torture

Tabor walks Revelation 20:11-15. A great white throne. The sea, death, and Hades give up the dead. The books are opened — including the Book of Life. **Everyone is judged by what they had done.** (Note: by *works*, not by belief.) Then death and Hades are thrown into the lake of fire — *this is the second death*. Those whose names are not in the Book of Life are also thrown in.

Tabor's read (following Edward Fudge): the punishment is *death*, not eternal conscious torture. The wicked *perish*. The standard European-art picture — souls being eternally roasted while devils prod with pitchforks — is Christian-culture inheritance, not biblical text. The fire *consumes*; it doesn't preserve.

Then comes Revelation 21: a new heavens and new earth. New Jerusalem comes down. God dwells with them. He wipes away every tear. Death is no more. Mourning and crying are gone. The river of the water of life. The tree of life with twelve kinds of fruit. *The leaves of the tree for the healing of the nations.*

This is cosmic transformation. Not soul-flight to heaven.

## Quotable Moments

> "Resurrection is not resuscitation. It doesn't have to do with someone dying and becoming what we call a corpse, a dead body, and then that body being revived to continue life — almost like a near-death experience. It isn't about that."

> "Most Christians probably hold views that are closer to Plato than Jesus or Paul, but they're not aware of it — because Christianity was just about totally platonized in the second and third centuries."

> "The first man Adam became a living being — that's *nephesh chayyah* in Hebrew, a living breather. The exact same term is given to cows and monkeys and animals and even insects crawling on the ground. In other words, any beings that live off of oxygen, that have the metabolism that we do. So it's a very physical idea."

> "Those who are accounted worthy to attain to that age and to the resurrection of the dead — I've never heard people talk about that in that language. You know — to say, do you think you will *attain* the resurrection of the dead? It's not for everybody. Not this kind of resurrection."

> "It is sown a physical body, it is raised a spiritual body. If there is a physical body — this has to do with the Adam body of the dust — then there's also a wind body that's not of the dust."

> "What kind of a body will the caterpillar have when it has its glory? If you've never seen a monarch butterfly, you'd say — I can't tell you. All I can tell you is that worm will go back into its primeval elements in the cocoon and will emerge as this new being that will be amazing and glorious and eternal."

> "We shall not all sleep, but we shall all be changed — *metamorphosis*. Isn't that something? So he doesn't use the word resurrection. You'd lose the verb 'raised up.' But being raised up is *not* the transformation. The transformation is what happens when you are raised up."

> "Our hope is not to be found naked — that would be the Platonic view, you die, you leave your tent, you leave your old clothes behind, and now you're naked and your soul is free. But in this Hebrew view that Paul is developing, he's staying consistent with the idea that death is death — not life. You wake up, and then you get re-clothed."

> "I am not preaching. I hope I'm teaching. I'm interested in the history of ideas. If you don't get your ideas straight you really can't make judgments or even have conversations about these different cultures and traditions."

> "Resurrection of the dead, as Paul understands it — as Jesus presented it, as the Book of Revelation presents it — has nothing to do with corpse revival."

## Misconceptions Addressed

| Misconception | Tabor's Response |
|---------------|------------------|
| "Resurrection means a corpse coming back to life" | That is *resuscitation* — what happens to Lazarus, Jairus's daughter, Eutychus, the widow of Nain's son, the corpse touching Elisha's bones. **All those people died again.** Resurrection in Paul's sense is *metamorphosis* — the perishable dust-body transformed into a categorically new imperishable spirit-body. Not the same body with a tune-up. A new mode of existence. |
| "Adam became a 'living soul' so humans have an immortal soul that animals don't have" | The Hebrew is *nephesh chayyah*, "living breather." **The exact same phrase is used for every animal, insect, and reptile in Genesis 1.** It is one of the most consequential mistranslations in Bible history — it smuggled Plato's anthropology into Genesis 2. |
| "When you die, your soul goes to be with Christ in heaven" | The Pauline framework: when you die, you *sleep* in the dust. Your shadow-self rests in the hand of God in Sheol/Hades. **You don't have a new body yet.** The new body is put on at the parousia, in the blink of an eye, at the last trumpet — together with everyone else, as a *cosmic event*. |
| "Death is a friend, a graceful passage home" | Paul: **"The last enemy to be destroyed is death" (1 Cor 15:26).** That's Socrates's posture, not Paul's. Paul says explicitly we want to be *clothed*, not *naked*. The hope is the new body, not escape from the body. |
| "The risen Jesus walked out of the tomb in his physical body" | Paul's framework — and Tabor argues this is the *earliest* Christian framework — is that Jesus was *transformed* into a life-giving spirit. The empty tomb in Paul's logic is a *dematerialization*, not a corpse revival. The gospel narratives that depict a fish-eating, wound-touching Jesus are later apologetic embellishment (covered in detail in Tabor video #09). |
| "Everyone gets resurrected automatically" | Luke 20:35: **"those accounted worthy to attain to that age and to the resurrection of the dead."** This kind of resurrection — the transformation into a glorified Spirit being — is conditional. Tabor flags this as a phrase most Christians have never noticed. |
| "Resurrection is an individual private event" | It's a **cosmic event**. The sea, death, and Hades give up the dead together (Rev 20). The whole creation is remade (Rev 21). Romans 8: the whole *ktisis* (creation) is in labor, waiting for its own release. Not soul-by-soul flight to heaven. |
| "The wicked are tortured forever in hell" | Tabor (following Edward Fudge's *The Fire That Consumes*): the lake of fire is the **second death**. The punishment is *death*, eternal in finality but not in duration. The fire *consumes*; it doesn't preserve. The Eternal-Conscious-Torment picture is later Christian inheritance, not what the texts say. |
| "Matthew 27:52-53 ('saints coming out of tombs') proves bodily resurrection" | Those people presumably went on to live further mortal years and *die again*. That is corpse revival, not Pauline transformation. Two verses, no other attestation — a strange isolated text, but in any case the *category* is resuscitation, not resurrection. |
| "1 Corinthians 15 is the resurrection chapter" | Tabor would rename it the **transformation chapter** — or the *eschatological transformation chapter*. The resurrection question is subsidiary. The chapter's actual topic is the cosmic metamorphosis of dust-creatures into a new race of glorified spirit-beings at the end of the age. |
