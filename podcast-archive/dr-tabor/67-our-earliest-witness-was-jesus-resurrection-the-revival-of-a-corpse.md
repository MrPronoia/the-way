## Key Teachings

### A Resuscitated Corpse Would Not Have Proved Resurrection to Paul

Tabor's headline in the clip: he does not believe the earliest Christian faith was that "a resuscitated corpse came out and went around eating with people for forty days." For Paul — the only first-person resurrection witness in the New Testament — that scenario "wouldn't prove anything." Resurrection in Paul's framework is cosmic transformation (apotheosis), not bodily revival. A revived corpse is the wrong category of event entirely.

### "I Would Be Just Fine with Visiting the Tomb of Jesus"

The tell-line in the clip. Tabor is signaling that the earliest resurrection faith does not depend on an empty tomb. If Jesus's bones were still in an ossuary somewhere, Paul's resurrection faith would be undisturbed — because what Paul means by "resurrection" is not the same physical body coming back, but a cosmic metamorphosis into a glorified Spirit-being. The tomb-and-corpse question is a *later* apologetic concern, not an early one.

## Misconceptions Addressed

| Misconception | Tabor's Response |
|---------------|------------------|
| "A revived corpse walking around for forty days is what proves resurrection" | For Paul, that would prove nothing. Resurrection in the earliest framework is cosmic transformation, not corpse-reanimation. A flesh-and-blood body still subject to the dust would be the *wrong category* of evidence. |
| "If Jesus's bones were found, Christianity would collapse" | Tabor: "I would be just fine with visiting the tomb of Jesus." The earliest resurrection faith does not depend on an empty tomb — that's a later apologetic concern, not an early one. |
| "The forty-days-of-meals account is the original resurrection story" | It's the *late* embellishment layer (Luke-Acts and John, 50-70 years after the events). The earliest layer (Paul, Mark) has no fish-eating, no forty days, no locked-door walk-throughs. |
