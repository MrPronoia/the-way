## Summary

Tabor sits down with John Pinna of the Crossing Faiths podcast — a Muslim host — for one of the most relaxed and far-ranging conversations in this playlist. The Muslim interlocutor angle matters: Pinna isn't pushing back from a Trinitarian-Christian frame, so the conversation cuts straight to the questions Tabor finds most interesting. Was Paul really the founder of Christianity? Was Jesus God in Paul's earliest letters? Who actually ran the Jerusalem movement before Paul showed up? And — the question Pinna keeps circling back to — could the **Ebionites** (Jewish followers of Jesus who rejected Paul, rejected the divinity of Jesus, and considered the New Testament corrupt) have been the bridge between original Jesus-faith and emerging Islam?

The argument is vintage Tabor, but pulled together in unusually concentrated form. Paul is "the most influential human who's ever lived" in terms of textual reach — Augustine's reading of Romans set the trajectory of Western Christianity. But if you go behind Paul to the **Dark Age of Christianity** (the 20-year gap between Jesus and Paul, plus the 40-year gap after Paul before the gospels), the picture changes completely. The dominant figure isn't Paul. It isn't even Peter. It's **James**, the brother of Jesus, sitting like a judge in Acts 15 banging his gavel and ruling that Gentiles can enter the movement *without* converting to Judaism — just keep the Noahide laws (the same general moral code Islam preserves). Paul calls these Jerusalem leaders the "so-called pillars" and says **"what they are means nothing to me"** — a line Tabor reads as Paul's open declaration that his revelation from the heavenly Christ outranks the apostles who actually walked with the earthly Jesus.

The conversation lands hard on three Muslim-resonant points: (1) Paul's seven authentic letters do **not** call Jesus God — even the famous Philippians 2 hymn is an *Adam* christology (the second Adam who *didn't* grasp at equality with God, unlike the first), not a pre-existence/Incarnation text; (2) the **Ebionites** held precisely the positions Muslims hold — one God, no Incarnation, scriptures corrupted, Paul as an enemy who sowed false seeds in the field — and they migrated into the Arabian peninsula in the centuries before Muhammad; (3) the famous Josephus passage about Jesus survives in an **Arabic version** that lacks all the Christological additions of the Greek manuscripts, suggesting an older, less embellished Jesus tradition was preserved through Muslim channels. Pinna closes by quoting his own great-grandfather: "Islam is the bloodline to the end of days, and the priesthood was restored by the Christians — the Jewish-Jesus movement." Tabor doesn't disagree.

## Key Teachings

### Paul as the Most Influential Human Who Ever Lived

Tabor opens with the provocation he uses on his students: **Paul is arguably the most influential human in history**. Not Jesus, not Moses, not Muhammad, not the Buddha — Paul. Why? Because the basic ethics Jesus taught (peace, nonviolence, care for the poor and meek) were not unique to him; they go back to the Hebrew prophets and the Torah, shared by all Abrahamic faiths. What's unique is Paul's **letters** — specifically Romans, the theological treatise that shaped Augustine, then Western Christianity, then the modern world.

Crucial qualifier: Paul was almost certainly **not** influential in his own time. He plants "small little beachheads" in Greco-Roman cities across Asia Minor and Greece. The archaeological record shows these were tiny communities, not a world-conquering movement. His influence is **textual** and **posthumous** — the letters got canonized, Augustine ran with them, and 1,500 years later Luther split the church over a single misread of Habakkuk 2:4.

### The Dark Age of Christianity — Why James Disappears and Paul Becomes Central

Jesus dies around 30 CE. Paul appears around 50 CE. That's a 20-year gap with almost no surviving documentation — Tabor calls this the **Dark Age of Christianity**. Then Paul writes for about a decade. Then another 20 years pass before Mark is written. Then 40 more years until Jerusalem falls in 70 CE and the gospels begin to settle.

Who was in charge during the Dark Age? **Not Paul. Not even Peter.** It was **James, the brother of Jesus.** Paul himself says it (Galatians 1-2). The Gospel of Thomas says it: "Wherever you go, go to James the Just, for whom heaven and earth came to be." Josephus says it. Acts 15 says it — though Acts buries the lede by not even introducing James as Jesus's brother or the head of the movement; he just walks on stage halfway through the book like a judge banging his gavel.

Tabor's image: he goes to the Vatican, sees the massive statues of Paul on the right and Peter on the left, and thinks — **where's James?** He should be in the center. Paul shouldn't be in the picture at all. John should be on the right. The Catholic foundation story ("upon this rock I will build my church") is a Matthean retrofit. The original movement was led by **James in Jerusalem**, with Peter and John as his pillars.

### "What They Are Means Nothing to Me" — Paul's Open Break with the Pillars

The most consequential line in Paul's authentic letters, Tabor argues, is in Galatians 2:6: Paul says he met the **"so-called pillars"** of the church — James, Peter, John — and **"what they are means nothing to me."** Read it slow. This is Paul telling his Gentile converts that the apostles who actually walked with Jesus, ate with him, were trained by him for three years — *their opinions don't matter* if they contradict Paul's revelation from the heavenly Christ.

Why? Because Paul says he got his gospel **not from men**, not through men, but by a direct revelation of Jesus Christ (Galatians 1:11-12). And by "Jesus" Paul means the **heavenly, exalted Jesus** — not the carpenter from Nazareth who taught the disciples in Galilee. Paul is claiming a vertical, mystical channel that supersedes the horizontal, in-the-flesh training the apostles received. "If even an angel from heaven preaches a different gospel," Paul says, "let him be cursed."

This sets up the central dynamic of Tabor's book *Paul and Jesus*: **the apostles inherited Jesus's actual teaching. Paul inherited a vision.** When the two conflict, Paul says the vision wins.

### Paul Did Not Think Jesus Was God

This is the section that lights up Muslim ears, and Tabor delivers it cleanly. **In the seven authentic letters of Paul, Jesus is not God.** Paul is a monotheist. 1 Corinthians 8:6 — "For us there is one God, the Father, and one Lord, Jesus Christ." Two different categories. *Lord* (Greek *kyrios*) means *master*, *sir*. You can call a human being *kyrios*. It does not mean *theos* (God).

What about Philippians 2 — the hymn where Jesus "though he was in the form of God, did not count equality with God a thing to be grasped, but emptied himself, taking the form of a servant"? Doesn't that prove pre-existence and Incarnation? Tabor's reading: **this is an Adam christology, not an Incarnation christology**. Who was made in the image of God and tempted by Satan with "you will become like God if you eat this fruit"? **Adam.** And Adam *grasped* at the equality. Jesus, the second Adam, **did not grasp** — he chose service instead. Therefore God exalted him.

And the verse right before the hymn — the one everyone skips — is the punchline: "Have this mind in you which was in Christ Jesus." Paul is telling **you** not to grasp at equality with God either. The pattern is applied to all believers. **It's a monotheism teaching, not a deity teaching.** Later Christians read pre-existence into it. Paul didn't put it there.

### Paul's Eucharist Was Not James's Eucharist

Tabor flags a sharp contrast that often gets glossed. In **1 Corinthians 11**, Paul writes: "I received from the Lord [meaning the heavenly Jesus] what I also delivered to you, that the Lord Jesus on the night he was betrayed took bread... this is my body... this is my blood... drink it, eat it."

Now ask: would a Torah-observant Jewish messiah figure say *drink my blood and eat my body* — even symbolically? Blood is one of the most explicit Torah prohibitions; consuming it is forbidden in **Genesis 9** (a Noahide law applicable to all humans, Jew and Gentile). The mental category doesn't exist in Second Temple Judaism. It exists in Greek mystery religions, in magical papyri, in Dionysian cult. **It's a Greco-Roman religious motif Paul has imported.**

And the Dead Sea Scrolls have a Messianic Banquet text — found in Cave 1 — describing a meal where the Messiah blesses bread and wine. **No body, no blood.** Just the standard Jewish blessing over bread and wine. This is what Tabor thinks James's table looked like. Not Paul's.

### The Ebionites — A Lost Form of Jesus-Faith That Looked Like Islam

Pinna keeps steering toward the Ebionites, and Tabor obliges. The **Ebionites** ("the poor ones," from Hebrew *evyonim*) were a Jewish-Christian movement that emerged in the 1st-2nd centuries. We only know about them from their enemies — the church fathers who hated them — but the picture is consistent:

- **One God.** No Trinity, no divine Jesus. Jesus is a prophet/messiah, not God.
- **Torah-observant.** They kept the law, circumcision, kosher.
- **The New Testament is corrupted.** They held that Paul's writings were false and that even the gospels had been tampered with. The true teaching is in **James** and in a hypothetical sayings-source of Jesus.
- **Paul is the enemy of the gospel.** They quoted the parable of the wheat and tares (Matthew 13): Paul came into the field and sowed false seeds among the true crop. God will sort it out at the end.
- **The Gospel of Thomas points to James, not Paul.** "Go to James the Just, for whom heaven and earth came to be."

Tabor notes that these Ebionite communities **migrated into the Arabian peninsula** in the centuries before Muhammad. Robert Eisenman and others have argued early Islam was in contact with these communities — explaining why so many Islamic positions (one God, no Incarnation, Jesus as prophet, scriptures corrupted, Paul as the corruptor) match Ebionite positions almost verse-for-verse.

Pinna's response is striking: "It just makes it more *likely* and *right* for when the final Revelation came down, you had a group of people that already thought and could chew on the idea of Islam in a much more equitable way." Tabor doesn't push back. He says the parallel deserves more archaeological research.

### The Arabic Josephus — Evidence of an Older, Less Christological Jesus Tradition

A specific datapoint Tabor offers: the famous **Testimonium Flavianum** in Josephus's *Antiquities* (Book 18) — the passage where Josephus describes Jesus — survives in two distinct manuscript traditions.

- The **Greek version** (preserved by Christians) has heavy Christological additions: "if it be lawful to call him a man... he was the Christ... he appeared to them alive the third day."
- The **Arabic version** (preserved through Muslim channels) has none of that. It's a sober, straightforward description: "At that time there arose a wise man who taught the people..." No deity claims, no resurrection apologetics.

This is a smoking gun, Tabor argues. **Christians preserved the Greek copies of Josephus precisely because of the divinizing additions** — they loved the passage because it made a Jewish historian into a witness for the deity of Jesus. The Arabic tradition preserved an older, pre-embellished version. The implication: even our "neutral" historical witnesses to Jesus were edited by Christian copyists over the centuries, and the Arabic preservation may be closer to what Josephus actually wrote.

### Paul Believed the End Was Years Away — And Was Wrong

Paul's entire ethical and social framework is collapsed by the fact that **the end didn't come**. He told the Corinthians not to bother getting married. He said slaves shouldn't seek freedom, that the form of this world is passing away, that "we who are alive and remain" will be caught up to meet the Lord in the air. He thought the parousia was 5-10 years out, maximum.

Two thousand years later, Christians either (a) say Jesus was a false prophet because the prophecy failed, or (b) allegorize the apocalypse into a "spiritual kingdom already here" (the **full preterist** position popular on YouTube these days). Tabor's verdict: neither works. Read Daniel 2 or Isaiah 11 — when the kingdom of God comes, the wolf lies with the lamb, swords become plowshares, nations stop making war. **"Look at the past 2,000 years — I'm not sure we're in the kingdom of God."**

So what do we do with Paul? Tabor's answer: capture him in his moment. Paul writing in 51 CE thinks history is wrapping up. He's not setting timeless doctrine for a 2,000-year church. The tragedy is that his moment-bound apocalyptic instructions got canonized as eternal teaching. **"If Paul could read Colossians or Ephesians" — letters written in his name after his death, that have Jesus creating the world — "I think he would say: I never said that. This is crazy."**

## Misconceptions Addressed

| Misconception | Tabor's Response |
|---------------|------------------|
| "Paul founded Christianity intentionally as the religion we know" | Paul thought the world was ending in 5-10 years. He never imagined a 2,000-year institutional church. His letters got canonized and theologized *beyond* what he himself meant — and later pseudo-Pauline letters (Colossians, Ephesians, Pastorals) pushed his thought in directions he wouldn't have recognized. |
| "Paul taught the divinity of Jesus from the start" | In the seven authentic letters, no. Paul is a monotheist. 1 Cor 8:6 distinguishes God from Lord. Philippians 2 is an Adam christology, not Incarnation. Pre-existence, full deity, and Trinitarian formulas come later — most after Paul's death. |
| "Peter was the founder of the early church" | The Catholic claim is built on Matthew 16. In actual historical sources — Paul's own letters, Josephus, the Gospel of Thomas, Acts 15 — **James** is the head of the movement. Peter is a pillar; James is the leader. |
| "The New Testament is internally consistent and was written by harmonious apostles" | Read Galatians 2 alongside Acts 15. Read James alongside Romans. Paul's "what they are means nothing to me" and James's "faith without works is dead" are not harmonized texts — they are two sides of an active first-century argument. |
| "The Ebionites were a fringe heresy" | They were a major branch of Jewish-Christianity that survived for centuries and migrated into Arabia. We only have them framed as heretical because the orthodox church wrote them out. From their perspective, **Pauline Christianity was the heresy**. |
| "Jesus instituted the Eucharist with 'eat my body, drink my blood'" | The earliest source for this is Paul (1 Cor 11), who says he received it from the heavenly Jesus by revelation. The contemporary Jewish Messianic Banquet text from Qumran has bread and wine — no body/blood language. Body-and-blood is alien to Second Temple Judaism. |
