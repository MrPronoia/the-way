**Speaker:** Dr. James Tabor (PhD, Univ. of Chicago; Professor Emeritus, UNC Charlotte)
**Interviewer:** Barbara DeLong (NightLight Radio, BlogTalkRadio)
**Source:** [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ltRUrbaOsnw)
**Published:** July 5, 2023

## Summary

A two-hour radio conversation with Barbara DeLong covering Tabor's Paul-trilogy — *Paul and Jesus*, *The Jesus Discovery*, and *Paul's Ascent to Paradise* — read together as a methodological set. DeLong is unusual among Tabor interviewers in that her background is in *mysticism*, not just New Testament studies, which pushes the conversation into territory other interviews skim past: Paul's actual mystical experience (the 2 Cor 12 ascent to paradise), the "things unutterable," the meaning of Paul's thorn-in-the-flesh, and the inner texture of what Paul claims happened to him.

The conversation's spine is Tabor's already-familiar Pauline reconstruction (Paul born ~5 BCE in Galilee → exiled to Tarsus → studied under Gamaliel in Jerusalem → "never met Jesus" in the flesh → claimed direct revelation → "13th apostle" claim → broke with the Jerusalem leadership), but the 2-hour format lets him develop two distinctive arguments: **(1)** the *already/not-yet* eschatological framework that explains why Paul's social ethics (women silent, slaves stay slaves, don't marry) were *circumstantial* rather than universal — calibrated to a 5-10 year horizon that never arrived — and **(2)** the mystical-versus-creedal Christianity divide. The "Apostles' Creed has a blank space" line is the centerpiece: between "born of a virgin" and "crucified, buried, raised," the entire *life and teaching of Jesus* is missing. Paul started a creedal Christianity (do you believe X, Y, Z?) that gradually displaced Jesus's actual ethics-and-Kingdom teaching.

DeLong asks Tabor for the *Mark vs. Paul vs. James* distinction and Tabor delivers a clean version: the Letter of James contains *no* Pauline blood-atonement theology, *no* divinity-of-Jesus, *no* substitutionary cross — just Sermon-on-the-Mount ethics in a Jewish key. The Didache (the early Christian "Teaching of the Twelve Apostles," found at Mount Athos in the 1870s) preserves a *Messianic-banquet* Eucharist with bread and wine blessed in anticipation of the Kingdom — *not* the body-and-blood-of-Christ formula Paul presents in 1 Cor 11. This makes the Didache the surviving witness to a pre-Pauline Christianity that the church largely buried. Tabor's verdict on the Last Supper: Jesus would never have said "this is my body, this is my blood" — that's a Pauline (or post-Pauline) construct grafted onto a Jewish blessing-meal that originally pointed forward to the Messianic banquet.

Closing themes worth flagging: a substantial section on **James the brother of Jesus** (missing from Vatican statuary; the actual leader of the Jerusalem movement; carries the Davidic bloodline through Mary); Tabor's forthcoming **Lost Mary book** (2023, then delayed); a long meditation on the **Mary-and-Martha story** as a feminist anchor (Mary "sitting at the feet" = registered as a rabbinic student); Tabor's vegetarianism and the **Noahide laws on respecting animal blood**; and a closing exchange on **Matthew 16's "you are Peter and on this rock"** as a Matthean editorial addition not in Mark (the Petrine-primacy foundation rests on one editorial layer in one gospel that Luke doesn't repeat).

## Key Teachings

### The Three Books as a Trilogy

DeLong is unusual in having read all three Tabor books in close succession:
1. **Paul and Jesus** — the popular trade book; Paul's six great ideas; the historical reconstruction
2. **The Jesus Discovery** — the Talpiot tomb / Tomb of the Shroud archaeological work
3. **Paul's Ascent to Paradise** — Tabor's revised dissertation; the mystical experience side

Tabor's recommended reading order: *Paul and Jesus* first (foundation), *Paul's Ascent to Paradise* second (the mystical companion). They are a paired set — the historical Paul and the mystical Paul, the same figure viewed through two methodological lenses.

### Paul's Biographical Reconstruction (Recap with Galilean Detail)

Tabor again advances Jerome's tradition: Paul probably born in Gish/Gischala in northern Galilee around 5 BCE, family exiled to Tarsus during the post-Herodian revolt of 4 BCE. This makes Paul and Jesus near-contemporaries possibly born **50 miles apart in Galilee** — though they never met as children, since Paul's family was displaced. Both then converge on Jerusalem: Jesus for festivals, Paul for rabbinic training under Gamaliel.

The key methodological move: Paul never met Jesus in the flesh. "I have lectured on this around the world and people still come up afterward saying 'I didn't know that.'" This biographical fact is suppressed by popular Christian preaching because it complicates the apostolic-authority claim — Paul never sat at Jesus's feet, never heard his teachings firsthand, never broke bread with him.

### The Three Worlds Paul Inhabits

A useful framing Tabor offers for this interview specifically:
1. **Greco-Roman/Hellenistic world** — Tarsus, Roman citizenship, Stoic philosophy, the mystery religions, Plato
2. **Jewish rabbinic world** — Jerusalem, Gamaliel, Pharisaic training, the Hillel/Shammai context
3. **The "third world" of Jesus** — which Paul enters only after his Damascus vision

Paul is more complex than figures like Augustine (Manichean → Christian) because he's juggling all three worlds simultaneously. The 2 Corinthians 4:18 quote — "we look not at the things that are seen, but at the things that are unseen, for the things that are seen are temporal but the things that are unseen are eternal" — could be straight Plato. But Paul also remains rooted in Hebrew Bible earthiness. This dual register is what produces Pauline thought.

### The Already / Not-Yet Framework

The interpretive key to Paul's social ethics. Paul thinks the parousia is imminent — 5 to 10 years out. He has a chapter Tabor calls "Already not yet" in *Paul and Jesus*: the new age is *already* breaking in (in Christ there is no male or female, no Jew or Greek, no slave or free — Gal 3:28) but *not yet* fully realized.

This explains everything that has been weaponized as universal Pauline ethics:
- "Women keep silent" (1 Cor 14) — circumstantial, not universal; "Don't rock the cultural boat for the brief time remaining"
- "Slaves obey your masters" (Eph 6, Col 3) — circumstantial; "the form of this world is passing away"
- "Don't get married" (1 Cor 7) — Tabor explicitly distinguishes *apocalyptic celibacy* from *Catholic celibacy*; "in the same chapter Paul tells couples to have sex regularly and not deprive one another"
- "Honor the emperor" even when the emperor is Nero (Rom 13) — circumstantial; Paul thinks the political order is about to be transformed

The problem: it didn't happen, and the church inherited the *instructions* without the *framework*. Fundamentalists weaponize Paul's apocalyptic circumstantial ethics as universal eternal commandments.

### Paul's Mystical Ascent (2 Corinthians 12)

The conversation's mystical centerpiece. Paul claims to have been "caught up to the third heaven... whether in the body or out of the body I do not know... caught up into paradise and heard things unutterable, which it is not lawful for a man to utter."

Tabor's analysis:
- **Third heaven** in Hellenistic cosmology is the lowest of seven heavens — the "escape velocity" tier. Mystics often stop there.
- **Paradise** (Hebrew *pardes*) is the highest — direct presence of God, the "force of all forces."
- "Things unutterable" — Greek *arrēta* can mean "forbidden to tell" OR "impossible to express." Tabor accepts both readings.
- The "whether in the body or out of the body I do not know" admission rules out a strictly *physical* ascent. Paul himself is unsure whether his body went anywhere — it might have been a purely inner experience.
- This is "the only autobiographical account of an ascent to the highest heaven from the ancient world." We have literary accounts (others claiming various people ascended) but only Paul says "I."

DeLong's contribution: she identifies Paul as fundamentally **a mystic**, not just a theologian. Tabor agrees and notes Paul is *also* an apocalypticist (thinks the cosmos is about to be transformed) — both dimensions are present.

### "Last But Not Least" — Paul's Authority Claim

A close textual move. Paul repeatedly puts himself down: "I am the least of the apostles," "I am unworthy to be called an apostle," "I persecuted the church." Then immediately: "but I labored harder than them all." 1 Corinthians 15:8 calls himself an *ektroma* — "untimely born," a kind of miscarriage.

The pattern: Paul humbles himself rhetorically while making the absolute strongest possible apostolic claim. He's last but not least. He didn't meet Jesus in the flesh, but he met the *glorified* Jesus — which is "even better." The other apostles knew the earthly Jesus; Paul claims to have encountered the cosmic Christ. This is a stronger claim, not a weaker one.

His "thorn in the flesh" / "messenger of Satan" (2 Cor 12:7) functions as the divine check on Paul's pride. *Because* of the super-abundant revelations, he's given some affliction to keep him from being too exalted. Tabor's speculation: the thorn may be sexual temptation (Paul talks in Romans 7 about the lust he doesn't want, the good he would do that he doesn't, the evil he wouldn't do that he does). DeLong reads it as more internal/emotional — a wounding of spirit, not body. Tabor allows the speculation but doesn't commit.

### The Two Adams (Recap)

A condensed version of the framework developed in #08, #12, #15, #26, #65: Jesus is Adam-2, the *first* human to successfully refuse to grasp at equality with God and instead empty himself completely. Therefore exalted. Therefore "firstborn of many" — believers will follow into the same cosmic glorification. The cosmos is "pregnant" with these new beings; Romans 8 calls the creation "groaning in labor pains."

Paul's mystical experience confirmed the framework: he saw a *glorified* Jesus, transformed from flesh to "life-giving spirit." Not a resuscitated corpse walking around. The transformation Jesus underwent is the transformation believers will undergo at the parousia.

### Why James Is Missing from the Vatican

A striking image Tabor returns to. Walk up the front steps of St. Peter's Basilica in Rome: on the right, the great statue of Paul; on the left, the great statue of Peter. **There is no monument to James the brother of Jesus.** Yet James — if you read Galatians, Acts 15, 1 Corinthians 15, the Letter of James, and the patristic sources on the Jerusalem community — was *the actual leader of the Jesus movement after Jesus*. Caliphate-style succession (passed through the Davidic bloodline via Mary).

The Vatican structure tells you something: the church Peter-and-Paul founded buried the James-led original community. The Letter of James (often called the "epistle of straw" by Luther, who put it in small print at the back of his Bible) preserves Jewish-Christian ethics with no blood-atonement theology, no Trinitarian formula, no divinity-of-Jesus — just Sermon-on-the-Mount ethics extended.

If you read James and Paul side by side, you'll see *two different Christianities*. The Vatican Vault chose Paul.

### Mary-and-Martha as Feminist Anchor (Luke 10)

A long affectionate digression on Tabor's favorite gospel story. The setup: Jesus is teaching at the home of Mary and Martha. Martha is in the kitchen serving the male disciples. Mary goes in and **sits at Jesus's feet**.

The phrase "sitting at the feet" of a rabbi is technical rabbinic language meaning **enrolled as a student**. Mary is in class. Martha protests; Jesus replies: "Martha, Martha, you are anxious and troubled about many things; one thing is needful. Mary has chosen the good portion which shall not be taken away from her."

This is, in Tabor's reading, Jesus enrolling a *woman* in his rabbinic academy. The historical analog: Harvard, Yale, William & Mary, Princeton were all founded as men-only seminaries; co-education didn't reach those institutions until the *20th century*. And yet here is Jesus in the first century placing a woman in the student position.

Luke 8 reinforces this — Jesus's entourage includes Mary Magdalene, Joanna (wife of Herod Antipas's chief of staff), Susanna, and "many others" who supported him "out of their resources." This isn't 12 guys walking up and down Galilee — it's a caravan of men, women, children, with women holding economic patronage roles. **No wonder the Roman authorities found this disturbing**: women leaving their domestic roles to follow a teacher.

### The Apostles' Creed Has a Blank Space

The conversation's most striking interpretive move. The Apostles' Creed:
> "...born of the Virgin Mary, [BLANK], suffered under Pontius Pilate, was crucified, died, and was buried..."

The blank is **Jesus's entire life and teaching**. Between birth and crucifixion, the Creed is silent. There's no Sermon on the Mount in the Creed. No parables. No Beatitudes. No "love your enemies." No "blessed are the poor." No Mary-and-Martha. No "go and learn what this means" (Tabor's favorite Jesus line). The Creed jumps from birth → cross → resurrection → ascension.

This is **Paul's gospel**, not Jesus's gospel. Paul's gospel is the *that*-ness — that Jesus was crucified, that he was buried, that he was raised. The teachings of Jesus drop out. And once the Creed becomes the test of orthodoxy, Christians are judged not by how they live but by what they confess. **Creedal Christianity displaces Kingdom Christianity.**

Tabor: "There are plenty of fundamentalist Christians who will ask 'do you believe the Virgin birth, the divinity, the inerrancy, the resurrection?' If you say yes to all of them, you're a Christian. They never ask 'did you visit the sick, did you go to the prison, did you care for the least of these?'" The questions Jesus said would determine judgment have been replaced by questions about credal assent.

### The Didache — Pre-Pauline Eucharist Preserved

A scholarly bombshell Tabor circles back to repeatedly. The **Didache** ("Teaching of the Twelve Apostles") is an early Christian text **lost for centuries and rediscovered at Mount Athos around 1870**. It contains a Christian liturgy from the late 1st century with prayers over **bread and wine** — but **NO body-and-blood interpretation**.

The Didache's Eucharist prayers bless God for "the holy vine of David" and "the knowledge" — explicitly Messianic-banquet anticipation, not substitutionary atonement. This proves a pre-Pauline (or non-Pauline) Christian tradition existed in which the bread-and-wine blessing was a **Jewish Sabbath-and-Messianic prayer**, not a mystical participation in the death of Christ.

The Last Supper is therefore *not* what Paul says it is (1 Cor 11) — at least not historically. Tabor's verdict: "I don't think Jesus ever said 'this is my body, this is my blood.' A Jew would not say that even symbolically. The only ancient parallel is **Egyptian mystery religions where you drink the blood of the god**. Paul appears to have grafted that pattern onto the Jewish blessing-meal."

Luke even preserves a *fragment* of the original non-Pauline tradition: in Luke's account, the cup is mentioned without the blood interpretation — a residual layer of the original Messianic-banquet meaning.

### Mark 7 Mistranslation — "Thus He Declared All Foods Clean"

A specific textual correction Tabor surfaces. Mark 7:19 has a parenthetical English gloss: "(Thus he declared all foods clean.)" Most English readers take this as Jesus abolishing kosher law.

**The phrase is not in the Greek.** It's an editorial insertion by translators. The actual Greek of Mark 7:19 — read carefully — is Jesus making a *joke* about ritual purity: what goes into your mouth goes into your stomach and then **into the toilet** (literal Greek), so don't worry about it. Your body takes care of physical defilement. The real impurity is what comes *out of your heart*.

This is **not about abrogating kosher law**. It's about the proper locus of spiritual concern. Both the historical Jesus and James (his brother) almost certainly kept kosher.

### The Antioch Confrontation (Galatians 2) — "What They Are Means Nothing to Me"

A standard Tabor exegesis revisited. The Antioch incident is *not* Peter eating non-kosher with Gentiles. Peter was likely Torah-observant. The dispute is about **table fellowship**: Peter is willing to share table with Gentiles when no one is watching, then physically withdraws when James's emissaries arrive from Jerusalem. Paul calls this hypocrisy and confronts him publicly.

The deeper structure: in Galatians 1-2 Paul says of the Jerusalem pillars (James, Peter, John): "what they are means nothing to me." Tabor's careful read: this isn't pure hostility. The phrase is conditional — *if* they had contradicted Paul's gospel, he would have walked away. Paul respects them but submits to his own revelation as superior to theirs.

The "I wish they would cut it off" line about the circumcision party (Gal 5:12) is Paul's "nastiest" comment — pure exasperation, "human Paul" at his most frustrated.

### Tabor Is Vegetarian — And the Noahide Laws Cover Animal Welfare

A personal aside that becomes substantive. Tabor is vegetarian, which he reveals while discussing the rabbinic concept of the **Noahide laws** — basic moral commandments that apply to all humanity (not just Jews) per Genesis 9 forward. The rabbis enumerate seven:
1. Don't murder
2. Don't lie / bear false witness
3. Don't steal
4. Don't be sexually immoral
5. Don't blaspheme the one God
6. Don't worship idols
7. **Don't be cruel to animals** (don't eat blood; respect animal life; drain blood with respect, as Native American tradition does)

The animal welfare command is striking — and Tabor reads it as a critique of modern slaughterhouses. Acts 15's instructions for Gentile believers include "abstain from blood" — preserving the Jewish-Christian Noahide framework rather than imposing full Torah observance.

### Pauline Christianity Is Not Required for Salvation in Jewish Theology

Tabor pushes against a common Christian misconception. Judaism does NOT teach you must convert to be saved. Maimonides explicitly: "All righteous Gentiles have a place in the World to Come." The rabbis have never said you must be Jewish to have a full relationship with God.

Paul understood this — his gospel for Gentiles is essentially Noahide-laws-plus-Jesus, not "convert to Judaism." James in Acts 15 confirms: Gentile believers do NOT need circumcision, do NOT need full Torah observance — just basic morality plus monotheism. The Christian misconception that "Paul liberated Gentiles from Jewish legalism" misreads what was actually the Jewish position all along.

### Why Tabor Is Not a Believing Christian (Personal Reflection)

A clarification that surfaces multiple times. Tabor is interested in *what Paul actually thought* and *what Jesus actually taught*. He's not personally a believing Christian, but he's "really attracted" to the historical Jesus — finds the Sermon-on-the-Mount ethics genuinely transformative, finds Hillel's Golden Rule ("Whatever is hateful to you, do not do to another") equally compelling.

This stance lets him read Paul critically without protecting later Christian doctrinal investments. Paul can be wrong about the parousia timing, can have misread Habakkuk and Genesis 15, can have started a "creedal Christianity" that displaced Jesus — and none of this is a problem for Tabor's project.

### Matthew 16 "On This Rock" — A Matthean Editorial Addition

Closing exchange. The famous Catholic prooftext for papal primacy — "you are Peter and on this rock I will build my church" — is **only in Matthew 16**. Mark's parallel (Mark 8) is the original; Matthew added the Peter-as-rock blessing material. Luke also has the parallel but does NOT include the Peter-as-foundation language.

The Protestant/Catholic dispute over *petros* (small rock) vs *petra* (big rock) "doesn't really hold up very well in Greek." The point Tabor makes is more fundamental: this is *Matthean editorial layering*, not historical Jesus material. The Catholic doctrine of Petrine primacy is built on one editorial expansion in one gospel that Mark didn't have and Luke didn't repeat.

Additional Matthew-as-late-development data: the Trinitarian baptismal formula in Matthew 28:19 ("baptizing them in the name of the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit") is **missing from some early manuscripts**, and the Hebrew Matthew preserved by medieval rabbis (Shem Tov's Matthew) doesn't have it either. The formula is likely a late insertion reflecting developing Trinitarian liturgy, not original Jesus instruction.
