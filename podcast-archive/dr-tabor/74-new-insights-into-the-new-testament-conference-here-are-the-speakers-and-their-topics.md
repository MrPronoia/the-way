## Summary

Dr. James Tabor previews the 2024 "New Insights Into the New Testament" conference sponsored by Bart Ehrman — a virtual gathering of ten internationally known scholars presenting new, cutting-edge work on Paul. Tabor frames Paul as "next to Jesus, the most important figure in the history of Christianity" and notes that the lectures are pitched at non-specialists while presenting material that even other scholars could learn from. Several talks will directly challenge mainstream scholarly consensus on Paul.

The video functions as an annotated lineup card. Tabor walks through each speaker and their topic, grouping them into three buckets: (1) talks challenging long-held assumptions about Paul (Fredriksen, White, Staples, Tabor himself), (2) talks on under-explored angles (Knust on Paul's sexual vocabulary, Walsh on Paul's Greek philosophical heritage, Charles on Paul's reimagining of time), and (3) talks on the fraught question of Paul, the Law, and Jewish identity (Marcus, Barclay). The lineup is a useful snapshot of Tabor's scholarly network — the historical-critical, Ehrman-adjacent wing of Pauline studies.

## Key Teachings

### Conference Format and Premise

The conference is virtual, two days of lectures with live Q&A, lifetime access to recordings, a pre-event mixer, a VIP scholar mixer, a post-conference roundtable, and a "Hot Topic" debate between two of the panelists on an issue not covered in the main lectures. Pricing: $99 standard, $79 early bird before August 24th. The pre-event "Historical Paul" course (a 50-minute Tabor lecture with Q&A) is included free for all registrants as a prequel. Tabor stresses there is nothing else like it — scholar-led but pitched for non-specialists, with no travel required.

### Talks That Challenge Mainstream Assumptions About Paul

Four of the ten lectures directly contest views most scholars and lay readers hold:

- **Paula Fredriksen** (Boston University emerita; Hebrew University) — *Paul and the Many Gods of Ancient Jewish Monotheism.* Argues, contrary to most assumptions, that Paul was **not** a strict monotheist who believed in only one God.
- **Benjamin White** (Clemson) — *Fuzzy Math: The Challenge of Counting Paul's Authentic Letters.* Challenges the century-old scholarly consensus that distinguishes seven "undisputed" Pauline letters from six "disputed" ones. Tabor calls this potentially huge if he can pull it off.
- **Jason Staples** (NC State) — *Not by Faith Alone: Paul's Gospel Wasn't What You Probably Think.* Argues that Paul's gospel was misread by later Christianity — Paul actually held that a person is saved by their **works**, by how they live, not by faith in Christ's death and resurrection alone.
- **James Tabor** (UNC Charlotte) — *Paul's Greatest Idea and How It Was Superseded.* Argues Paul's views shifted before vs. after his death. For Paul, Jesus wasn't the only figure to be exalted — Paul's gospel was that **all believers** would be transformed into divine beings as Christ was. Only later did Christian believers narrow this to Jesus alone, producing the high Christology of later centuries.

### Talks on Under-Explored Angles

- **Jennifer Knust** (Duke) — *The Apostle Paul's Sexual Vocabulary.* Paul uses many Greek words for sex that are hard to translate; mistranslations have produced false readings of Paul's views on sex and sexuality.
- **Robin Faith Walsh** (Univ. of Miami) — *Paul's Philosophical Heritage.* Argues Paul was heavily influenced by non-Christian, non-Jewish Greek philosophy — often overlooked but significant for understanding his teachings on the afterlife and the human spirit.
- **Ron Charles** (Univ. of Toronto) — *Paul's Radical Reimagining of Time.* Paul's idea of time is undervalued but central to his message — past and future history reorganized around what Christ did and will do.

### Talks on Paul, the Law, and Jewish Identity

- **Joel Marcus** (Duke emeritus) — *Reformer or Renegade: Paul, the Law, and the Jewish People.* Argues Paul's letters give **mixed signals** on whether the Law had to be kept, whether Law-observant Jews had to convert, and why the Law was given in the first place.
- **John Barclay** (Univ. of Durham, England) — *Grace and Giving: The Economics, Politics, and Theology Behind Paul's Collection for Jerusalem.* Argues Paul's Gentile collection for the poor in Jerusalem was, in part, a strategy to prevent Christianity from splitting into two religions — one for Jews, one for Gentiles.

### Tabor's Own Talk: Paul as Predicted Messenger

Tabor's second talk for the conference (separate from the "Paul's Greatest Idea" listing) is titled *Visions of Grandeur: Paul and the Plan of God.* He will argue Paul believed God had chosen him personally to take salvation to all nations as part of an eternal plan laid down from the beginning of time. Paul saw himself as **the one predicted by Isaiah** — the "light to the Gentiles" of Isaiah 49:6, God's ultimate messenger of redemption to the ends of the earth.
