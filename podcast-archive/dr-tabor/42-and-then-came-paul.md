## Summary

Dr. Tabor delivers a tight, narrative-style overview of how Saul of Tarsus stepped onto the stage and progressively dismantled the Jerusalem-based Jesus movement his brother James was leading. Tabor opens with a striking biographical possibility — Jerome's 4th-century tradition that Paul's family originated in **Gisela in Galilee**, just ~25 miles from Nazareth, and were exiled to Tarsus during the 4 BC revolts following Herod the Great's death. If true, Paul's family and Jesus's family lived as neighbors and were shaped by the same Roman crackdown — but in opposite directions. Jesus's people stayed and became revolutionary; Paul's people were forcibly exiled and may have learned to **accommodate Rome rather than resist it**. That accommodation shows up explicitly in Romans 13, where Paul tells believers to pay taxes and honor the Emperor as "God's agent for good" — the precise inverse of Jesus's "kingdom of God" preaching.

The bulk of the video is Tabor's compressed indictment of Paul's theological innovations: a pre-existent divine Christ "in the form of God"; substitutionary blood atonement; faith as the mechanism of justification rather than Torah observance; an entirely **heavenly orientation** with believers caught up in the clouds to meet Christ. Tabor frames this as a sharp break from the Jerusalem community — "the message that Jesus had preached became transformed for Paul into Jesus as the message." The Acts 21 confrontation with James in 58 CE is presented as the unresolved climax: James and the elders directly challenge Paul over reports he's teaching diaspora Jews to forsake Moses. Paul never gives a clean answer. We don't know whether the break was ever decisive — but Paul's letters, by being canonized and read for two millennia, won the literary war. **Paul's gospel became the only Christianity most Christians ever knew.**

## Key Teachings

### Paul's Galilean Origins (Jerome's Tradition)

Jerome, writing in the 4th century, preserves a tradition that contradicts Acts: Paul's parents were from **Gisela in Galilee**, ~25 miles north of Sepphoris. They were captured during the 4 BC post-Herod revolts and exiled to Tarsus in Cilicia along with other Galilean inhabitants. Tabor inclines toward Jerome's account precisely because Jerome had to know it contradicted Acts to repeat it — meaning he must have had good evidence.

If true: Paul's family and Jesus's family lived as **near-neighbors in Galilee** before the revolts. Same political earthquake. Opposite responses. Mary and Joseph moved to Nazareth and remained. Paul's family was deported. That trauma — Tabor speculates — may explain Paul's later Rome-accommodating posture. **Devastation taught accommodation.**

### Paul's Pro-Rome Posture vs. Jesus's Kingdom Preaching

Romans 13, written ~56 CE under Nero, tells Christians to pay taxes and honor Roman officials, including the Emperor, who Paul calls "God's agent for good." Tabor's verdict: **this is surely opposite from the revolutionary message that Jesus preached.**

For Paul, the kingdom of God became a **spiritual kingdom in heaven** — not on earth. He still expected an apocalyptic judgment, but counseled patient citizenship until Jesus appeared in the clouds to take his followers away. Jesus's earthly, restorationist kingdom became Paul's heavenly evacuation plan.

### Paul's Christology — Pre-Existent Divine Christ

Tabor compresses Paul's high Christology into its essentials:
- Jesus was a divine, pre-existent heavenly being, **firstborn of all creation**
- He existed in the form of God and was equal to God
- He emptied himself, took human form, was born of a woman
- His purpose was sinless living and atoning death
- God raised him and transformed him back to his glorious heavenly body
- Christ ascended and was seated at God's right hand

**God reconciled the sinful world — both Jews and Gentiles — to himself through faith in this Christ, not through good deeds.**

This pre-existent-divine-being framework is not in the Jerusalem community's preaching. It is Paul's contribution.

### The Heavenly Evacuation Eschatology

Paul expected he and most of his followers to live to see Christ return. The mechanics:
- The living and the dead would rise in the air
- They would meet Christ in the clouds
- They would sit in judgment over angels and humans
- They would share heavenly glory with Christ forever

**Paul's entire orientation was vertical and heavenly.** Earthly Jerusalem, Israel as a nation, the land — none of it figures in Paul's eschatology the way it does in the synoptic apocalypses or the Jerusalem community's hope.

### "My Gospel" — Jesus as Message, Not Messenger

Tabor's sharpest framing line: **"The message that Jesus had preached became transformed for Paul into Jesus as the message."**

Paul seldom mentions anything Jesus taught. He says little about Jesus's life. The death on the cross and the heavenly exaltation are everything. Paul calls this "my gospel" — and that possessive matters. It is *Paul's* gospel, distinct from what Jesus preached or what James and the Twelve continued to teach.

### The Torah as "Temporary Custodian"

By the early 50s CE, Paul was teaching in Galatians that the Torah given through Moses was a **temporary revelation now annulled by Christ**: "The Torah was our custodian until Christ came that we might be justified by faith; but now that faith has come we are no longer under a custodian."

Tabor's read: the implications are clear. **God's covenant with Israel has been nullified.** Being Jewish and following the commandments has become obsolete in Paul's framework.

This was the breaking point for the Jerusalem leaders. They didn't object to Paul welcoming Gentiles — the Nazarene movement already did that. What they objected to was Paul teaching **diaspora Jews** that they could forsake Moses, abandon circumcision, and live as Gentiles.

### The Acts 21 Confrontation (58 CE)

The book of Acts records Paul's visit to Jerusalem in 58 CE. James — "still clearly in charge" — and the elders confront Paul directly with the report that he is teaching Jews among the Gentiles to forsake Moses, not to circumcise their children, not to observe Jewish tradition.

Luke never records Paul's answer. Tabor's read: **Paul tacitly allowed James to assume he was still Torah-observant** — but Paul had already written the Corinthians that he saw himself under "a higher Torah, that of Christ" and would adapt to whatever circumstances he found himself in. Among Gentiles, he lived as a Gentile — which no Torah-observant Jew would ever do.

The confrontation is unresolved in the text. **We don't know whether Paul ever made a decisive break with James and the Jerusalem leaders, or they with him.**

### Paul's Literary Victory

Paul was beheaded in Rome under Nero, sometime before 68 CE. James was killed in Jerusalem in 62 CE. Jerusalem itself was destroyed in 70 CE.

The Jerusalem community **perished and was largely lost**. Paul's letters survived, were copied, were canonized, and eventually shaped the Gospels themselves. Tabor: **"Paul's triumph to some degree was a literary one"** — his letters and the influence of his ideas embedded in the New Testament writings became so persuasive that they came to constitute what was viewed as the only authentic Christianity.

Tabor's hopeful close: we *do* have sources from which we can recover the original message of James and the Twelve, and reasonably reconstruct the legacy of the **Jesus Dynasty**.
