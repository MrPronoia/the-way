## Key Teachings

### The Lecture Title — "Paul's Greatest Idea and How It Was Superseded"

This is the headline announcement. The lecture frames Paul as a thinker whose own gospel was overridden by the religion built in his name. The verb **superseded** is the operative move — not "lost," not "forgotten," but *replaced* by a competing framework that won the theological argument. The shift was not accidental; it was a deliberate displacement of Paul's Adam-Christology by the incarnation-Christology of later centuries that produced creedal orthodoxy.

### Paul's Lifetime View vs. Post-Death Reception

The compressed thesis: **during Paul's lifetime**, Jesus's exaltation was the *prototype*, not the *exception*. Paul's gospel was that the entire community of believers would undergo the same transformation Jesus underwent — dust beings becoming glorified spirit-beings, a new genus of divine sons. **After Paul's death**, this framework was inverted: Jesus alone was the exalted one, and the rest of humanity received forgiveness-of-sins rather than apotheosis. The cosmic-transformation gospel collapsed into a salvation-of-souls religion, and the foundation was laid for the later theological apparatus of Christ as God in a way Paul himself would not have recognized.

### Why This Talk Is "Challenging" — The Scholarly Context

The introducer flags Tabor's talk as one of four conference papers that "challenge what many of us have long thought" — meaning even within the Ehrman-conference scholarly audience, the Adam-Christology / cosmic-apotheosis reading of Paul is *not* the consensus. The introducer's framing is candid: "topics that most lay folk have not actually thought much about and that most scholars, I'd say, probably have not thought about enough." Tabor's lecture is positioned as one of the conference's intentionally disruptive contributions.
