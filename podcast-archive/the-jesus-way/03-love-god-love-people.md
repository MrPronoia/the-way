## Summary

This episode dives deep into what the hosts argue is the authentic gospel message of Jesus (Yeshua) versus what has become mainstream Protestant Christianity. Aaron and James contend that Christianity has drifted far from Jesus's actual teachings, instead centering on Paul's theology of "confess and believe" (Romans 10:9) while neglecting Jesus's emphasis on keeping the commandments, repentance, and the greatest commandment to love God and love neighbor.

The hosts establish Jesus's historical context as a first-century Jewish rabbi emerging from the Dead Sea Scroll community (the Ebionim/Nazarenes), who opposed the corrupt temple sacrifice system. They argue Jesus was crucified not primarily for claiming divinity, but for his opposition to the temple system when he cleansed the temple and condemned its practices.

The episode presents what they call the "three pillars" of Jesus's soteriology: (1) Repentance to enter the Kingdom, (2) Obedience to remain in the Kingdom, and (3) Love God/Love People to increase in the Kingdom. They contrast this with Pauline theology repeatedly, showing direct contradictions between Jesus and Paul on the law, salvation, and works. The hosts conclude that Christians must choose whose gospel to follow, and they unequivocally choose Jesus's teachings as sufficient for salvation.

## Key Teachings

### The Three Pillars of Jesus's Soteriology

1. **Repentance** - The Greek word "metanoia" means to change your mind/thinking. Turn your heart to God. This is how you ENTER the Kingdom. Jesus preached "repent for the Kingdom of Heaven is at hand" - meaning when you repent, you are in the Kingdom.

2. **Obedience to the Commandments** - This is how you STAY in the Kingdom. "If you love me, keep my commandments" (John). Jesus equates keeping the commandments directly to eternal life.

3. **The Greatest Commandment** - Love God with all your heart, love your neighbor as yourself. This is how you INCREASE in the Kingdom and achieve theosis (union with God). This one commandment fulfills all the law.

### The Historical Jesus vs. Theological Jesus

- Jesus emerged from the Dead Sea Scroll community (Ebionim = "the poor ones")
- The Nazarenes were a sect (Acts 24 - Paul accused of being "ringleader of the Nazarene sect")
- Jesus practiced communal living with voluntary poverty (Acts 2, 4)
- His primary mission was opposing the temple sacrifice system and reforming Judaism back to Mosaic law
- Jesus was crucified for cleansing the temple, not primarily for claiming divinity

### What "Keep the Commandments" Actually Means

- NOT the 600+ liturgical laws of Leviticus (man-made traditions)
- YES the Ten Commandments / spiritual laws
- The first commandments = Love God (have no other gods, don't take name in vain)
- The second commandments = Love People (don't kill, steal, covet, commit adultery)
- The law will never pass away (Matthew 5:18) because it's based on God's eternal nature

### Theosis (Eastern Orthodox Doctrine)

- Also called "divinization" or "deification"
- Participating in the divine nature by becoming more Christlike
- God is love (1 John 4:8), so when we love others, we come into direct contact with God's nature
- This is what Christianity SHOULD be focused on - becoming like Christ

### The Problem with "Perfect Behavior" Teaching

- The word "perfect" (Greek: teleos, Hebrew: tamim) does not mean flawless perfection
- It means "turning your heart to God" / complete devotion
- Abraham, Noah, Job, Samuel were all called tamim but were not sinless
- "Be perfect as your Father is perfect" appears in context of forgiveness - always forgive (70 x 7)
- God never asked for or expected perfect immaculate behavior from humanity

## Jewish Christianity Context

### The Ebionim (The Poor Ones)

- Self-designation of Jesus's community in Dead Sea Scrolls: "congregation of the Ebionim"
- Hebrew root word for "the poor" or "poor ones"
- This is why Jesus constantly blessed "the poor" - it was his community's identity
- The Sermon on the Mount language (meek, poor in spirit) reflects Ebionim identity

### The Nazarenes

- Jesus called "the Nazarene" - not just from Nazareth but a sectarian designation
- Acts 24: Paul accused of leading "the Nazarene sect"
- Early Christians called "followers of The Way"

### Temple Opposition

- The Nazarene/Ebionim community opposed the temple sacrifice system
- They practiced atonement baptism as alternative to animal sacrifice
- They only adhered to the Ten Commandments, rejecting the 600+ liturgical laws
- Jesus's temple cleansing was the direct cause of his crucifixion (Mark's Gospel)
- "Destroy this temple and I will rebuild it" - opposition to physical temple system

### Communal Living

- "Sell everything you possess and give to the poor" was invitation to JOIN the community
- Acts 2 & 4: They shared everything in common, nobody had unmet needs
- This was the lifestyle of early Jewish Christians

## Misconceptions Addressed

### Common Belief: Jesus came to teach us we CAN'T keep the commandments
**Actual Teaching:** Jesus never said following his commands was impossible. Deuteronomy 30:11 says "this commandment is not too hard for you." God wrote the law on our hearts. Jesus taught keep the commandments and you will enter eternal life.

### Common Belief: God requires perfect sinless behavior
**Actual Teaching:** "Be perfect as your Father is perfect" means perfect DEVOTION, not flawless behavior. The Greek (teleos) and Hebrew (tamim) mean completeness/wholeness, not sinlessness. God wants your whole heart, not perfection.

### Common Belief: You must confess Jesus as Lord to be saved (Romans 10:9)
**Actual Teaching:** Jesus never said this. He said "Not everyone who says Lord Lord will enter my kingdom." He asked "Why do you call me Lord Lord and not do what I say?" The emphasis was always on DOING, not confessing.

### Common Belief: We are saved by faith alone apart from works
**Actual Teaching:** Faith without works is dead (James). Jesus said you'll be judged according to your works (stated 4 times in NT). "Believe" (pisteuo) means to commit oneself to, trust, obey - not mere mental assent.

### Common Belief: Jesus's teachings were just for Jews under the Old Covenant
**Actual Teaching:** This is "dispensationalism" taken to extreme. If Jesus knew his teachings would become worthless after his death, why didn't he tell anyone? Why would he never mention confess/believe salvation?

### Common Belief: Good works are "earning salvation" (therefore bad)
**Actual Teaching:** Nobody teaches you can "earn" salvation. Works are the DEMONSTRATION of faith, not earning merit. Faith and works are inseparable like inhale and exhale.

### Common Belief: God can't look upon sin
**Actual Teaching:** Habakkuk 1:13 means God is "too pure to recognize" evil - God doesn't have a dualistic mind. God is omniscient and knew every sin you'd commit for eternity. God isn't angry or surprised.

### Common Belief: Without blood there is no forgiveness (Hebrews 9:22)
**Actual Teaching:** Jesus forgave the woman who washed his feet with her hair - "her sins which are many are forgiven because she loved much." No blood, no sacrifice. Love and repentance bring forgiveness.

## Connection to Other Research

### Jesus vs Paul Theme
This episode is ground zero for the Jesus vs Paul distinction. The hosts provide direct scriptural contradictions:
- Law eternal (Jesus) vs Law ended (Paul)
- Keep commandments for salvation (Jesus) vs Confess/believe (Paul)
- Forgiveness through love/repentance (Jesus) vs Blood sacrifice required (Paul)
- Works demonstrate faith (Jesus/James) vs Faith alone apart from works (Paul)

### Kingdom NOW vs Future
The hosts emphasize "repent for the Kingdom is at hand" - meaning the Kingdom is accessible NOW through repentance. This aligns with non-eschatological readings of Jesus's message.

### Temple Opposition
Jesus's primary mission was opposing the corrupt temple sacrifice system. His crucifixion resulted from the temple cleansing, not claims of divinity. This connects to understanding Jesus as a Jewish reformer rather than founder of a new religion.

### Near Death Experiences
The hosts cite NDEs as verification of Jesus's gospel over Paul's:
- Every NDE emphasizes love, service, forgiveness
- Christians who confessed Jesus but didn't live lovingly end up in dark realms
- Life review shows importance of how we treated others
- People are NOT judged by God but judge themselves

1. Jesus emerged from the Dead Sea Scroll community

3. Jesus's teachings differ fundamentally from Paul's
