# Episode 12: Why This Bible Scholar LEFT Christianity (w/ Dr. CJ Cornthwaite)

**Host:** Aaron Abke

## Summary

Dr. CJ Cornthwaite shares his journey from being a devout fundamentalist Christian, youth pastor, and aspiring biblical scholar to deconstructing his faith through rigorous academic study. His background in Plymouth Brethren fundamentalism, where he was preaching from the King James Bible at a young age, eventually led him to seminary where the "cracks in the armor" began. His PhD focused on Christian origins, specifically diaspora Judaism and Greco-Roman religions, giving him a unique scholarly perspective on how early Christianity developed.

The conversation explores the psychological and social costs of deconstruction, including the fear of losing community, livelihood, and relationships. CJ spent nearly 10 years as a youth pastor before his seminary studies led him to question fundamental beliefs about biblical inerrancy. A pivotal moment came on the shores of the Sea of Galilee when he witnessed evangelical tourists on a worship boat, oblivious to the pain and division around them, and realized he no longer wanted to be part of that world.

Both Aaron and CJ discuss how certainty becomes "a hell of a drug" and why fundamentalism creates communities where belonging is conditional on believing "the right things." They advocate for a more open, exploratory approach to spirituality that doesn't require checking your brain at the door, suggesting that the current mass exodus from fundamentalism represents an opportunity for Christianity to reinvent itself around the actual teachings of Jesus rather than fear-based dogma.

## Key Teachings

### The Psychology of Deconstruction
- **Cognitive dissonance** increases anger - the angrier someone is defending their beliefs, the more fragile those beliefs likely are
- **Certainty is addictive** - questioning certainties feels like giving up safety and comfort
- People often "self-select into quietness" because they know the social cost of speaking up about doubts
- The need to **belong** often overrides the need to be **honest** until a breaking point is reached

### Biblical Inerrancy as a Trap
- Making inerrancy foundational to Christianity makes the faith "falsifiable" - one contradiction destroys everything
- CJ: "If you're going to have to believe the Bible is inerrant to be a Christian, as soon as the Bible's not inerrant, you can't be a Christian anymore. And like that's dumb."
- The fundamentalist approach to education: "I already know the truth. I just need the paper."

### The Real Salvation Teachings of Jesus
- Jesus never told anyone to "confess and believe" for salvation
- When asked "How do I attain eternal life?" Jesus consistently answered: keep the commandments, love your neighbor
- The synoptic gospels emphasize: repentance, forgiveness, love, service to others
- "You could go to any Christian church on Sunday and ask how do I get to heaven, and you'll absolutely never hear a pastor say what Christ said when he was asked that question"

### The "Paganization" of Jesus
- Jesus's original message about repentance, humility, and loving your neighbor couldn't survive the Greco-Roman world without being "paganized"
- Justin Martyr defended Christian theology by comparing Jesus to Apollo and Perseus
- The orthodoxy we know developed between the 2nd-4th centuries and became "the oxygen" of Christianity

### Fear-Based Conversion
- "Every Christian conversion I've ever seen is you induce somebody into a fear state"
- The eternal hell threat is a "false dilemma" - no one would choose infinite torment
- "Do you really want a religion full of psychological captives?"
- "It's not salvation, it's a wedding proposal with a gun to the bride's temple"

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **Common Belief:** The Bible contains no contradictions
- **Actual Teaching:** Seminary education reveals multiple sources, different accounts (e.g., Judas's death, Genesis creation stories). As CJ says, "Today I don't care about contradictions - they don't matter to me. But to a fundamentalist, one contradiction destroys everything."

- **Common Belief:** Deconstruction is caused by people who "were never really Christians"
- **Actual Teaching:** CJ: "I was the world's best Christian for like 20 years." Most who deconstruct were the most devout.

- **Common Belief:** Pilate was relatively benevolent in Jesus's trial
- **Actual Teaching:** This Roman-friendly narrative in the Gospels is historically unlikely - it represents later editing to make Christianity palatable to Roman audiences.

- **Common Belief:** James the brother of Jesus and Peter led parallel Christian movements
- **Actual Teaching:** James was Jesus's appointed successor who led the Jerusalem church for almost three decades. The Catholic emphasis on Peter over James serves institutional purposes.

- **Common Belief:** Paul's theology represents authentic Christianity
- **Actual Teaching:** "Paul" includes layers of later interpretation. Paul used Essene terminology (sons of light/darkness) suggesting connections to Jewish sectarian groups, and there's evidence original letters were later expanded with orthodox additions.

- **Common Belief:** You must believe specific doctrines to be saved
- **Actual Teaching:** Jesus's actual answers to "how do I get eternal life" were about keeping commandments and loving your neighbor - never about confessing beliefs.
