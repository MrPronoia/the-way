## Summary

This Q&A episode tackles the most frequently asked question about The Jesus Way philosophy: "Do you believe in the resurrection?" Aaron and James articulate their position that the Jesus Way is based solely on Jesus's words and teachings, not on theological doctrines developed later by Paul and church fathers. They argue that Jesus never told anyone they must believe in his death and resurrection to be saved. Instead, Jesus taught that what forgives sins is love, repentance, and forgiving others.

The hosts take an epistemically humble position on the resurrection itself: "I don't know if Jesus resurrected. I wasn't there." They lean toward believing it probably happened because early disciples seemed to genuinely believe it, but they refuse to claim certainty about an event 2,000 years ago. More importantly, they argue that God would never make salvation dependent on believing in an unverifiable historical event rather than on the transformation of one's heart and character.

The episode also addresses John 14:6 ("I am the way, the truth, and the life"), the role of the Holy Spirit, reading the Bible with discernment, and whether the hosts pray to Jesus (yes, daily). Throughout, they emphasize that following Jesus means embodying his teachings on love, forgiveness, repentance, and non-judgment - not merely confessing beliefs about him.

## Key Teachings

### The Purpose of Jesus's Death
- Jesus was NOT incarnated to be "God's last animal sacrifice"
- Jesus's mission centered on abolishing the temple sacrifice system and the demonic practice of animal sacrifice
- The Hebrew Gospel of Matthew quotes Jesus: "I have come to abolish sacrifices and until you cease from sacrificing, the wrath of God will not cease from you"
- Jesus gave his life as a "ransom" to liberate the Jewish people and the animals from the oppressive temple system
- Jesus predicted his death because he knew opposing Rome via the temple system would get him killed
- Mark 11:18 - After Jesus cleansed the temple, "the priests sought how they would put him to death"

### Physical vs Spiritual Resurrection
- Official Jesus Way stance: "I don't know if Jesus resurrected. I wasn't there"
- They lean toward believing it happened because early disciples seemed to genuinely believe it
- There's attestation from both Jewish Christian and Gentile Christian sides
- The bigger point: God would never make salvation dependent on believing in an unverifiable event
- Even AFTER the resurrection, Jesus told disciples to "preach repentance for the forgiveness of sins" (Luke 24) - NOT to preach "confess and believe in my resurrection"
- Early Jewish Christian texts focus on the teachings and lifestyle of Jesus, with resurrection mentioned more in passing

### What Actually Saves According to Jesus
- Love (Luke 7:47)
- Repentance (Luke 24:46, Acts 2:38)
- Forgiving others (Luke 6:37, Matthew 6:14, Mark 11:25)
- Keeping the commandments - "If you want to enter eternal life, keep the commandments"
- Love God with all your heart, love your neighbor as yourself
- Jesus NEVER said "believe in my death and resurrection to be saved" - that's exclusively from Paul (Romans 10:9)

### The John 14:6 Question
- The Gospel of John is not a synoptic gospel - scholars consider it more theological narrative than historical account
- The "I Am" statements appear ONLY in John, not in Matthew, Mark, or Luke
- If Jesus said these things aloud in first-century Palestine, he would have been stoned immediately
- These statements come from the Christ principle/nature that incarnated into Jesus, not from a personal ego
- Jesus himself made the distinction between person and nature: "Don't call me good, only God is good"
- The Christ nature is in everyone - Jesus said "when you didn't love the least of these, you didn't love me"

### Christ Consciousness Defined
- Being conscious of Christ - "What would Jesus do?"
- Tapping into the part of you that is the Christ nature, the I Am principle
- The first-person present-tense consciousness is the godly principle in everybody
- Not a "hairy fairy new age idea" but practical imitation of Christ
- Paul got this right: "The same spirit that raised Christ Jesus from the dead dwells in me"

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **Common Belief:** Jesus came to be God's last animal sacrifice, dying for our sins
- **Actual Teaching:** Jesus came to ABOLISH the sacrifice system and show God doesn't require blood to forgive. Old Testament prophets (Isaiah, Jeremiah, Hosea, Amos, Micah) condemned animal sacrifice as an abomination God never wanted.

- **Common Belief:** You must confess Jesus as Lord and believe in his resurrection to be saved
- **Actual Teaching:** Jesus never said this. He said "if you want to enter eternal life, keep the commandments" - love God, love neighbor. This is exclusively Paul's theology (Romans 10:9).

- **Common Belief:** The Gospel of John accurately records what Jesus said
- **Actual Teaching:** John deviates wildly from the synoptic gospels. No "I Am" statements appear in Matthew, Mark, or Luke. Modern scholars don't consider John historically accurate in the same way.

- **Common Belief:** Satan is a literal being who tempts us
- **Actual Teaching:** "Satan" (Hebrew for "adversary") is a metaphor for the ego - the accuser, the liar. The ego is the open door through which all negative influences attach.

- **Common Belief:** The Bible is perfect and infallible like God
- **Actual Teaching:** That's "Bible worship" and idolatry. The Bible is a spiritual tool and pointer, not God. It must be read through the lens of God's immutable nature (the Five O's).

## The Five O's - Epistemological Framework

God's ontological nature is the standard for evaluating ALL scripture and theology:

1. **One** - Cannot be divided
2. **Omnipotent** - All power in the universe
3. **Omniscient** - All knowledge
4. **Omnipresent** - Everywhere at all times
5. **Omnibenevolent** - Always working toward life, health, wholeness, and flourishing of creation

If a scripture violates any of these, it's not divinely inspired. If it aligns with them, it can be trusted.
