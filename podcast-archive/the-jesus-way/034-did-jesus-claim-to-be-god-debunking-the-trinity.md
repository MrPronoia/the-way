## Key Teachings

### What is Unitarianism vs. Trinitarianism?

**Unitarianism:**
- Only the Father is ontologically God (God by nature/identity)
- The Father is the one whom scriptures give the name Yahweh
- The Holy Spirit is God's spirit, power, presence - an extension of the Father
- Jesus is the Son of God - a true human being who shares in divine prerogatives but is not God by ontology

**Trinitarianism:**
- God is one being in three persons (Father, Son, Holy Spirit)
- The persons are co-equal, co-eternal, and consubstantial
- The persons are "truly distinct" from each other yet "numerically one God"
- This is considered logically impossible since rejected explanations include:
  - Modalism (persons as modes of one God - like water as solid/liquid/gas)
  - Partialism (persons as parts of God - like three sides of a triangle)
  - Tritheism (three separate gods)

### The Law of Identity Problem

The Trinity violates basic logic:
- If the Father is God and the Son is God, by transitivity the Son would be the Father
- But Trinitarians deny this - they say the persons are "distinct not different"
- This is a denial of how we comprehend identity and relationships
- If the god the Father is differs from the god the Son is, you have two gods

### Historical Development Timeline

1. **Plato's Forms** - Perfect ideal models existing beyond space/time
2. **Aristotle** - Developed ousia (true being/essence) and hypostasis concepts
3. **Justin Martyr** (2nd century) - First to use ousia theologically for Christian God; called Jesus "another God and Lord subject to the maker of all things" - subordinationist, not Trinitarian
4. **Origen** (3rd century) - First to describe persons as hypostasis; explicitly said the Father is "superior to every being that exists" including the Son
5. **Tertullian** (3rd century) - First three-in-one formula and term "trinitas" but believed divine substance was corporeal (borrowed from Stoics)
6. **Council of Nicaea (325 CE)** - Their creed confused hypostasis and ousia in their anathema
7. **Council of Constantinople (381 CE)** - Updated Nicene Creed, added Holy Spirit
8. **Council of Chalcedon (451 CE)** - Defined hypostatic union (one person, two natures)

**Critical Point:** The ousia/hypostasis distinction (being vs. person) wasn't fully defined until end of 4th century. The earliest church fathers lacked the language to even articulate Trinitarian theology.

### The Hypostatic Union Contradictions

If Jesus is one person with two natures (fully God, fully man), he would possess contradictory attributes:

| Human Attribute | Divine Attribute | Contradiction |
|----------------|------------------|---------------|
| Was given all authority (Matt 28:18) | Already has all authority | Can't be given what you have |
| Subject to death | Cannot die (eternal) | Same person dying yet can't die |
| Didn't know day/hour (Mark 13:32) | Knows all things (omniscient) | Same person knowing yet not knowing |
| Was tempted by evil (Matt 4, Heb 4:15) | Cannot be tempted (James 1:13) | Same person temptable yet untemptable |

### Why This Matters

1. **Makes Jesus unrelatable** - If Jesus is ontologically God, his obedience isn't really obedience (disobedience must be possible for obedience to be meaningful). He can't be our example.

2. **Breaks the Adam-Christ parallel** - "By man came death, by man came resurrection" (1 Cor 15). The victory must answer the failure on equal terms. A God-man defeating sin doesn't redeem what a man lost.

3. **Replaces righteousness with dogma** - Instead of "you shall know them by their fruits," salvation becomes about believing correct metaphysics.

4. **Centralizes authority** - You can't understand God yourself; you need ecclesiastical authority to interpret these mysteries.

### The Biblical Use of Key Terms

- **Hypostasis** - Used 5 times in Bible. Hebrews 1:3 uses it for "nature" - the opposite of how Trinitarians use it for "person"
- **Ousia** - Used 2 times in Luke 15 meaning "property/estate" - no theological use in Bible

## Misconceptions Addressed

### "Mystery" as Defense
- Invoking "divine mystery" is a euphemism for contradiction
- No other Christian doctrine is verified through mystery
- Mystery cannot be falsified - it's losing the debate outright

### "There are no natural analogies for the Trinity"
- Jesus used natural theology constantly
- Modalism (water as solid/liquid/gas) has natural analogies but is rejected as heresy
- Father, mother, child has natural analogy but was masculinized out of existence

### "The Holy Spirit is a separate person"
- Human spirits aren't separate persons from us
- Why would God's spirit be a separate person?
- Matthew 10:20 explicitly calls it "the spirit of your Father"

### "Jesus claimed to be God"
- "I and the Father are one" = oneness of spirit, mission, purpose (same as Jesus prayed we would be one with him and the Father)
- Jesus distinguished himself from God repeatedly
- Claiming a man is God would have been the most blasphemous idea in Jewish monotheism

### "You must believe the Trinity to be saved"
- Jesus said eternal life is to know the Father as "the only true God" (John 17:3)
- Greatest commandment: Love God, love neighbor - no mention of correct metaphysics
- "You shall know them by their fruits" - not by their theology

### "The early church believed in the Trinity"
- Most ante-Nicene fathers were middle Platonists who believed Jesus was subordinate to the Father
- They lacked the philosophical vocabulary to even articulate Trinitarianism
- Many were later declared heretics (including Origen and Tertullian)

### "Genesis 1:26 'Let us' proves Trinity"
- Verse 27 switches to singular verbs ("God created")
- Over 20,000 times God is described with singular adjectives, suffixes, verbs
- Better explanations: divine council (Psalm 82) or plural of majesty
