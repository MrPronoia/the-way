## Summary

This episode features Patrick Durkin, known online as Boston2% and Boston Born Again, sharing his testimony of deliverance from 25 years of meth addiction, homelessness, incarceration, and demonic possession. Patrick's story illustrates how addiction serves as a gateway to the demonic realm, and how Christ met him at his absolute lowest point to bring transformation. The hosts explore the spiritual warfare dimension of addiction, the reality of demonic attachment, and how Jesus's gospel was always oriented toward "the least of these."

- Addiction as an ego weapon and gateway to demonic attachment

- The prodigal son journey of returning to God from the depths
- True repentance (metanoia) as turning around, not just feeling guilty
- The difference between intellectual belief and committed trust (pistuo)
- God's willingness to meet people in their filthiest, most broken state

## Misconceptions Addressed

1. **"Easy believism" saves**: The hosts and Patrick strongly challenge the idea that mental assent to Christian concepts equals salvation. Jesus's gospel requires repentance, transformation, and obedience.

2. **Addiction is purely physical/psychological**: The episode presents addiction as having a significant spiritual dimension - as a gateway to demonic attachment and spiritual bondage.

3. **You must be "clean" before coming to God**: Patrick encountered the Holy Spirit while still actively using, sitting on a filthy mattress in a trap house. "You do not have to be perfect, but you need to turn your heart to God."

4. **Meth psychosis is just hallucination**: Patrick describes supernatural experiences that manifested in the physical world (electrical sparks, stove needing to be rewired) witnessed by multiple people.

5. **The worst sinners are beyond redemption**: Patrick's story - from armed robbery facing 156 years in prison to best-selling author and recovery advocate - demonstrates there is no sin too great for God's redemption.

6. **Christianity is mainly about beliefs**: The hosts emphasize Jesus's teaching was about how you live, love, and treat others, not intellectual doctrines.
