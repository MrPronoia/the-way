### 1. James Presided Over the Jerusalem Church -- Paul Did Not

The New Testament itself preserves the authority structure:

| Reference | Evidence |
|-----------|----------|
| **Acts 15:13-19** | At the Jerusalem Council, James renders the final decision. Peter and Paul present arguments; James rules. |
| **Galatians 2:9** | Paul calls James, Peter, and John "pillars" -- with James listed first (ancient convention = highest rank). |
| **Galatians 2:12** | "Before certain men came from James, [Peter] used to eat with the Gentiles. But when they arrived, he began to draw back." Peter's behavior changed at James's word -- not Paul's. |
| **Gospel of Thomas, Saying 12** | "Wherever you go, go to James the Just, for whom heaven and earth came to be." Jesus designates James as successor. |

### 2. James Confronted Paul with Apostasy (Acts 21:21)

The most explosive passage. When Paul arrives in Jerusalem, James and the elders tell him:

> "You see, brother, how many thousands there are among the Jews of those who have believed. They are all zealous for the law, and **they have been told about you that you teach all the Jews who are among the Gentiles to forsake [apostasia] Moses**, telling them not to circumcise their children or observe the customs."
> -- Acts 21:20-21

The Greek word is **apostasia** -- literally apostasy. James's own community is accusing Paul of leading people away from the Torah. James doesn't defend Paul. He tells Paul to prove the charges wrong by performing a Temple purification ritual (Acts 21:23-24). This is a leader managing damage from a subordinate who has gone off-script.

### 3. The Epistle of James Directly Rebuts Paul

| James | Paul | Contradiction |
|-------|------|---------------|
| "A person is justified by works and not by faith alone" (James 2:24) | "A person is justified by faith apart from works of the law" (Romans 3:28) | Direct, word-for-word opposition |
| "Faith without works is dead" (James 2:26) | "By grace you have been saved through faith... not by works" (Ephesians 2:8-9) | Same vocabulary, opposite conclusions |
| "Was not Abraham our father justified by works when he offered up Isaac?" (James 2:21) | "If Abraham was justified by works, he has something to boast about -- but not before God" (Romans 4:2) | Both use Abraham as their example, reach opposite verdicts |

Martin Luther recognized this. He called the Epistle of James "an epistle of straw" and wanted it removed from the canon precisely because it contradicts Paul. Luther's honesty here is revealing -- the contradiction is real, not invented by modern critics.

### 4. The Incident at Antioch (Galatians 2:11-14)

Paul's own account of open conflict with Peter -- triggered by James:

1. Peter was eating with Gentile converts in Antioch
2. "Certain men came from James" (James's representatives arrived)
3. Peter immediately withdrew from the Gentile table
4. Paul "opposed Peter to his face" and called him a hypocrite

Paul frames this as Peter being weak. From James's perspective, Peter was complying with the agreed-upon dietary rules from the Jerusalem Council (Acts 15:20). Scholar James D.G. Dunn calls Peter a "bridge-man" caught between James and Paul. L. Michael White argues this was "a total failure" for Paul -- leaving him "persona non grata" in the Jerusalem church.

### 5. Paul's Own Admissions of Tension

| Reference | Paul's Words | What It Reveals |
|-----------|-------------|-----------------|
| **Galatians 2:6** | "As for those who were held in high esteem -- whatever they were makes no difference to me... they added nothing to my preaching." | Paul dismisses the original apostles' authority. "Whatever they were makes no difference" is a remarkable statement about the men Jesus personally trained. |
| **Galatians 1:11-12** | "The gospel I preached is not of human origin. I did not receive it from any man." | Paul explicitly bypassed the apostolic chain of custody. His gospel came from a private vision, not from Jesus's chosen leaders. |
| **2 Corinthians 11:5** | "I do not think I am in the least inferior to those 'super-apostles.'" | The sarcastic "super-apostles" almost certainly refers to James and the Jerusalem leadership. Paul is positioning himself against them. |

### 6. The Ebionite Witness

The Ebionites -- the direct descendants of the Jerusalem church led by James -- universally rejected Paul:

- **Irenaeus (c. 180 CE):** "The Ebionites... repudiate the apostle Paul, maintaining that he was an apostate from the Law." (*Against Heresies* 1.26.2)
- **Eusebius (c. 325 CE):** The Ebionites "reject all the epistles of the apostle [Paul]... whom they called an apostate from the Law." (*Church History* 3.27)
- **Clementine Literature:** Paul is coded as "the enemy" who attacks James on the temple steps, and as "Simon Magus" opposing Peter in theological debate.

The Ebionites were not a random heretical sect. They were the continuation of James's Jerusalem community after the flight to Pella in 66 CE. Their rejection of Paul is the community memory of the people who actually knew James.
