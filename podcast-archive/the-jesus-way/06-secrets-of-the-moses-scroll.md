## Summary

This episode features Hebrew scholar Ross K. Nichols discussing the explosive implications of "The Moses Scroll" (also called the Shapira Scroll), an ancient document discovered in 1865 in a Bedouin cave near Qumran in the Trans-Jordan region. The scroll appears to be an early version of Deuteronomy written in Paleo-Hebrew, but with a crucial difference: it contains no mention of animal sacrifices or Levitical ritual laws. Chapters 12-26 of our current Deuteronomy (the bulk of the "law code") are completely missing from this text.

Ross explains how he came to study this document after questioning what Moses actually wrote, prompted by Deuteronomy 31:9 and 31:24-26 which describe Moses finishing "the Torah" and giving it to the Levites - yet three chapters remain in our current Torah after this point. This led him on a quest to understand authorship and the "tensions" within the biblical text regarding sacrifices. The scroll was brought to public attention by Moses Shapira in 1878, dismissed as a forgery by 19th-century scholars, but has been vindicated by subsequent Dead Sea Scroll discoveries which match its unique features.

The hosts and Ross connect this text to the Essenes, Nazarenes, and Ebionites - Jewish groups who all rejected animal sacrifice and believed Moses never commanded such practices. They trace a "golden thread" from these ancient groups through Jesus's ministry (including his temple cleansing and anti-sacrifice teachings) to the Jewish Christians who fled to the Trans-Jordan region before Jerusalem's destruction in 70 CE. The episode also covers the mysterious circumstances surrounding Shapira's death and the scroll's disappearance.

## Key Teachings

### The Documentary Hypothesis
The Pentateuch (first five books) was not written by one person named Moses, but represents a collection of sources (J, E, P, D) compiled over centuries. The Moses Scroll may actually predate all four sources, serving as a foundational text upon which sacrificial elements were later added.

### Evidence the Torah Has Multiple Authors
- Deuteronomy 34 describes Moses's death and burial (written in third person)
- Numbers states Moses was "the meekest man on earth" (unlikely self-description)
- Exodus 16:35 says Israel ate manna for 40 years until entering Canaan (Moses never entered)
- Two different creation accounts with contradictory details
- The divine name (YHWH) revealed at different times in different sources

### The Moses Scroll's Key Features
- Written in Paleo-Hebrew (ancient script)
- Uses Scriptio Continua (continuous script with no word breaks)
- Contains inter-puncts between words in the Ten Commandments only
- Missing Deuteronomy chapters 12-26 (the sacrificial law code)
- Uses "Elohim" throughout the main body, with YHWH only in opening/closing lines
- Found in two nearly identical copies (42 columns total)

### Post-Dead Sea Scroll Vindication
Every feature that 19th-century scholars cited as proof of forgery has been vindicated by Dead Sea Scroll discoveries:
- Leather manuscripts surviving in caves
- Paleo-Hebrew on leather (12 manuscripts found at Qumran)
- Scriptio continua in Paleo manuscripts
- Inter-puncts in ancient texts
- Linen wrapping with bitumen/asphalt coating
- Folded format rather than scrolled

### The Trans-Jordan Connection
A geographical pattern connects anti-sacrifice groups:
- Joshua 22: Tribes of Reuben, Gad, and half-Manasseh build an altar "not for sacrifice"
- John the Baptist performing baptism (not sacrifice) for remission of sins
- Dead Sea Scroll communities near Qumran
- The Moses Scroll discovered in Trans-Jordan caves
- Nazarenes/Ebionites documented by Epiphanius as living in Trans-Jordan
- Jewish Christians fleeing to Pella/Berea before 70 CE (per Hegesippus)

### The 10th Commandment Addition
The Moses Scroll contains commandments that expand the heart dimension:
- "Do not hate your brother in your heart" (corresponding to murder)
- Internal prohibition against lusting after neighbor's wife (corresponding to adultery)
This matches Jesus's teachings in the Sermon on the Mount about anger and lust.

### Love God, Love Neighbor as Summary
The first blessing in the Moses Scroll says "Blessed is the man who loves Elohim"
The last blessing says "Blessed is the man who loves his neighbor"
This perfectly matches Jesus's summary of the law: "On these two commandments hang all the law and the prophets."

## The Shapira Scroll Mystery

**Discovery (1865):** Bedouins fleeing enemies found leather strips in a cave near Wadi Mujib (Trans-Jordan), thought it was gold.

**Shapira's Acquisition (1878):** Moses Shapira, a well-known antiquarian in Jerusalem, purchased the fragments and recognized them as an ancient version of Deuteronomy.

**European Tour (1883):** Shapira brought 16 leather strips to Europe seeking authentication and sale (asking price: ~1 million British pounds, equivalent to ~$120 million today).

**Examination:** Christian David Ginsburg spent three weeks examining and publishing the text in London newspapers. Initial excitement turned to rejection by August 23, 1883.

**The Rejection:** French, German, and English scholars declared it a forgery based on features they'd never seen before (leather in caves, Paleo-Hebrew on leather, continuous script, bitumen coating).

**Suspicious Death (March 1884):** Shapira found dead in Rotterdam hotel room. Police reports conflict: Was he on bed or floor? One report mentions two bullet holes in his head. Was it suicide or murder?

**Masonic Connections:** Every early examiner in London was a Freemason. Walter Besant was a founding member of a major Masonic research organization. The last known display of the scroll was at a Masonic lodge meeting.

**Disappearance:** After Shapira's death, his widow requested the scroll's return. It was sold at Sotheby's auction for pennies on the dollar. Philip Brooks Mason became last known possessor.

**Current Search:** Ross and Cameron are investigating:
- Germany: One strip sent to a German scholar
- Burton-on-Trent: Local researcher David pursuing leads about Mason's estate
- Eyewitness testimony: An 80-year-old man claims to have seen the strips at Mason's estate sale in the 1970s
- Mason's grave: Mysterious inscription beginning with "Never lost..."
