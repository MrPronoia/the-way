## Summary

This episode features biblical scholar Dr. James Tabor discussing his new book "The Lost Mary: Rediscovering the Mother of Jesus." The central thesis is that while Mary is the best-known woman in history, she is paradoxically the "least known" - having been stripped of her Jewish identity, her motherhood of Jesus's siblings, her sexuality, her culture, and her agency by later Christian theology.

Dr. Tabor argues that the perpetual virginity doctrine served two purposes: (1) the Neoplatonic/Hellenistic dualism that denigrated the body and sexuality, and (2) the deliberate erasure of James the Just, Jesus's brother, who led the movement for nearly 30 years after the crucifixion. If Mary had other children, James exists as Jesus's successor - a dynastic "caliphate" that threatened later Pauline Christianity's authority structure.

The episode explores Mary's historical context: she was likely born in Sepphoris (the urban capital of Galilee), not Nazareth, and witnessed the burning of that city and crucifixion of 2,000 revolutionaries when Jesus was an infant. She raised a distinguished Davidic family that worked in the building trades, celebrated Jewish festivals, and operated within a literate religious culture.

Most provocatively, Dr. Tabor argues that Luke's genealogy (going through Nathan, not Solomon) is actually Mary's lineage, not Joseph's - and that this genealogy reveals both Davidic AND priestly ancestry, explaining how James could have entered the holy areas of the Temple. The episode closes with Dr. Tabor's thesis that Mary herself may have been the wellspring of Jesus's revolutionary teachings, not merely a passive vessel.

## Key Teachings

### "Best Known, Least Known Woman in History"
Mary is lost not because she's forgotten, but because what's remembered about her is theological construction rather than historical reality. She's been stripped of:
- Her Jewish faith (Shabbat, festivals, Torah observance)
- Her children (perpetual virginity doctrine)
- Her sexuality and marriage to Joseph
- Her cultural context (Roman-occupied Palestine)
- Her humanity (later debates about whether she even went to the toilet)

### The Perpetual Virginity as Double Erasure
Two forces drove the perpetual virginity doctrine:
1. **Neoplatonic Hellenistic dualism** - denigrating the body, sexuality, and material world; the celibate life as the holy life
2. **Erasure of James** - if Mary had other children, James's authority as Jesus's successor is established; removing her children removes the Davidic dynasty's continued leadership

### Mary's Historical Context: Sepphoris, Not Nazareth
- Mary was likely born in Sepphoris (Tzippori in Hebrew), the "jewel of the Galilee"
- Sepphoris was the urban capital with Herod Antipas's royal palace, not a backwater village
- Nazareth was a small suburb just 4 miles south
- Jesus likely worked as a builder (tecton, not just carpenter) in Sepphoris
- In 4 BC, when Jesus was an infant, Sepphoris was burned and 2,000 people crucified - Mary witnessed this nursing her Davidic child

### Mary's Davidic AND Priestly Lineage
Dr. Tabor argues Luke's genealogy is Mary's (not Joseph's):
- Goes through Nathan (David's son), not Solomon
- Contains priestly names: Levi, Matat/Matthew (6-8 forms)
- Matthew's genealogy (Joseph's line) contains no priestly names
- Eastern Orthodox Christianity preserved knowledge of Jesus's dual priestly-Davidic lineage
- This explains how James could wear the priestly miter and enter holy areas of the Temple

### James as Jesus's Successor (The Caliphate)
- Gospel of Thomas (Saying 12): "Wherever you go, go to James the Just, for whom heaven and earth came to be"
- Acts 15: James stands up and declares "My decision is..." - showing his authority over Peter and Paul
- Paul in Galatians calls James "the pillar of the church, the head, the brother of the Lord"
- James led the movement for nearly 30 years - longer than Jesus's ministry
- At the Vatican: Peter and Paul everywhere, no James in sight

### The Melchizedek Passage Reread
Dr. Tabor offers a new reading of Psalm 110:4:
- Traditional: "You are a priest forever after the order of Melchizedek"
- Hebrew: "You are a priest forever according to my word, O righteous king"
- The hyphen making "Melchizedek" a name was added later - scrolls don't have hyphens
- 2 Samuel 8:18: "David's sons were priests" - showing Davidic figures could function as priests
- This explains James's priestly role and the dual lineage

### Mary as the Wellspring of Jesus's Teaching
Dr. Tabor's provocative thesis in his final chapter:
- "The hand that rocks the cradle rules the world"
- If the revolutionary teachings appear in John the Baptizer, Jesus, AND James, the common thread is Mary and the family tradition
- Historical Jesus scholarship's failure to consider Mary as source reflects 19th-century misogyny in the academy
- Mary moved with Jesus to Capernaum with his brothers - she was part of the movement, not left behind in Nazareth
- Acts 1: After crucifixion, the apostles gather with "his mother Mary and brothers"

## Misconceptions Addressed

### "Mary Lived Quietly as a Catholic Nun"
Mary was a Jewish mother who raised a large family (5+ sons, multiple daughters), celebrated Jewish festivals, prepared Shabbat meals, and was likely literate. She was not a celibate, passive figure but the matriarch of a distinguished Davidic household.

### "Jesus Grew Up in a Backwater Village"
Nazareth was a suburb 4 miles from Sepphoris, the urban capital of Galilee with Herod's palace. Jesus likely worked as a builder (tecton) in this cosmopolitan city, walking there daily. He grew up seeing "the city set on a hill that cannot be hid."

### "The Family Was Poor Illiterate Peasants"
The family was in the building trades (not just carpentry) - a business. Jewish homes were literate homes because Torah was read and studied. Jesus debated with "doctors of the law" at age 12. Rabbis record Jesus making sophisticated halakhic arguments.

### "The Brothers Were 'Cousins' or 'Stepbrothers'"
This is theological invention to preserve perpetual virginity. Paul explicitly calls James "the brother of the Lord." The brothers must be Mary's children to inherit Davidic lineage. Matthew 1:25's "until" makes clear Joseph and Mary had normal relations after Jesus's birth.

### "James Just Happened to Become a Leader"
James was designated by Jesus as successor (Gospel of Thomas), led the movement for ~30 years, was called "James the Just," and was so holy he was numbered among the "Lammed Vavnik" (36 righteous who sustain the world). His leadership was dynastic, not accidental.

### "Luke Made Up His Genealogy"
Luke had sources - the Q source, travel diaries ("we" sections), and records. Royal lineage records were kept at Sepphoris. Roman emperors (Vespasian, Hadrian) actively hunted Davidic descendants using these records. The genealogy reflects real family history.
