## Summary

This episode explores the explosive discovery of "The Moses Scroll" - an ancient copy of Deuteronomy that predates any other version we have and contains NO animal sacrifices or ritual purity laws. The hosts examine how this discovery validates what the prophets (Jeremiah, Isaiah, Hosea, Amos) and early Jewish Christians (Essenes, Ebionites, Nazarenes) consistently claimed: that animal sacrifices were never commanded by God but were added later by "the lying pens of the scribes."

## Key Teachings

### The Moses Scroll Discovery

The Moses Scroll was found in the 1800s by Moses Shapira, an antiquities dealer in Jerusalem. Key characteristics:
- Written in paleo Hebrew (older form predating standard Hebrew)
- Contains interpunks (punctuation marks between words, especially on the Ten Commandments)
- Wrapped in leather and linen
- Found in a cave near Moab (same area where 2 Maccabees says Jeremiah hid the Ark and the Law)
- Dated to approximately 3rd century BCE - older than any other Deuteronomy copy

British scholars rejected it as a forgery in the 1880s, claiming they'd "never seen a document like this." Shapira was publicly humiliated, his career destroyed, and he was found dead by apparent suicide in Rotterdam. However, the Dead Sea Scrolls (found 1947) vindicated him - 13 documents with identical characteristics (paleo Hebrew, leather parchment, interpunks, wrapped in linen).

### What the Moses Scroll Contains (and Omits)

**Missing from the Moses Scroll:**
- All animal sacrifice instructions
- Chapters 12-26 of Deuteronomy (the "cult laws" or "law code")
- The 600+ ritual purity laws

**Present in the Moses Scroll:**
- The Ten Commandments (expanded versions matching Jesus's teachings)
- Smooth flow from Chapter 11 directly to blessings and curses
- BOTH blessings AND curses (current Deuteronomy only has curses in that section)
- "Do not hate your brother in your heart" (matches Matthew 5 - Jesus saying "don't even hate")
- Expanded adultery teaching about the mind/heart (matches Jesus's "if you lust, you've already committed adultery")

### The Second Kings Discovery

In 2 Kings, during King Josiah's reign, the priest Hilkiah (likely Jeremiah's father) found a "law of Moses." Key details:
- The priests read it from morning to midday (~6 hours) - too short to be current Pentateuch
- King Josiah tore his clothes (reserved for greatest heresies/grievances)
- They performed a Passover "like never before" - implying it was NEW, not just bigger
- Shortly after, Jeremiah begins his fierce condemnation of sacrifices

### Jesus's Temple Cleansing Reframed

The temple cleansing was not spontaneous but a coordinated protest:
- Jesus and disciples did "recon" the night before (Mark)
- Disciples blocked doorways, preventing animals from being brought IN
- Jesus freed the animals already inside (goats, rams, pigeons)
- Happened during Passover week - peak business time
- Combined Isaiah 56:7 ("house of prayer for all nations") with Jeremiah 7:11 ("den of thieves/murderers")
- Mark explicitly states: "Upon hearing this, the priests began to look for a way to kill him"

### The Missing Commandment

The Moses Scroll reveals expanded Ten Commandments that match Jesus's Sermon on the Mount:
- Not just "do not kill" but "do not hate your brother in your heart"
- Not just "do not commit adultery" but addressing adultery of the mind
- Jesus wasn't creating NEW teachings - he was quoting an ORIGINAL law the scribes had shortened

## Jewish Christianity Context

### The Ebionites
- Name means "the poor ones" (Hebrew) - "Blessed are the poor"
- Followers of James the Just (Jesus's blood brother)
- First-century Jerusalem church was called "the congregation of the poor"
- Rejected Paul as false apostle who taught differently than Jesus
- Strict vegetarians
- Opposed all animal sacrifice
- Believed scriptures were changed by lying scribes
- Read the Gospel of the Hebrews (banned by Rome)

### The Nazarenes
- Jesus called "Jesus the Nazarene"
- Name possibly means "the keepers" (of the law) or "branch" (Isaiah 11)
- Practiced Judaism in every way EXCEPT sacrifices
- "They believe Moses received some legislation, but not this legislation"
- Based across the Jordan in same area where Moses Scroll was found
- Vegetarian lifestyle as opposition to temple system

### The Essenes
- Proto-Christian mystical Jews
- Did not practice slavery (unlike Pharisees/Sadducees)
- Vegetarian - documented by multiple first-century historians
- Focused on righteousness, inner purity, meditation, prayer
- Likely overlapped significantly with early Jesus movement
- Based near Qumran (Dead Sea Scrolls community)

### Common Beliefs Across Groups
- Ten Commandments are the true law of Moses
- 600+ ritual laws were added by corrupt scribes
- Animal sacrifice was never commanded by God
- Vegetarianism as protest against temple system
- Baptism as replacement for animal sacrifice (water vs. blood)
- Jesus as "the true prophet" foretold by Moses (Deuteronomy 18:15)
- Following the "spiritual laws" not ritual laws

- **Common Belief:** Jesus was okay with sacrifices as part of the Old Covenant
- **Actual Teaching:** Jesus actively opposed sacrifices, coordinated the temple cleansing as a direct attack on the system, and was crucified specifically for this opposition (Mark explicitly states priests sought to kill him "upon hearing this")

- **Common Belief:** Moses wrote Deuteronomy with all the sacrifice laws

- **Common Belief:** The Old Testament prophets accepted sacrifices as God's will
- **Actual Teaching:** Isaiah, Jeremiah, Hosea, Amos consistently condemned sacrifices as never commanded by God

- **Common Belief:** Gehenna/Hell is an afterlife concept

- **Common Belief:** Early Christians ate meat normally

- **Common Belief:** Jesus multiplied fish and loaves

- **Common Belief:** Paul's letters represent Jesus's teachings
