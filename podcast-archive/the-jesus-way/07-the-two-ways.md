## Summary

This episode features Chris Sartain, who pastors the "Essene Ebionite Church of Christian Mysticism" in southern Chile, bringing expertise on early Jewish Christianity. The panel discusses "The Two Ways" - the oldest Jewish Christian theology that can be identified in the textual tradition. This doctrine, found in the Didache, Dead Sea Scrolls, and other early texts, presents the fundamental choice between the "way of life" and the "way of death" - a teaching that predates Paul and represents Christianity in its original, uncorrupted form.

## Key Teachings

### The Two Ways Doctrine
The oldest identifiable Jewish Christian theology - a fundamental binary choice between:
- **The Way of Life:** Love of God, love of neighbor, repentance, forgiveness, baptism, doing no harm
- **The Way of Death:** Sin, separation from God, violence, bloodshed, sacrifice

This doctrine appears consistently across all early Jewish Christian texts and predates Pauline Christianity.

### First Century Jewish Christian Texts
Chris Sartain identifies the four most important first-century Jewish Christian texts:
1. **Q Source** - The sayings source used by Matthew and Luke; Jesus's earliest, most authentic wisdom teachings
2. **Epistle of James** - Written by Jesus's brother, represents the earliest Jerusalem Church tradition
3. **The Didache** - Contains the Two Ways material; used as a catechism before baptism
4. **Gospel of Thomas** - Non-dual mystical teachings; more primitive versions of Q sayings; exalts James

Additional important texts: Hebrew Gospel of Matthew (anti-sacrifice), Revelation (anti-Pauline), Epistle of Barnabas (Two Ways teaching)

### The Essene-Christian Connection
The hosts present evidence that Jesus's movement emerged from the Essene tradition:
- Same distinctive practices: vegetarianism, anti-sacrifice, communalism, ritual baptism
- Same terminology: "the poor ones," "the true prophet," "the two ways"
- Archaeological evidence at Mount Arbel shows an Essene community near Galilee with Mikvah pools
- James described as "vegetarian from his mother's womb, never ate flesh, only wore white linen" - classic Essene markers

### Baptism vs. Blood Atonement
The central argument: Baptism had ALREADY replaced animal sacrifice before Jesus. There was no theological need for a human sacrifice because:
- The Essenes practiced baptism for forgiveness of sins
- John the Baptist preached "baptism for the remission of sins"
- Jesus and his disciples baptized
- The Community Rule explicitly states atonement without "flesh of holocausts and fat of sacrifices"

## The Two Ways Doctrine

### Textual Evidence Across Multiple Sources

**The Didache (1st Century)**
> "There are two ways, one of life and one of death, and there is a great difference between the two ways. The way of life is this: First of all, you shall love the God who made you. Second, love your neighbor as yourself. And all things you would not want done to you, do not do to another person."

**Dead Sea Scrolls - 4Q473 "The Two Ways"**
> "...two ways, one good and one evil. If you walk on the good way, he will bless you. If you walk on the evil way, he will bring upon you destruction."

**Dead Sea Scrolls - 1QS Community Rule**
> "He has created man to govern the world and has appointed for him two spirits in which to walk until the time of visitation: the spirits of Truth and Falsehood."

**Epistle of Barnabas 18**
> "There are two ways of teaching, one of light and one of darkness, and there is a great difference between these two ways."

**Deuteronomy 30:15, 19**
> "See, I have set before you today life and good, death and evil... I have set before you life and death, blessing and curse - therefore choose life."

### The Historical Thread
Chris Sartain traces a continuous lineage:
1. **Pre-Qumran Essenes** (described by Josephus/Philo) - vegetarian, anti-sacrifice, communalist, baptizers
2. **Cave 4 Community Rule** - earliest version, no Zadokite priesthood mention, contains Two Ways
3. **The Nazarenes/Nazareans** - Northern Essene group (possibly at Mount Arbel)
4. **Jesus and his disciples** - emerge from this tradition
5. **Didache community** - uses Community Rule as source, same theology
6. **Ebionites** - later name for Jerusalem Church after flight to Pella

The key insight: The Community Rule from Cave 4 (without Zadokite redactions) is anti-sacrifice and contains the Two Ways material that later appears in the Didache. This proves textual continuity across centuries.

## Jewish Christianity Context

### The Nazarenes
- Northern Essene group based around Galilee (possibly Mount Arbel)
- Distinguished from Qumran Essenes by accepting marriage
- Jesus, John the Baptist, and James likely emerged from this tradition
- Described by Josephus/Philo: vegetarian, pacifist, communalist, healers, wore white linen

### The Ebionites
- Name means "the poor ones" (Hebrew: Evionim)
- Same term found in Dead Sea Scrolls: "congregation of the Evionim"
- Jerome called the Jerusalem Church "the holy poor"
- Continued as Jewish Christians after the fall of Jerusalem (70 CE)
- Fled to Pella in trans-Jordan region

### The Essenes (Two Groups)
1. **Qumran Essenes** (Zadokite priesthood) - Some differences from Philo/Josephus descriptions
2. **Philo's Essenes** (Nazarenes) - Vegetarian, anti-sacrifice, communalist, celibate group (Ossaeans to the South) vs. marriage-accepting group (Nazarenes to the North)

### The Didache
- Full title: "The Teaching of the Twelve Apostles"
- First 6 chapters contain the Two Ways material
- Read to converts before baptism - choosing the way of life
- Nearly made it into the biblical canon
- Used Community Rule as source (per Jonathan Draper)
- Athanasius of Alexandria recommended it to converts

### Mount Arbel Archaeological Evidence
- Cave complex overlooking Sea of Galilee
- Mikvah pools (ritual baptismal baths) found inside caves
- Dining hall and kitchen discovered
- Evidence of flax cultivation (for linen garments)
- Possibly the headquarters of the Nazarene movement
- Pre-existed Jesus's lifetime; may have been where he interacted with Essenes

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **Common Belief:** Christianity began as one unified tradition from Jesus through the apostles to the Catholic Church
- **Actual Teaching:** Early Christianity was extremely diverse; Jewish Christianity (Nazarenes, Ebionites) was a separate and older tradition than Gentile/Pauline Christianity

- **Common Belief:** Peter founded the Roman Church and wrote the epistles bearing his name
- **Actual Teaching:** Acts 4:13 calls Peter and John "agrammatos" (illiterate); no historical evidence Peter went to Rome; the Petrine epistles are pseudonymous

- **Common Belief:** Blood atonement through Jesus's death is the core Christian teaching
- **Actual Teaching:** Baptism had already replaced animal sacrifice in the Essene/Jewish Christian tradition; Jesus taught repentance for forgiveness

- **Common Belief:** The Gospel of Thomas is a Gnostic text
- **Actual Teaching:** Thomas has more in common with Jewish Christian texts (exalts James, emphasizes "the poor," non-dual but not Gnostic); found at Nag Hammadi but also at Oxyrhynchus in Greek

- **Common Belief:** Faith alone saves (sola fide)
- **Actual Teaching:** James 2:24 - "A person is justified by works, not by faith alone." Works demonstrate genuine faith; belief in the brain is meaningless without action.

- **Common Belief:** "Believe" in Greek means intellectual assent
- **Actual Teaching:** Greek "pisteuo" means to trust, commit oneself to, obey, follow - not merely mental agreement

## Core Takeaway

The Two Ways doctrine - the choice between life and death - represents the oldest recoverable Christian theology. It appears in the Dead Sea Scrolls, the Didache, the Epistle of Barnabas, Deuteronomy, and Jesus's own teachings. This doctrine teaches that salvation comes through choosing the path of life: loving God, loving neighbor, repentance, forgiveness, and right action.

Pauline Christianity inverted this by making salvation about correct belief and blood atonement - literally centering the faith on death rather than life. The goal of this podcast series is not to attack Christianity but to restore it - to resurrect the original way of life that Jesus taught before it was corrupted into a "death cult of bloodshed and sacrifice."

As Chris Sartain summarizes: "All we're trying to do here is restore the original Christianity. Nothing more than that. It's a reformation and a restoration of the original way."
