## The Three Nazarene Practices

### 1. Stop Eating Flesh (Vegetarianism)

**The Core Teaching:** Jesus's entire surrounding environment - his biological brothers, his mentor John the Baptist, his disciples, and all subsequent Nazarene/Ebionite communities - abstained from flesh consumption.

**Key Evidence:**
- **Eusebius (Proof of the Gospel 3.5):** "Speaking of the 12 disciples, they embraced and persevered in a laborious and strenuous life with fasting and abstinence from wine and meat."
- **Acts of Thomas:** "The Apostle Thomas continually fasted and prayed and abstained from the eating of flesh. He eats only bread and salt."
- **Clement of Alexandria on Matthew:** "The Apostle Matthew partook of seeds and nuts, hard-shelled fruits and vegetables without flesh."
- **Jerome on the Nazarenes:** "The Nazarenes abstained from eating animal food and from the flesh of living beings."
- **Hegesippus on James the Just:** "James the Just was holy from his mother's womb. He drank no wine or strong drink nor did he eat meat."
- **Syriac Aramaic Gospel of Luke 24:42:** "Do not let your hearts be hardened by the drinking of wine or the eating of flesh" (changed in later Greek manuscripts to "dissipation")

**The Logic:** If James was "holy from his mother's womb" because he ate no flesh, and Jesus was presumably also holy from birth, the logical conclusion is Jesus also abstained. It's improbable that a mother would raise one child vegetarian and the other eating flesh at the same table.

**The Fish Stories:** The hosts reference Cam Waters' work showing these were likely later interpolations borrowed from Greek mythology, added to harmonize with gentile Roman eating customs.

### 2. Give Clothes to the Poor (Care for the Ebionim)

**The Core Teaching:** The Jerusalem community was called the Ebionim ("poor ones"). Caring for the poor was the central mission - not an afterthought.

**Key Evidence:**
- Jesus's confrontations with Pharisees centered on healing the poor on the Sabbath
- James chastises Paul in Galatians, requiring him to "remember the poor ones" - indicating Paul had forgotten this core mandate
- The Dead Sea Scrolls community rule required members to "offer all that you possess to share among the community"
- Paul's offering to the Ebionim was likely rejected (he never mentions its acceptance, then writes scathingly about "false apostles" in 2 Corinthians)

**Practical Application:**
- Keep a bag of clothes in your car at all times
- Don't just donate to Goodwill for tax write-offs - engage directly with people
- Buy bulk socks to give to homeless people
- Look into their eyes, treat them as humans, not "modern-day lepers"
- The hosts share powerful personal stories of caring for the unhoused

**The Baby Formula Experiment:** A viral video showing a woman calling 33 Christian churches (including Joel Osteen's $90M+ operation) asking for baby formula - all refused. Only a Catholic individual offered personal money, and Muslim/Buddhist places of worship helped.

### 3. Practice the Sabbath on Saturdays

**The Core Teaching:** The Nazarenes observed Saturday Sabbath with preparation fasting on Wednesday and Friday, distinguishing themselves from other Jewish groups' Monday/Thursday pattern.

**Key Evidence:**
- **Epiphanius (Panarion 29.7.5):** "They [the Nazarenes] disagree with Jews because of their belief in Christ [Messiah], but they are not in accord with Christians because they are still fettered by the law - circumcision, the Sabbath, and the rest."
- **Gospel of Thomas, Saying 27:** "If you do not fast from the world, you will not find the kingdom. If you do not observe the Sabbath as a Sabbath, you will not see the Father."
- **Didache Chapter 8:** Believers instructed to fast on Wednesday and Friday (preparation day), still observing Saturday as Sabbath

**Why It Matters:**
- Friday-Saturday is when the "matrix" wants you partying/drinking
- The Sabbath is a rebellion against constant task-orientation
- Constantine changed it to Sunday in 321 AD - another "Roman fitting"
- Heaven is not about God needing rest - it's about what's good for us spiritually

**Practical Application:**
- Friday evening Shabbat dinner with community
- Break bread (hummus, unleavened bread, simple foods)
- Practice water immersion (daily Essene practice)
- The Eucharist as outlined in Didache - no "drinking blood of Christ" language
- Journal, light candles, unplug from devices
- Fast for your enemies (not just pray)
