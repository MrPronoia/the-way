## Summary

This inaugural episode of The Jesus Way Podcast establishes the foundational framework for understanding Jesus's teachings through "The 5 O's of Ontology" - the immutable characteristics of God's nature. Aaron Abke and James Benefico argue that Christianity has strayed from Jesus's original message by allowing theology (man-made doctrines) to inform ontology (the nature of God), when it should be the reverse. They contend that the first-century Jewish Christians (Nazarenes, Ebionites) held the closest understanding of Jesus's actual teachings.

The hosts present a compelling case that Jesus's soteriology (doctrine of salvation) was fundamentally different from Paul's. While Paul emphasized "confess and believe" (Romans 10:9), Jesus consistently taught that eternal life comes through keeping the commandments - specifically loving God and loving neighbor as yourself (Matthew 19:16, Mark 10:17, Luke 18:18). They argue that near-death experiences consistently validate Jesus's works-based teaching rather than belief-based salvation.

The episode challenges mainstream Christian doctrines including eternal hell, blood atonement theology, and the idea that God requires sacrifice. They propose that these concepts violate God's ontological nature and represent corruptions that entered Christianity through Hellenistic Gentile influence rather than Jesus's original Jewish teaching.

## Key Teachings

### The 5 O's of Ontology

The hosts present five immutable characteristics of God's nature that should inform all theology:

1. **Omnipotent** - God has all power; nothing is impossible for God
2. **Omniscient** - God knows everything; past, present, future
3. **Omnipresent** - God is everywhere; there is nowhere God is not (even in "hell")
4. **Omnibenevolent** - God is all-loving; everything God does leads to flourishing
5. **One** - God is indivisible, unchanging, self-sufficient, and eternal

### Ontology Must Inform Theology (Not Reverse)

The fundamental error of mainstream Christianity is allowing man-made doctrines to define God's nature. The proper approach:
- Start with the unchanging ontological truths about God's nature
- Test all theological claims against these truths
- Reject anything that violates God's ontological nature

### Jesus's Soteriology vs. Paul's Soteriology

**Paul's teaching:** Confess Jesus as Lord + believe in resurrection = saved (Romans 10:9)

**Jesus's teaching:** Keep the commandments, love God, love neighbor = eternal life

Jesus was asked "how do I attain eternal life" three times (Matthew 19:16, Mark 10:17, Luke 18:18). Each time he answered: "Keep the Commandments."

### The Devil as Ego

The hosts argue that "Satan" or "the Devil" is an ancient-world personification of the human ego - the part of the mind that doesn't know God and tempts us to wander from truth. Not a literal second power opposing God.

### Evil as Absence, Not Presence

Evil has no ontological reality - it is the absence of good, like a shadow is the absence of light. Evil arises from ignorance of God's nature, not from a separate power.

## Jewish Christianity Context

### The Nazarenes and Ebionites

- First followers of Jesus called themselves "followers of the Way"
- Also called "Nazarenes" (Nazarini)
- The name "Ebionites" means "the poor ones" - this was the self-designation of the Dead Sea Scroll group at Qumran
- Jewish Christianity survived until approximately the 4th century
- Fled to Pella (trans-Jordan region) around 66-68 AD before temple destruction in 70 AD
- Led by James the Just (Jesus's brother) for almost 30 years
- Continued to be led by relatives of Jesus

### The Parting of the Ways

- Scholars refer to this split between Jewish and Gentile Christianity
- Two parallel versions formed in first two centuries:
  1. Jewish Christianity of Jesus and disciples (Nazarenes, Ebionites)
  2. Proto-Catholic Gentile Christianity
- Jewish Christians were opposed by both:
  - Gentile Christians (who eventually declared them heretics)
  - Orthodox Judaism (daily curses in synagogues)

### Dead Sea Scroll Connection

- The hosts believe Jesus and the Nazarenes emerged from the Dead Sea Scroll group at Qumran
- Commonly known as "Essenes" but this name doesn't appear in Dead Sea Scrolls
- Self-designated as "Ebionim" (the poor ones)
- Same name as the first-century Jerusalem Church

### Jewish Christian Approach to Scripture

- Rejected scriptures that violated God's ontological nature
- Would not accept passages showing God with human emotions (anger, reactivity)
- Believed the Torah had been corrupted by "the lying pen of the scribes" (Jeremiah 8:8)
- Rejected animal sacrifice as man-made corruption

## Misconceptions Addressed

- **Common Belief:** God requires blood sacrifice/atonement to forgive sin
- **Actual Teaching:** God can forgive with a repentant heart; sacrifice theology violates omnibenevolence

- **Common Belief:** Hell is eternal separation from God
- **Actual Teaching:** Psalm 139 says God is even in Sheol; near-death experiences show people can call out to God/Jesus from hellish realms and be rescued

- **Common Belief:** Confessing Jesus as Lord is required for salvation
- **Actual Teaching:** Jesus said "Not everyone who says Lord, Lord will enter the kingdom" - works of love matter more than confession

- **Common Belief:** People reject God and choose hell
- **Actual Teaching:** People reject false understandings of God; no one can truly reject the source of all goodness

- **Common Belief:** The Devil is a literal being opposing God
- **Actual Teaching:** The devil is the human ego - the voice in our minds that tempts us away from God's truth

- **Common Belief:** Christianity represents Jesus's original teachings
- **Actual Teaching:** Christianity is largely based on Paul's teachings; Jesus's actual gospel (keep commandments, love neighbor) is rarely emphasized

- **Common Belief:** Free will is honored in Christian soteriology
- **Actual Teaching:** "Confess or burn forever" is coercion, not free choice - it removes genuine free will

## Connection to Other Research

### Jesus vs. Paul Theme
This episode directly supports the synthesis that Paul's teachings diverged significantly from Jesus's original message. The hosts provide multiple examples:
- Jesus taught salvation through keeping commandments; Paul taught confession/belief
- Jesus taught forgiveness comes from forgiving others; Paul taught blood atonement
- Jesus emphasized works; Paul emphasized faith alone

### Kingdom NOW Theology
The hosts' emphasis on theosis (becoming Christlike through daily practice) and the Kingdom of Heaven as present reality aligns with Kingdom NOW theology rather than future-focused eschatology.

### First-Century Jewish Christianity
Strong validation for researching Nazarenes, Ebionites, and Dead Sea Scroll connections to understand Jesus's original teaching context.

### Near-Death Experience Evidence
The hosts extensively cite NDE research as empirical validation of Jesus's works-based soteriology - people in hellish realms describe being there due to how they lived (anger, unforgiveness), not what they believed theologically.

### Ontology as Hermeneutic
This framework of testing all scripture against God's immutable nature provides a powerful tool for discerning authentic Jesus teaching from later theological additions.
