## Summary

This episode explores how fasting, one of the most powerful spiritual practices in the early Jesus movement, was systematically removed from biblical manuscripts. The hosts examine Mark 9:29 and Matthew 17:21, where Jesus states that certain demons can only be cast out through "prayer and fasting" - yet modern translations either omit "and fasting" entirely or remove the entire verse. Kameron Waters, who is in the midst of a 40-day water fast (which he calls a "fast for justice"), shares his experience and presents textual evidence showing this was an intentional editorial change.

The discussion reveals that while the two earliest codices (Sinaiticus and Vaticanus) omit "and fasting," virtually all other Greek manuscripts include it, and early church fathers like Clement, Origen, Jerome, Epiphanius, and Chrysostom all attest to the reading including fasting. The papyrus P45, one of the earliest manuscripts, has a space where "fasting" should appear, suggesting it was originally there. The hosts argue that the phrase was removed possibly due to anti-fasting movements in Alexandria or to protect a "high Christology" view that the divine Jesus shouldn't need to fast to cast out demons.

The episode connects fasting to the broader Nazarene/Essene tradition, showing how all the early apostles (Peter, Thomas, Matthew, James the Just) practiced both regular fasting and abstinence from flesh and wine. This is presented as part of the "three temples" concept - cleansing the body, mind, and spirit - which was central to the Jesus Way but was lost as Christianity became increasingly Greco-Roman and gentile. The hosts call for a return to these practices as a form of "participatory eschatology" - living as if the kingdom of heaven is already here.

- **Mark 9:29 and Matthew 17:21 originally included "prayer AND fasting"** - Modern translations have removed this, but the manuscript evidence overwhelmingly supports the original inclusion

- **The Didache (1st century) required fasting before baptism** - Both the baptizer and the one being baptized were to fast one or two days beforehand

- **Fasting cleanses all three temples** - Body (physical detox), Mind (mental clarity, shadow work), Spirit (reliance on God)
- **Parasites may be connected to demonic attachment** - The Essene Gospel of Peace story of Jesus expelling a worm through fasting illustrates this
- **The "god of the belly" is a major spiritual idol** - Referenced in Apocalypse of Elijah as a warning against anti-fasting movements
- **Abstaining from flesh and wine is connected to fasting practice** - Daniel, James the Just, and all apostles practiced this
- **Fasting builds spiritual fortitude** - The struggle against hunger is the ego/adversary being confronted and overcome

## Jewish Christianity Context

The episode heavily emphasizes the Nazarene/Essene context of fasting:

- **Essene Initiation Rite**: Jesus's 40-day fast appears to be an Essene initiation practice that only the greatest masters of that lineage would attempt. John the Baptist likely did the same.

- **James the Just as Model**: As the appointed successor to Jesus and leader of the Jerusalem church, James's lifetime practice of abstaining from flesh and wine (from his mother's womb) represents the authentic tradition. The hosts ask: if Mary taught James this, why would she not teach Jesus?

- **Clementine Literature**: These texts (Travels of Peter, etc.) are dated by many scholars to 1st century, preserve the Nazarene tradition, and show Peter living on bread, olives, and rarely pot herbs - indicating the simple diet was standard.

- **The Didache**: This 1st-century Jewish-Christian document has no Pauline theology and mandates fasting for baptism, representing authentic Nazarene practice.

- **Lost Practices**: Three staple practices of the Jesus movement were obscured by the Greco-Roman church takeover: baptism (changed from daily purification to one-time event), Eucharist (changed from alternative to animal sacrifice to weekly ritual), and fasting (removed from texts and practice).

- **Anti-Temple Movement**: The hosts connect fasting to Jesus's temple cleansing - both are about purifying the temple (body) and eliminating the sacrificial/death system.

## Misconceptions Addressed

1. **"Modern translations are based on the earliest manuscripts"** - While Sinaiticus and Vaticanus are early, they show signs of editorial changes. Later manuscripts, papyri, and church father citations all support the original reading including fasting.

2. **"1 Timothy 4:3 condemns those who require abstaining from foods"** - Actually, the Greek structure shows Paul is condemning those who FORBID abstaining (forbid fasting), not those who practice it.

3. **"Jesus said fast in secret, so public fasting is wrong"** - Jesus was saying not to look sad and sorrowful performatively; Old Testament prophets fasted publicly to inspire change, and communal fasts were normal.

4. **"Jesus ate fish and meat"** - If James the Just abstained from flesh from birth because Mary taught him, why would she not teach Jesus the same? The flesh-eating passages may be later additions.

5. **"Fasting is just willpower/self-denial"** - It's actually about humbling oneself to draw near to God, hear His will clearly, and align with the golden rule (loving God and neighbor).

6. **"Wine is acceptable because Jesus turned water to wine"** - The hosts promise a future episode debunking this, noting that wine consumption became normalized through Greco-Roman influence.

7. **"We need meat to be healthy/strong"** - Aaron shares he's working toward a 500lb deadlift, 405lb squat, 315lb bench at single-digit body fat on a plant-based diet with only 50-75g protein daily.
