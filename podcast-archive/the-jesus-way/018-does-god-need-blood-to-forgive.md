# Episode 18: Responding to George Janko: Does God Need Blood to Forgive?

**Hosts:** Aaron Abke & James Benefico

## Summary

Aaron and James react to a clip from Christian influencer George Janko's podcast about blood atonement and the Abraham/Isaac story. Using this as a springboard, they articulate their core critique of mainstream Christian soteriology: that penal substitutionary atonement (PSA) is illogical, metaphysically incoherent, and antithetical to Jesus's own teachings about salvation.

The central thesis: God cannot "need" blood to forgive because God is omnipotent (possessing all power), omniscient (all knowledge), and omnibenevolent (all love). If God needs something external to accomplish forgiveness, God is no longer omnipotent. The hosts argue this is "begging the question" - asserting premises without logical justification.

They trace this blood sacrifice theology to the priestly class in ancient Judaism who, scholars now recognize, edited and interpolated sacrificial passages into the Hebrew Bible. The prophetic tradition, by contrast, consistently condemned animal sacrifice: "I desire mercy, not sacrifice" (Hosea 6:6), "I never commanded you animal sacrifices" (Jeremiah 7:22).

The episode presents Jesus's actual soteriology: repentance + forgiveness of others = forgiveness from God. "Judge and you will be judged. Condemn and you will be condemned. Forgive and you will be forgiven" (Luke 6:37). No blood required.

## Key Teachings

### The Five O's of God (Ontological Axioms)

Aaron presents five axioms that define God's nature - any theology contradicting these axioms should be rejected:

1. **Omnipotent** - All power; needs nothing, lacks nothing
2. **Omniscient** - All knowledge; understands why we sin
3. **Omnipresent** - Everywhere; you can never be separate from God
4. **Omnibenevolent** - All love; wills no harm to creation
5. **One** - There is only one source; no second power opposes God

### The Debt Problem

If God wants to forgive and has all power, what could prevent God from forgiving? The PSA answer - that sin creates a "debt" that must be "paid" - raises unanswerable questions:
- How does blood pay a debt?
- What can blood give to God that God doesn't already have?
- Whose debt is it - mine or God's?
- If I can't earn salvation, then God just wants repentance - why add blood?
- How does my sin negatively impact an infinite, omnipotent God?

### Jesus's Actual Soteriology

Jesus made salvation contingent on behavior, not confession or sacrifice:
- "Why do you call me Lord, Lord, and not do what I say?" (Luke 6:46)
- "Forgive and you will be forgiven" (Luke 6:37)
- "If you want to enter eternal life, keep the commandments" (Matthew 19:17)
- "Judge and you will be judged" - reciprocal spiritual law

The hosts emphasize: Jesus never once said "confess me as Lord and Savior," "believe in my death and resurrection," or "my blood will cover your sins."

### Priests vs. Prophets

The Old Testament represents a battle between two traditions:
- **Priestly tradition**: Institutionalized religion, temple cult, animal sacrifice, blood magic
- **Prophetic tradition**: Populist movement condemning sacrifice, calling for justice and righteousness

Scholars identify four sources (JEDP theory): Yahwist, Elohist, Deuteronomic, and Priestly (P). The P source contributed most of the sacrificial passages. The prophets angrily condemned animal sacrifice even after priestly editing.

### The Nature of Sin and Death

"The wages of sin is death" (Romans 6:23) cannot mean physical death (sinners live long lives). It means:
- Separation from God in awareness
- Spiritual darkness, suffering, privation
- Loss of felt connection to the source

Similarly, Jesus's promise "whoever believes in me will never die" refers to spiritual death/separation, not physical death.

### True Sacrifice

What does God actually want as sacrifice?
- "A broken and contrite spirit" (Psalm 51:17)
- Giving up what is false: pride, self-righteousness, separation
- Not giving up what is good (God is good; God doesn't need good things destroyed)

If God killed Jesus (who is unquestionably good), then God is sacrificing his own goodness - a self-refuting contradiction.

### The Circumcision of the Heart

Real transformation is cutting off what is evil within your own heart - not external ritual, not blood magic. This is the inner work Jesus taught.

## Misconceptions Addressed

| Misconception | Response |
|---------------|----------|
| "God is just AND loving (implying tension)" | God's justice is not in opposition to love - it is an extension of love. True justice leads to redemption, not eternal torment. |
| "Anti-sacrifice passages mean God wants sacrifice WITH a humble heart" | Show the passage where God says this. It doesn't exist. Hosea 6:6 uses "vello" (no/never) - an unequivocal infinitive. |
| "Guilt is a sign of spiritual sensitivity" | Guilt is a sign of separation from God, not virtue. True repentance (metanoia) means changing your mind - no longer identifying as a "guilty sinner." |
| "We are totally depraved sinners incapable of righteousness" | The Bible never says God demands perfect behavior. God asks for repentance with a humble heart - then forgives completely. |

| "Jesus sacrificed himself to pay for sins" | Jesus gave up a weekend, then resurrected and lives eternally in glory. Abraham killing Isaac would have been permanent loss. There's no real "sacrifice" if nothing is actually lost. |

## Personal Reflection

This episode provides the clearest articulation yet of the logical and metaphysical problems with penal substitutionary atonement. The core question - "What can blood do for God?" - is simple but devastating.

The framing of animal sacrifice as indistinguishable from black magic/demonic ritual is provocative but logically sound: any ritual that requires killing innocents to gain spiritual benefit is, by definition, what we'd call dark magic in any other context.

The alternative soteriology - repentance + forgiving others = forgiveness from God - is both simpler and more aligned with Jesus's actual recorded teachings.

---

## Key Takeaway

God is omnipotent. An omnipotent being cannot "need" anything external to accomplish its will. Therefore, God cannot need blood to forgive. Blood atonement theology, however sincere its adherents, ultimately implies a limited God whose hands are tied without violence. This is a cartoonish deity unworthy of worship.

Jesus's actual message: Love God, love neighbor, repent when you fail, forgive others as you want to be forgiven. That's the Jesus Way.
